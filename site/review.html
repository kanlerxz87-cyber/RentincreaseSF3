Tenant-lawyer test

Would a free tenant-side lawyer accept these increases?

San Francisco tenants' access to free counsel means an increase will be tested adversarially, and procedure is tested before policy. The nine checks below are the ones that actually decide cases, ordered so that the cheapest objection comes first.

The nine checks

#Check and what would satisfy itSource
1Is there a licence on the effective date?

Look the property up in the Rent Board Portal and check the licence record for the exact date the increase takes effect, not the date the notice was served. No licence means no enforceable increase, whatever the percentage.

sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance
2Is the amount at or below the published allowance?

Recompute it. Base rent × the rate in force on the effective date. Check that nothing was rounded up and that no passthrough was folded into the base.

sf.gov — Learn about rent increases · Rent Board Form 571 — allowable increases 1982–2027 (PDF)
3Is any banked portion actually bankable?

Itemise each year claimed, check each against Form 571, and check the gap requirement. This is the weakest link in the file and is disputed between official pages — see F-02.

sf.gov — Banked rent increases · Rent Board Form 571 — allowable increases 1982–2027 (PDF)
4Has 12 months passed, and on the right anniversary?

Count from the tenancy start or the last increase. For Unit 1 the anniversary is 1 February, not 1 April: a continuing tenancy with an added occupant does not restart it.

sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
5Was the notice the right length and content?

30 or 90 days by the size of the increase, plus 5 days if mailed. Dollar, percentage, effective date, and the banked breakdown.

Civ. Code §827 · Code Civ. Proc. §1013 · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
6Is the reason free of protected grounds?

Any reference to who the tenant is, to a voucher, to family composition, to a complaint, or to organising is fatal.

Gov. Code §12955 (FEHA — housing discrimination) · 42 U.S.C. §3604 (Fair Housing Act) · Civ. Code §1942.5 (retaliation)
7Was the increase used to pressure the tenant out?

A rent increase substantially above market comparables, or within six months of an attempt to recover possession, can be evidence of bad faith harassment.

Admin. Code §37.10B — tenant harassment (sf.gov)
8Is the tenant being charged for the extra occupant?

Unit 1 gained an occupant. If any part of the increase is attributable to that, it is unlawful.

sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
9Is the same policy applied to both units?

Write the policy down. Differences explained by anniversary date and rent history are defensible; differences explained by the tenants are not.

sf.gov — Get help for discrimination in housing · Gov. Code §12955 (FEHA — housing discrimination)

An increase that passes all nine is ordinary business practice and should survive review. An increase that fails any one of the first five will be overturned regardless of how reasonable the percentage looks.

Recommendation: adopt one written policy for the property — the published allowance plus properly earned banked years, applied on each unit's anniversary — and apply it to both units. The resulting amounts will differ, because the tenancy histories differ, and that difference is the explanation.
