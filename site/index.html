Answer first

Does an individual owner need permission from the Rent Board before raising rent?

You do not need the Rent Board's permission for the amount, and you do not need to file the increase notice with it. But you do need two things from the Rent Board's Housing Inventory before the increase can take effect. First, report both units into the Housing Inventory (due 1 March each year). Second, make sure the rent-increase licence that the report generates is current on the effective date of the increase. A correct 1.6% increase served without a current licence is unenforceable, and the tenant can recover it.

“Do I need to inform the Rent Board before notifying tenants?”Yes, once a year. All owners of residential property must report into the Housing Inventory by 1 March. Reporting a tenant-occupied unit is what produces the licence. Reporting is free and is done online.sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov)
“Do I need to apply for a licence if I am an individual, not a corporation?”Yes. There is no individual-owner exemption. The ordinance draws its line by building size (10+ units vs 1–9 units and condominiums), never by whether the owner is a person or a company. A two-unit building is in the 1–9 unit group, which has been reporting since 1 March 2023.sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov) · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance
“Do I need the Rent Board to approve the amount of the increase?”No. Annual allowable increases and banked increases require no petition and no approval. The licence is a reporting-compliance licence, not a per-increase authorisation. Petitions are only for extra increases above the allowance (operating and maintenance, capital improvement and similar).sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years · Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov)
“What happens if I skip it?”The licence is suspended while reporting is not substantially compliant, and the tenant is not obligated to pay the increase for the months of the suspension. A notice that took effect while unlicensed may be null and void for several years. The Rent Board may write to your tenants to tell them.Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov) · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance
“Does getting the maths right fix it?”No. The licence condition is independent of the percentage. Both have to be right.sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
“Which rate applies to me?”1.6% for an increase effective 1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027. The prior year's rate was 1.4%. Use the rate in effect on the effective date, not the day you serve the notice.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases · sf.gov — 1.6% increase for 3/1/26–2/28/27 announced · Rent Board Form 571 — allowable increases 1982–2027 (PDF)
“Do I need to file the increase notice itself with the Rent Board?”No. There is no filing requirement for an annual or banked notice. Your obligation is to give the tenant written notice (30 or 90 days) and to keep proof of service.Civ. Code §827 · sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases

The order matters: report → confirm licence → serve notice. Doing it the other way round is the most common reason a lawful-percentage increase gets overturned.

The one fact that decides the most

Unit 1 has a continuing tenancy. One original tenant is still there and a new occupant moved in on 1 April 2026. That means there was no vacancy, so the rent cannot be reset to market, and the owner may not charge more because an extra person moved in. Rule §6.14 would allow a market-rate increase only if the last original occupant had left and a proper written 6.14 notice had been served; and under Costa-Hawkins a co-tenant — someone with an agreement with the owner — is not subject to that increase regardless of when they moved in. This is the point a tenant-side lawyer would test first, and it is absent from every earlier edition of this site.
