Notice & reasons

How to tell the tenants, and what may be said as the reason

The procedure is fixed by statute and is the part most often got wrong. The reason is narrower than most owners expect: for an annual or banked increase, the reason is the ordinance formula, and the legal risk lies in what must not be said or relied upon.

If the tenant disputes it

A tenant in a covered unit can file an Unlawful Rent Increase Petition with the Rent Board. There is no filing fee and no time limit. The case is usually mediated, and if not it goes to a hearing before an Administrative Law Judge. The hearing is informal and counsel is not required, though both sides may be represented.

If the notice is defective but the increase has not yet taken effect, a Tenant Summary Petition can be used instead, which prompts the Board to write to the owner.

sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase · sf.gov — Tenant Petitions · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance

This is why the file matters more than the argument. An itemised, correctly calculated notice with proof of service and a licence record settles most petitions before a hearing.
