Reasonable & normal

What a reasonable increase means for a rent-controlled unit

For these units "reasonable" is not a matter of judgement or local market custom. The lawful ceiling is the normal figure, because the ordinance sets it citywide at 60% of the Bay Area CPI change.

The benchmark

QuestionVerified answerSource
What is the lawful ceiling now?1.6% for an increase effective 1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027; 1.4% the year before.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases · sf.gov — 1.6% increase for 3/1/26–2/28/27 announced
How is it set?On 1 March each year the Board publishes the CPI change for the preceding 12 months, and the allowance is 60% of that, never more than 7%.Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov)
What is a normal increase in practice?For a covered unit, the published allowance applied once a year on the anniversary. Anything larger means a banked breakdown or a granted petition, and either will be examined.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov)
What is a normal increase for this ZIP code?Blank — no official figure exists. No government source publishes a lawful or market rent benchmark at ZIP level, so none is asserted here.California Attorney General — limits on rent increases · Rent Board Form 571 — allowable increases 1982–2027 (PDF)
Can market rent be used as the target?No. A covered unit's rent can only reach market through the mechanisms in the ordinance — banked years, a granted petition, or a lawful vacancy reset. The owner cannot simply move the rent toward market because it is below it.Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov) · Civ. Code §1954.53 (Costa-Hawkins)
Is 1.6% below the cost of living?That is a policy question the ordinance answers, not a legal one. The formula is 60% of CPI by design, so the allowance normally lags CPI. Disagreement with it is not a lawful basis for charging more.Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov)

How large owners and individual owners actually differ

The rules are identical. What differs is process discipline, and that is what decides whether an increase survives review.

The last row matters. It is often asserted that corporations raise rents faster than small owners. No official source in the record supports a figure either way, so the claim is not made here and is published instead as an open item on F-07.

What is genuinely normal, and defensible

  1. One written rule for the whole property: "each covered unit is increased annually by the Rent Board's allowable percentage, plus any banked percentage that has been properly earned, applied on that unit's anniversary date."
  2. Applying it every year, even when the amount is small. The allowance is lost if it is not banked, and losing it is not reversible.
  3. Phasing a large accumulated figure rather than serving it in one notice.
  4. Notifying a few weeks earlier than the statutory minimum, so the effective date holds even if service is disputed.
  5. Keeping every notice and proof of service. A petition can be filed years later, with no deadline.
