Process

From coverage check to served notice: nine steps

Written for an owner who has never dealt with the Rent Board. Steps 1 to 3 must happen before any notice is served. Steps 4 and 5 are the ones that invalidate otherwise correct increases when they are skipped.

The licence and reporting questions in detail

QuestionVerified answerSource
Who must report?All owners of residential property. Most owners must report even if they are not currently letting a unit. There is no exemption for an owner who is a private individual.sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov)
Is there a separate licence application?No. The licence is generated automatically once an occupied unit is reported. There is no form to file and no fee.sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov)
When must the licence be in place?On the effective date of the increase. Being licensed by the time you serve the notice is not enough if the licence lapses before the increase takes effect.sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
What is the deadline?1 March each year. Late filing is possible and carries no monetary penalty, but it does not retroactively validate an increase that took effect while unlicensed.sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory
What is the consequence of non-compliance?The licence is temporarily suspended, and the tenant is not obligated to pay the increase for the months of suspension. The Board may notify tenants directly. Tenants may file an Unlawful Rent Increase Petition, at no charge and with no time limit.Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov) · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase
Is the licence the same as approval of the amount?No. It is a reporting-compliance licence. The amount is separately constrained by §37.3 (60% of CPI, maximum 7%) and needs no approval.Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov) · Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov)
Does the notice need to be filed with the Rent Board?No. There is no filing requirement for an annual or banked notice.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years
When is an extra petition needed?Only for increases above the annual allowance: operating and maintenance expenses, capital improvements, rehabilitation, energy improvements, utility and bond passthroughs. None of those are assumed here.Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov) · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases

Step by step

#What to doSource
1Confirm the units are covered

Check the certificate of occupancy date, not the build year. Units built on or before 13 June 1979 that are not single-family dwellings are generally covered by both rent control and eviction protection. A 1940 two-unit building almost certainly is; confirm it.

sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws
2Pull the rent history for each unit

You need the date and amount of every increase, or the absence of one, because that fixes the anniversary date and the bankable percentage. Without it nothing else can be calculated safely.

sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
3Create a Rent Board Portal account and add the property

Reporting is done online, is free, and can be done at any time. Paper filings are possible but slower.

sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov / Rent Board Portal
4Submit the Housing Inventory for both units

For each non-owner-occupied unit you report the owner or manager contact details, any business registration number, approximate square footage in 250 sq ft increments, number of bedrooms and bathrooms, the date the current occupancy began, vacancy and occupancy dates for the past 12 months, and the base rent in $250 increments plus which utilities it includes. The submission is made under penalty of perjury.

sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov)
5Confirm the rent-increase licence

Reporting an occupied unit generates the licence automatically. In the Portal, search the property, open it, and check that a licence is listed and that its period covers your intended effective date. Tenants can see the same record.

sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance
6Fix the effective date and the percentage

At least 12 months must have passed since the tenancy began or since the last increase. Use the rate in force on the effective date. Do not round up. Do not compound. Base rent only.

sf.gov — Learn about rent increases · Civ. Code §827
7Choose a figure you can defend

The published allowance alone is the safest. Adding banked years is lawful but invites scrutiny and may be capped by the pending ordinance; a phased increase keeps you inside both the current and the proposed rule.

sf.gov — Banked rent increases · Legistar File #261020 — pending 10%/yr cap (not law)
8Serve the written notice

30 days if the increase is 10% or less, 90 days if more, plus 5 calendar days if mailed inside California. State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which part is banked.

Civ. Code §827 · Code Civ. Proc. §1013 · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
9Keep the file, and repeat every March

Keep the notice, proof of service and the licence record together. Re-file the Housing Inventory by 1 March each year and update owner or manager contact details within 30 days of any change.

sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase

What the statute actually says

§37.15(e) is the provision that creates the licence, and it is worth reading in full because it contains both the licence and the penalty for not having one: “If a landlord has substantially complied with the obligation to report information about a rental unit as required under this Section 37.15, the landlord shall receive a license to impose rent increases on tenants in that unit under Sections 37.3(a)(1)-(2). If the landlord has not substantially complied with the reporting obligation, then the license to impose rent increases shall be temporarily suspended during the period of the landlord's noncompliance. Upon receipt of the required information from the landlord, the suspension shall be terminated, and the license to impose rent increases shall be restored prospectively, but a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension.”

Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov) · Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov)

Note the words restored prospectively. Catching up on reporting fixes the future; it does not cure the months that have already passed.
