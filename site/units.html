The 3.0% option depends entirely on F-02. On the reading that a skipped year needs a 24-month gap before it can be banked, the 1.4% from 2026 is lost and only 1.6% ($63.92) is available on 1 February 2027. On the reading that a 12-month gap is enough — which is what the Rent Board's own worked examples use — 3.0% is available. The 1.6% figure is safe under both readings, which is why it is the conservative recommendation.

Every banked figure assumes no increase has been imposed since 1 June 2019, which is what the owner reported but which has not been evidenced. See F-03. An increase served now would also fall within the scope of the pending ordinance described on sf.gov, which applies to banked increases noticed on or after 15 September 2026. See F-01.

What the notice must say for a banked increase

Where any part of the increase is banked, the notice should identify which part is banked. In practice that means breaking the percentage down by the year it was earned, so that the tenant can check it against the published rate table. A notice that simply says 13.1% invites a petition even when the arithmetic is right.

Assumptions, stated plainly

  • Both units are covered by the Rent Ordinance (1940 building, two units). The certificate of occupancy was not produced — see F-08.
  • The owner's statement of rent history is accurate and complete — see F-03.
  • No increase was imposed on Unit 1 before 1 February 2026 and none on Unit 2 since the tenancy began.
  • Neither unit is subsidised, which would displace the ordinance arithmetic — see F-06.
  • No petition-based increase (operating and maintenance, capital improvement, passthrough) is included.
  • Base rent excludes any passthrough, and includes any parking or storage charge.
