The legal framework

L-01The SF Rent Ordinance (Administrative Code Ch. 37) covers rent control and eviction protection for many units built on or before June 13, 1979. A 1940 two-unit building is therefore presumptively covered; both units are treated as covered here.sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws
L-02The allowable annual increase is 60% of the published CPI change, set each March 1, and in no event more than 7%.Admin. Code §37.3 (sf.gov) · Rent Board Form 571 — allowable increases 1982–2027 (PDF)
L-03The allowable annual increase effective 3/1/2026–2/28/2027 is 1.6%; the prior year, 3/1/2025–2/28/2026, was 1.4%.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases · sf.gov — 1.6% increase for 3/1/26–2/28/27 announced · Rent Board Form 571 — allowable increases 1982–2027 (PDF)
L-04An increase may be imposed once every 12 months. The first increase is available 12 months after the tenancy began; once imposed, the effective date becomes the anniversary date.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
L-05Increases are calculated on base rent only, must not be rounded up, and must not be compounded. Parking or storage rent is generally part of base rent; passthroughs are not.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
L-0630 days' written notice if the increase is 10% or less (alone or combined with other increases in the prior 12 months); 90 days if more than 10%. If served by mail, add 5 calendar days.Civ. Code §827 · Code Civ. Proc. §1013 · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
L-07The notice should state the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and — for a banked increase — which part is banked. Use the rate in effect on the effective date, not the service date.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
L-08Banked increases are additive, never compounded, have no cap back to April 1, 1982, do not expire during a tenancy, may be used by a new owner, and do not carry over to a new tenant.sf.gov — Banked rent increases
L-09No petition or per-increase approval by the Rent Board is required for an annual or banked increase; the Rent Board's role is the Housing Inventory licence, not approval of the amount.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years · sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory

L-11Reporting a tenant-occupied unit generates a rent-increase licence automatically; there is no separate application. The licence must be current on the effective date of the increase.sf.gov — Rent Board Housing Inventory · Admin. Code §37.15 (sf.gov)

L-14A landlord may not charge additional rent because an additional occupant moved into a unit with an existing tenant, even if the lease provides for it.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
L-15§6.14 allows an unlimited (market) increase when the last original occupant vacates, if the owner timely served a written 6.14 notice on each subsequent occupant; about 60 days is generally considered reasonable. Under Costa-Hawkins a co-tenant is not subject to such an increase regardless of when the co-tenant moved in.sf.gov — Rent increases under §6.14 and Costa-Hawkins · Civ. Code §1954.53 (Costa-Hawkins)
L-16Costa-Hawkins / AB 1482 initial-rent reset requires that no tenant from the prior tenancy remains in lawful possession. A co-tenant with an agreement with the landlord is not a new tenancy for that purpose.Civ. Code §1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act, AB 1482) · Civ. L-17AB 1482 caps increases at 5% + CPI, maximum 10%, but does not apply to housing under a local ordinance that restricts increases to a lower amount. SF's 1.6% is lower, so the state cap does not govern these units.Civ. Code §1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act, AB 1482) · California Attorney General — limits on rent increases

The practical effect of the interaction is that the lowest applicable ceiling governs. Quoting the 8.8% state figure to a tenant on a covered unit would be a misstatement of the lawful rent.

Rules that constrain the reason, not the amount

These do not change what may be charged, but they decide whether a charge that is arithmetically correct is nevertheless unlawful:

  • Discrimination in the terms of a rental on protected grounds is unlawful under federal law, California law and the Rent Ordinance's harassment provision. 42 U.S.C. §3604 (Fair Housing Act) · Gov. Code §12955 (FEHA — housing discrimination) · sf.gov — Get help for discrimination in housing
  • Retaliation — an increase within 180 days of a tenant's habitability complaint, or for organising, is prohibited. Civ. Code §1942.5 (retaliation)
  • Harassment — a rent increase substantially above market comparables, or within six months of an attempt to recover possession, can be evidence of bad faith. Admin. Code §37.10B — tenant harassment (sf.gov)
  • Additional occupants — no extra rent for a new occupant joining an existing tenancy. sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
  • Algorithmic rent-setting — sale and use prohibited for San Francisco residential units. Admin. Code §37.10C — algorithmic devices (sf.gov)

Not yet law

Legistar File #261020, Administrative Code — Cap On Rent Increases for Rent-Controlled Units, would limit combined banked and capital-improvement increases to 10% a year for three years. Status as recorded on the Board of Supervisors' own system: Pending Committee Action, introduced 15 September 2026, in the Land Use and Transportation Committee, with a Youth Commission response received 7 October 2026 and no final action. sf.gov's own notice states that it has not been enacted. It is therefore not applied to any figure in this review, but it is the reason the recommended Unit 2 figure is capped at 10%.
