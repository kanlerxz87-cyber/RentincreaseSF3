F-01 High Pending ordinance could cap a banked catch-up at 10%

Legistar File #261020 would limit combined banked and capital-improvement increases to 10% per year for three years, and sf.gov states it would apply to banked increases noticed on or after 15 September 2026 — that is, to a notice served today. It is not enacted (status: Pending Committee Action), so the 13.1% figure remains lawful today, but a >10% notice served now could be reduced retroactively if it passes.

What would close it: Recommended action: keep the increase at or below 10% until the file's final action is known. Check the Legistar record before serving any banked notice.

 Legistar File #261020 — pending 10%/yr cap (not law) · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
F-02 High The Rent Board's banking threshold is internally inconsistent: 24 months vs 12 months

The 'Banked rent increases' page states a skipped increase is banked only if at least 24 full months pass without any increase. But the worked example on the same page (tenancy 1 Aug 2022, first increase 1 Aug 2025) allows 5.3% = 3.6% + 1.7%, which requires only a 12-month gap for the later year; the 'multiple years' page likewise uses 12 months; the 'irregular periods' page states the 12-month test expressly. Both readings are published here, with the lower figure recommended.

What would close it: Closing this needs written confirmation from a Rent Board counsellor (415-252-4600). It changes Unit 1 by 1.4% and Unit 2 by 1.4%.

F-04 Medium No official ZIP-level or neighbourhood rent benchmark exists

The owner asked what is normal for the unit's specific ZIP code. Neither HUD, Census, the Rent Board nor the Attorney General publishes a lawful-or-market benchmark at ZIP level. The value is therefore left blank, not estimated. The lawful ceiling is citywide and does not vary by ZIP, so no conclusion depends on it.

What would close it: If a market comparison is wanted, pull HUD Fair Market Rents for the San Francisco metro area directly and record the retrieval date.

How to close an item

Each entry states what would close it. In most cases that means one of three things: a document the owner holds (lease, ledger, notice), a written answer from a Rent Board counsellor on 415-252-4600, or a final action recorded on the Board of Supervisors' own legislation system. When an item is closed, the figure it affects can be restated with certainty; until then the conservative figure is the one to rely on.
