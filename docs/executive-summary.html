Scope & reliance warning. This is research and analysis, not legal advice. 1. What this is about

The owner of a 1940-built, two-unit rental building in San Francisco (location withheld) asked whether rent increases for the two units would be reasonable, normal, and lawful under local, state, and federal law; how large corporations and individual owners typically handle increases; the correct way to inform tenants; whether an increase would survive scrutiny by SF tenant-side advocates and lawyers; and whether increasing only one unit — or both — best avoids discrimination exposure.

4. The bottom line

Yes — a reasonable, normal, lawful increase is available in both units, and it is small. SF’s allowable annual increase is the ceiling for the amount and the measure of “normal.” Applying it plus documented banking, on each unit’s anniversary, with compliant notices and a current license, is exactly what SF corporations and careful individual owners do — and what any SF tenant lawyer should find objectively reasonable.

Recommendation: Increase both units — Unit 1 on Feb 1, 2027 by up to 3.0% ($4,114.85), Unit 2 on its June 1 anniversary by its lawful capped amount (up to 13.1% incl. banking, or the conservative 1.6% → $3,200.40). Use one written, uniform policy for the property: “each covered unit is increased annually by the SF Rent Board allowable percentage plus documented banked increases, applied on its anniversary date.” Serve notices 30/90 days per size, keep proof of service, and confirm the Housing Inventory license before the effective date.

Do not raise Unit 1 to “market” because of the April 2026 occupancy change; do not charge an “additional occupant” fee; do not exceed the allowable/banked amounts; do not round up; do not skip the license check.

5. Action checklist

  1. Confirm building coverage & exemptions with SF Rent Board counselors (415-252-4600): construction date/certificate of occupancy, not single-family/condo, no substantial rehab, not subsidized. (FR-02)
  2. Pull the property’s Housing Inventory filings 2022–present; confirm the rent-increase license is current for 2026–27. (FR-06)
  3. Reconstruct Unit 2’s complete rent ledger 2019–2026 (amounts, dates, prior notices). Banked 11.5%/13.1% stands only if zero increases were imposed. (FR-01)
  4. Unit 1: calendar Feb 1, 2027. Decide 3.0% banked vs 1.6% conservative. Serve written notice by Jan 2, 2027 (personal; or by Dec 28, 2026 if mailed — 30 + 5 days).
  5. Unit 2: if imposing >10% total, serve written notice 90 days before the effective date (95 if mailed). If staying ≤10%, 30 days suffices.
  6. Notice must state: dollar amount, percentage, effective date, which part is banked and from which years; use the rate in effect on the effective date; no rounding up.
  7. Document the reason in the file: “Annual allowable increase (CPI-based) per SF Rent Ordinance §37.3(a)(1) and Rent Board 571, plus banked unused increases per §37.3(a)(2).”
  8. Send both notices under one written, property-wide policy; keep copies + proof of service in the property file.
  9. If any tenant pushes back: refer them to free help (SF Rent Board counselors; SF Tenants Union; Bay Area Legal Aid) — do not negotiate outside the documented formula.
  10. Have a tenant-side-experienced landlord attorney review the two notices before service (est. 1 hour).

7. Discrimination guardrails

Neither increase may be motivated by, or have an unjustified disparate impact on, any protected class (federal FHA: race, color, national origin, religion, sex, disability, familial status; California FEHA: adds marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity/expression, source of income, age, and more; SF: adds creed, height, weight, place of birth). Using one written formula for both units, applied on objective anniversary dates, defeats any inference of discrimination. Raising only one unit is lawful only with a documented, objective, non-protected-class reason — and offers no legal advantage. Full analysis →
