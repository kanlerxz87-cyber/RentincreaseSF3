Direct answer to “raise one tenant’s unit or both?”

Raise both units — under one written formula, each on its own anniversary date. That is the lowest-risk posture and the strongest evidence of “normal business practice.”
  • Raising only one unit is not automatically illegal — different lease-start dates legitimately produce different timelines (Unit 1 in February, Unit 2 in June). An owner may also lawfully skip an increase entirely (banking) for one unit while raising the other.
  • But it creates avoidable questions. If Unit 1’s occupants and Unit 2’s occupants differ on any protected characteristic (they might, on the facts), a selective increase invites: (1) an SF HRC complaint, (2) a CRD complaint, (3) a HUD FHEO complaint, or (4) a Rent Board unlawful-increase petition — even when the motive was purely administrative.
  • Uniformity defeats the inference. One written policy — “Both covered units receive, on their own anniversaries, the SF allowable increase plus documented banked increases; neither more, neither less, absent a Rent Board petition” — applied identically to both, is facial proof there was no protected-class motivation. The unavoidable timing asymmetry (February vs. June) is objectively explained by the units’ anniversary dates, not by occupants.
  • Disparate impact is not the test used for rent setting absent a specific theory, but the burden-of-persuasion flip is real in practice: selective treatment shifts the owner onto the back foot. The 1-hour attorney review (step in checklist) should confirm nothing in this file suggests selection by class.
  • Practical sequencing: serve both notices in the same week, same template, same policy memo attached to each file. Unit 1 effective Feb 1, 2027; Unit 2 effective on its chosen June date. Identical wording; different numbers lawfully derived.
  • Never tie the increase to: complaints, code inspections, organizing, family expansion, a new roommate, a tenant’s voucher status, disability accommodations, or any protected class. Any of these converts a lawful increase into an unlawful act.

D. Verdicts, one page

Unit 1 — Feb 1, 2027

PASSES

If: license current; 30-day full-content notice; 3.0% additive; uniform policy shared with both units; no retaliation timing. Amount is modest ($119.85/mo); looks routine to any reviewer.

Unit 2 — June 1, 2027

PASSES WITH PROOF

If: zero-increase history reconstructed and documented; 90-day notice; itemized banking; uniform policy. Otherwise reduce to 1.6% ($3,200.40) — lawful, small, unchallengeable.

Both units, one policy

RECOMMENDED

Uniform written formula on both anniversaries ⇒ discrimination risk ≈ 0; the highest legal hygiene available without a lawyer writing new policy.

E. If a tenant objects or petitions

  1. Do not renegotiate outside the formula; offer the Rent Board counselor line (415-252-4600) to the tenant — it is free and it calms most disputes.
  2. If petitioned (forms 516B/516C/518), respond with: license, ledger, notices, service proofs, this policy memo.
  3. If CRD/HUD/HRC complaint: produce the uniform written policy and service records; do not contact the complainant directly — route through counsel.
  4. If any fact contradicts this analysis (prior increase discovered, service failed), rescind the notice immediately. A rescinded notice costs nothing; an unlawful increase invites triple-damages-style exposure and a petition hearing.
