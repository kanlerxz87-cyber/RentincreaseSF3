Every row cites an official primary source. “Verified 2026-11-06” means the linked page was fetched and read on that date. Items marked flagged are carried to Flagged for Review with the reason.

How the layers stack (SF, San Francisco): Local SF Rent Ordinance is strictest → it controls over AB 1482’s rent cap (AB 1482 expressly exempts units subject to a stricter local ordinance). Costa-Hawkins preempts local vacancy control only — it does not allow above-allowable increases inside a tenancy. Federal law sets no rent levels at all. Zip code San Francisco changes nothing — the ordinance is citywide.

C. State — California

#RuleEffect on these unitsSource
S-1Civil Code §827 — written 30-day notice for increases ≤10% (alone or combined in trailing 12 months); 90 days if >10%. Service personally or by mail (CCP §1013: +5 days in-state).Unit 1: 30 days. Unit 2 (>10%): 90 days.leginfo — Civ. Code §827 · leginfo — CCP §1013
S-2Civil Code §1947.12 (AB 1482) — statewide cap: 5% + regional CPI-U, or 10%, whichever lower; max two increases per 12 months; applies to units >15 years old; no banking beyond the annual ceiling; operative Jan 1, 2020; expires Jan 1, 2030.Does not apply — units are under a stricter local ordinance (S-3). SF’s AB 1482 cap would be 8.8% (Aug 1, 2026–Jul 31, 2027) if it applied.leginfo — AB 1482 · sf.gov — AB 1482
S-3AB 1482 exemption: units already subject to a local rent-control ordinance restricting increases to less than the state cap are exempt from the state rent cap (local just cause also applies if more protective).SF allowable (1.6%) < AB 1482 cap (8.8%) ⇒ local controls.sf.gov — AB 1482 (“does NOT apply for units under the rent increase limitations of the San Francisco Rent Ordinance”)
S-4Civil Code §1946.2 (AB 1482 just cause) — just cause required after 12 months; no-fault evictions require relocation assistance (one month’s rent); SB 567 (2024) narrowed at-fault grounds for breach/refusal to renew and requires relocation for violation of lease terms etc.Eviction context only; local SF just cause is broader and controls.AB 1482 text (leginfo) · SB 567 text (leginfo) · sf.gov
S-5Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act, Civ. Code §§1954.50–1954.535 — (a) new construction after Feb 1, 1995 exempt from local rent control; (b) single-family homes/condos exempt for tenancies beginning on/after Jan 1, 1996; (c) vacancy decontrol: initial rent unregulated for a new tenancy; (d) no local vacancy control.Vacancy decontrol is unavailable here: no full vacancy in either unit. Unit 1’s Apr 2026 partial turnover changed nothing.leginfo — Civ. Code §1954.53 · sf.gov
S-6FEHA (Gov. Code §12900 et seq.) — prohibits housing discrimination incl. terms, conditions, privileges; protected classes broader than federal (adds, e.g., marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity/expression, source of income, age, genetic information, ancestry).Increases motivated by any protected class are unlawful; uniform formula defeats the inference.calcivilrights.ca.gov — Housing

D. Federal

#RuleEffect on these unitsSource
F-1There is no federal rent-control statute. No federal agency may cap unsubsidized private rents; rent levels are state/local matters.Federal law is silent on the amount; local SF law caps it. FR-15 — conclusion drawn from absence, not from a single codified “no rent control” sectionhud.gov (FHEO scope)
F-2Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. §3604(a),(b) — unlawful to discriminate in terms, conditions, or privileges of rental, or to make unavailable, on the basis of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, disability, familial status.A rent increase applied selectively by protected class violates §3604(b) (“terms, conditions”). Uniform formula = compliance.hud.gov — Report Housing Discrimination
F-342 U.S.C. §3617 — retaliation against anyone opposing discriminatory practices or aiding enforcement is unlawful.Do not let the increase follow (causally) any tenant complaint activity; timing alone can evidence retaliation.hud.gov
F-4Section 8 / HCV — tenants with vouchers cannot be charged more than the voucher’s rent reasonableness; increases need PHA approval. (Applies only if a tenant holds a voucher — none stated.)If either occupant uses a voucher, stop and get PHA approval first. FR-14hud.gov (voucher process)
Preemption map. Costa-Hawkins (state) sets the floor for local control (no vacancy control; no control of new construction/SFH/condo). AB 1482 (state) sets a statewide ceiling that yields to stricter local ordinances. SF’s Rent Ordinance is stricter ⇒ it controls everything inside these tenancies. Federal law adds only anti-discrimination overlay.

* Only one of the two 1992–93 increases may be imposed, based on anniversary date — note preserved from the official document.
