Notifying Tenants — the Correct Way, and Lawful Reasons

The exact notice mechanics for Unit 1 and Unit 2, the contents the law requires, service rules, and a candor list of what counts (and never counts) as a legitimate reason.

C. Model notice contents (structure, not legal form)

NOTICE OF CHANGE OF TERMS OF TENANCY — RENT INCREASE (San Francisco Rent Ordinance; Civ. Code §827) To: [Occupants], Unit [#], San Francisco, CA From: Owner, [Property] Date of notice: [date] Effective date of increase: [anniversary date] Current base rent: $[base].00 per month New base rent: $[new].00 per month Increase: $[delta].00 per month ([x]%) Rate applied: SF Rent Board allowable annual increase for [period]: [x]% Banked component (if any): [x]% from [years itemized, e.g., 1.4% Feb 2026] License: Rent Board Housing Inventory filing current as of [year] Service: [personal / mail — if mail, this notice is deposited on [date], adding 5 days per CCP §1013] Signature: ______ [owner/agent; agent contact info]

This skeleton is educational. Have counsel or the Rent Board produce the actual form; do not serve a homemade notice without review. Free help for tenants (who may scrutinize this): Rent Board counselors 415-252-4600; SF Tenants Union; Bay Area Legal Aid — see Tenant Review.

D. Fixed-term vs. month-to-month

Both tenancies appear to be month-to-month continuations (fixed terms expired years ago). Civ. Code §827 governs changes to month-to-month tenancies; if a written fixed-term lease were in force, rent could not change until it expired — and upon expiry the tenancy becomes periodic with rent unchanged. Verify there is no written lease currently in force with a different increase clause before serving anything. Civ. Code §827 (leginfo)
