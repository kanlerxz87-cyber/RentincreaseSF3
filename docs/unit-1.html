D. The April 1, 2026 continuing tenancy — why the rent did not (and could not) reset

  • Continuing tenancy, not a new tenancy. One initial occupant remains in lawful possession; at least one occupant continued in possession on Apr 1, 2026. Costa-Hawkins vacancy decontrol (Civ. Code §1954.53) lets an owner reset rent only when no tenant from the prior tenancy remains — that did not happen. Civ. Code §1954.53 (leginfo) · sf.gov
  • Rent stayed at $3,995. The client reports the rent was unchanged on Apr 1, 2026 — exactly what the law requires: no increase without a lawful notice, and no market reset without a full vacancy.
  • No “additional occupant” charge. Even with a new roommate added, landlords may not charge extra rent for that reason alone; such a charge is an unlawful increase. sf.gov — rent increases
  • Anniversary date unchanged. Because no increase was imposed, the anniversary remains February 1 — even if the landlord later raises off-cycle, the example in the official guidance shows an off-cycle increase creates a new anniversary and can forfeit banked increases not yet ripe (e.g., the 1.4% from Feb 1, 2026 could not be imposed before Feb 1, 2027). sf.gov — rent increases (examples)

E. Lawful increase roadmap for Unit 1

  1. Now → Jan 2027: confirm Housing Inventory license is current (sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory). Pick target date Feb 1, 2027.
  2. Decide amount: $4,114.85 (3.0% incl. banking) or $4,058.92 (1.6%, banking preserved for later — note banking does not expire during the tenancy).
  3. Serve written 30-day notice by Jan 2, 2027 (personal/mail CCP §1162 manner) — if mailed within CA from a CA address, deposit by Dec 28, 2026 (+5 days, CCP §1013).
  4. Notice contents: dollar amount, percentage, effective date, bank/no-bank split, rate in effect on effective date. Keep proof of service.
  5. Do not impose before Feb 1, 2027 — the 2025–26 increase only becomes bankable after 12 full months elapse (Feb 1, 2027). sf.gov — multiple years example
Watch out. If the client instead wants an increase effective sooner (e.g., Dec 2026), the only lawful basis would be the 1.4% banked year — and it is not yet ripe (12 months elapse Feb 1, 2027). Any earlier notice would be void. See FR-05/FR-06.
