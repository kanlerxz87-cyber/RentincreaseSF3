D. Notes on the 2019 lease date and Costa-Hawkins

  • The June 1, 2019 initial rate ($3,150) was lawfully set at whatever the market bore — Costa-Hawkins (Civ. Code §1954.53) lets an owner set the initial rent for a new tenancy, even in a rent-controlled building. Civ. Code §1954.53 (leginfo)
  • Every increase inside the tenancy since then is capped by the SF allowable amount — no unit tenure ever converts to “market.”
  • Banked increases survive a sale of the building; they do not transfer to a new tenancy if this one ends. sf.gov — banking
  • If the tenancy is unchanged since 2019, the landlord left ≈7 anniversaries of increases unused. SF allows reclaiming them — but the Rent Board can demand proof (ledger, prior notices, Housing Inventory) to support the banked component. sf.gov — banking
Watch item (FR-17). SF Legistar File #261020 (introduced 9/15/2026) would cap combined banked + capital-improvement increases at 10% per year for three years. E. Lawful increase roadmap for Unit 2

  1. Verify first (FR-01/FR-06): ledger 2019–today; Housing Inventory filings and license for each March 1 from 2023 on (1–9 unit building ⇒ requirement started Mar 1, 2023).
  2. Choose: conservative (1.6%, keeps banking intact) vs. full 11.5% now vs. 13.1% on Jun 1, 2027.
  3. If >10%: serve written notice 90 days before effective date; +5 days if mailed (95 total). If ≤10%: 30 days (+5 mailed).
  4. Contents: exact dollar amount, percentage, effective date, split between current-year and banked years (list each: e.g., “1.8% (June 2020) + 0.7% (2021) + 2.3% (2022) + 3.6% (2023) + 1.7% (2024) + 1.4% (2025) + 1.6% (2026–27)”).
  5. Do not combine with an “additional occupant” charge or rounding up. sf.gov
  6. Diary the next eligible increase 12 months after the effective date.
