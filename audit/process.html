Process & notice

StepAction / limitationOfficial source
1 · Verify the unitCheck the property record and exemptions privately. The owner’s 1940 description of the building and two described units and rent-controlled status are inputs, not independently verified property facts.SF rental-law coverage
2 · Reconstruct tenancyCheck leases, the last actual increase and base rent excluding temporary passthroughs. First annual increase requires 12 months; subsequent increases require at least 12 months from the last increase. Fixed-term lease restrictions must also be reviewed.Rent Board annual increases / notice · Civil Code §827
3 · Report and obtain licenseUse the Rent Board Portal for the Housing Inventory process. Reporting is free; required information includes owner/business contacts and unit/occupancy data. Reporting generates the license. Do not invent missing facts or submit a declaration without authority.Housing Inventory / license
4 · Check current licenseConfirm it is current for each unit when the increase takes effect. Annual deadline is March 1. Best practice: do this before service. No requirement to attach the license to the rent notice is stated; the City expressly says it need not be included.Housing Inventory / license
5 · Choose ordinary vs special increaseNo petition for the ordinary annual increase; banked increases need correct calculation, not Board approval. Certain capital-improvement, operating/maintenance and other increases have separate petitions, documentation and approval rules. Do not treat a cost increase as automatic permission.Official Form 571 · Rent Board banking · SF landlord petitions
6 · CalculateUse the rate in effect when the increase takes effect, base rent only; do not round up to the nearest dollar. Add eligible banked percentages, never compound or prorate them.Rent Board annual increases / notice · Rent Board banking
7 · Prepare written noticeState the dollar increase, percentage and effective date; identify any banked portion. Show old/new base rent and banking ledger as a recommended clarity measure. Review the lease and any additional requirements before service.Rent Board annual increases / notice · Rent Board banking · Civil Code §827
8 · Serve correctlyFor applicable periodic residential tenancies, §827 provides personal delivery or mail. At least 30 days for ≤10%; 90 days for >10%, considering increases over the preceding 12 months. SF guidance adds 5 days if mailed; confirm §1013 for the actual mailing circumstances. Email alone is not one of §827(b)(1)’s listed methods.Civil Code §827 · Rent Board annual increases / notice
9 · Retain proof and monitorKeep the license, rent history, computation and service record as a recommended review file. Recheck laws before service and the effective date; licensing is not immunity from review.Housing Inventory / license · Civil Code §827 · Civil Code §1942.5
ReasonsFor an ordinary annual increase, the published guidance lists notice amounts/timing, not a cost-justification petition. A truthful explanation is “the published annual allowable adjustment.” Do not invent expenses. Extra cost recovery may require a petition; never cite a new roommate or protected activity as the reason.Rent Board annual increases / notice · SF landlord petitions · Civil Code §1942.5

Separate obligations: Housing Inventory reporting is free; the Rent Board fee is separate and is generally due each year unless a valid exemption applies. A rent-increase license is not the same thing as a business registration; check any separate registration requirements. Rent Board fee guidance · Housing Inventory guidance.

Housing Inventory submissions are public according to the City. Website anonymity does not make the statutory filing private. Supply accurate legally required information through the official process; do not use website placeholder identities on government filings.

Public, anonymized research — not a private owner file and not legal advice. No tenant notices served or Housing Inventory declarations submitted. Official rules checked 7 November 2026; recheck before action. Dates and amounts supplied by the owner are preserved, not certified.
