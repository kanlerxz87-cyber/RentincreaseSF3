Federal law

Fair Housing Act, HUD regulation, antitrust enforcement, and the absence of a federal rent cap

There is no federal rent-control statute governing a private San Francisco tenancy. The federal constraints that do apply are the Fair Housing Act (including disparate-impact liability) and the antitrust laws as applied to rent-setting software.

F01 No federal rent cap

No federal statute or regulation caps the rent a private landlord may charge on an unassisted San Francisco unit.

No such provision was located in the sources reviewed. The federal rent-related instruments identified in this review are the Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. 3601 et seq.), the HUD fair-market-rent mechanism, and the antitrust laws.

F02 FHA terms and conditions

It is unlawful to discriminate in the terms, conditions or privileges of a rental because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status or national origin.

42 U.S.C. 3604(b): "To discriminate against any person in the terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental of a dwelling ... because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status, or national origin."

F03 FHA — who is liable

The FHA applies to these units: the small-owner exemptions do not fit a non-owner-occupied two-unit building.

42 U.S.C. 3603(b)(1) exempts a single-family house rented by a private owner of no more than three such houses; (b)(2) exempts rooms or units in a dwelling of no more than four families "if the owner actually maintains and occupies one of such living quarters as his residence".

F04 FHA — disparate impact

Liability can attach for a discriminatory effect even without discriminatory intent, subject to a legally sufficient justification.

24 C.F.R. 100.500: a practice has a discriminatory effect where it "actually or predictably results in a disparate impact on a group of persons or creates, increases, reinforces, or perpetuates segregated housing patterns". A justification must be necessary to a substantial, legitimate, non-discriminatory interest, supported by evidence, and not achievable by a less discriminatory alternative.

F05 Antitrust — algorithmic pricing

The Department of Justice obtained a consent judgment requiring RealPage to stop using competitors' non-public data to set rents.

DOJ release 25-1111 (24 Nov 2025): RealPage must cease using competitors' non-public competitively sensitive information in runtime operation; limit model training to data aged at least 12 months; not model geography narrower than state level; remove features limiting price decreases or aligning pricing; accept a court-appointed monitor; and cooperate against landlord defendants.

F06 HUD fair market rent

HUD fair market rents are 40th-percentile gross rent estimates used to set voucher payment standards. They are not a rent cap and are not a market-rent benchmark for a private increase.

HUD USER: FMRs "are estimates of 40th percentile gross rents for standard quality units within a metropolitan area or nonmetropolitan county" under 24 CFR 888.113, used for Housing Choice Voucher payment standards, project-based Section 8 renewals, HOME rent ceilings, and public housing flat rents.

F07 FHA protected classes

The federal protected classes are race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status and national origin.

42 U.S.C. 3602 definitions of handicap (physical or mental impairment substantially limiting a major life activity, a record of one, or being regarded as having one) and familial status (a person under 18 domiciled with a parent or custodian).
