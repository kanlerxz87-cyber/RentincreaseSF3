How to close each item

  • Rent increase licence (R02, U14). Look up both units in the Rent Board Portal (portal.sfrb.org). If a licence is missing, file the Housing Inventory report before serving any notice — without it the tenant owes nothing for the suspension period.
  • The February 2026 anniversary (U01). Establish whether a 1.4% increase was actually served on Unit 1 in February 2026. If it was, only the 1.6% is available now; if it was not, both bank.
  • The 2019 rent history (U11). Produce the 1 June 2019 lease and every notice since. The 13.1% calculation rests on $3,150 being the lawful base rent on that date.
  • The 180-day retaliation window (R08). Pull the repair and complaint log for both units before fixing an effective date.
  • The banking threshold (L13). Ask a Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) whether a skipped year banks after 12 months or 24 months. It does not affect Unit 2 either way.
  • Coverage and title (L01, S07). Confirm the certificate of occupancy on the Planning Property Information Map, and confirm the building has not been condominium-converted, which would open the Costa-Hawkins analysis.

L13 Value unverified San Francisco law

Two official Rent Board pages state different waiting periods before a skipped increase may be banked.

Rule 4.12 (the adopted regulation, doc 625, amended through 1/13/2026): "A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable." The Rent Board's plain-language page says: "A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase."

Why it is here: The regulation and the summary page disagree. Both sources are official. This does not change the Unit 2 result, because every year used for Unit 2 is more than 24 months old. It would change results for a single recently-skipped year. Resolve by calling a Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) before relying on banking for a recent year.

Source: 625 — Complete Rent Board Rules and Regulations (amended 1/13/2026)www.sf.gov · primary source

No such provision was located in the sources reviewed. The federal rent-related instruments identified in this review are the Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. 3601 et seq.), the HUD fair-market-rent mechanism, and the antitrust laws.

S07 Needs a decision California law

Costa-Hawkins allows an owner to set initial and subsequent rates for separately alienable units, subject to exceptions.

Sec. 1954.52(a)(3) applies to a dwelling "alienable separate from the title to any other dwelling unit", but not where the preceding tenancy was terminated on notice under 1946.1 or by a change in terms under 827.

Why it is here: as fact-dependent. Two units in one 1940 structure are normally not separately alienable unless the building has been condominium-converted. The title structure was not provided, so no exemption is assumed.

Source: California Civil Code § 1954.52 — Costa-Hawkins exemptionsleginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source

F06 Needs a decision Federal law

HUD fair market rents are 40th-percentile gross rent estimates used to set voucher payment standards. They are not a rent cap and are not a market-rent benchmark for a private increase.

HUD USER: FMRs "are estimates of 40th percentile gross rents for standard quality units within a metropolitan area or nonmetropolitan county" under 24 CFR 888.113, used for Housing Choice Voucher payment standards, project-based Section 8 renewals, HOME rent ceilings, and public housing flat rents.

Why it is here: The numeric FY2026 two-bedroom figure for the San Francisco metro did not render on the HUD documentation page, so no dollar value is published on this site. as unverified.

R11 Needs a decision Review test — one unit or both

Neither the Rent Ordinance nor the FHA requires that two units receive the same increase.

No provision reviewed requires uniform treatment across units. The FHA prohibits discrimination because of a protected characteristic; it does not impose a most-favoured-tenant rule. The Rent Board computes the allowance per tenancy, from that tenancy's own base rent and anniversary date.

Why it is here: as an inference from the structure of the ordinance rather than a quoted prohibition. It is a strong inference — the computation is tenancy-specific by design — but it is not a cited holding.

Source: 571 — Allowable Annual Rent Increases (3/1/26 – 2/28/27)www.sf.gov · primary source

R13 Needs a decision Review test — one unit or both

The discrimination risk is highest when the treated group is small and the distinction is unexplained.

With two units, a differential can look like selection. The mitigation is a written record created before the notices: the same method applied to both, the same notice template, the same effective-date logic, and a short written basis for each number.

Why it is here: as risk management judgement, not a citation. It follows from the disparate-impact standard in F04 rather than from any single rule.

Source: California Government Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discriminationleginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source

R15 Needs a decision Review test — one unit or both

Recommended course: apply the same method to both units, and record the calculation for each.

Serve each unit the allowance its own anniversary date and history supports; use one notice template; attach a one-page calculation showing base rent, rate, banked years and arithmetic; keep proof of service; and log a counseling call with the Rent Board beforehand.

Why it is here: as a recommendation. It is a procedure assembled from verified requirements, not a legal opinion. It is what makes the file reviewable.
