Executive summary

Two rent-controlled flats in one 1940 two-unit building in San Francisco. This page answers, in order: (1) whether an individual owner must report to the Rent Board and hold a rent increase licence before serving a notice; (2) the maximum lawful increase for each unit; (3) what to serve, when, and to whom; and (4) what could not be verified. Verified 7 November 2026.

1. The Rent Board licence question — answered first

Short answer: yes, an individual owner of a two-unit building must report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory and must hold a rent increase licence in effect on the date the increase takes effect. Being an individual rather than a corporation changes nothing.

What the official sources actually say
QuestionOfficial answer (quoted)Source
Does every owner have to report, or only companies? “All owners of residential property are required to report.” “Most owners are required to do this even if you are not a landlord.” sf.gov
Does a 2-unit building have to comply? Reporting began 1 March 2023 “for condominiums and units in buildings with fewer than 10 units.” For buildings of 10+ units the law was operative 1 July 2022. sf.gov
Is there a separate application for the licence? “Once you have reported into the Housing Inventory, a rent increase license will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units.” Reporting is “free”. sf.gov
When must the licence exist? “A landlord must have a current rent increase license on file with the Rent Board before any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect.” “your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice.” sf.gov
Deadline? “March 1 of every year.” Late filing is allowed and “The Rent Board does not assess monetary penalties for filing after the deadline” — but the notice stays unenforceable until the licence exists. sf.gov
Is the licence part of the ordinance itself? § 37.3(a)(1): “A landlord who has reported the required information about their rental unit to the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose annually a rent increase…” § 37.3
Do I have to “inform the Rent Board” of each increase? For annual and banked increases, no petition or approval is required: “Landlords are not required to file a petition with the City to impose these types of rent increases”; “Banked increases do not have to be approved by the Rent Board”. The licence does “not need to be included with your rent increase notice”. sf.gov · sf.gov
Which increases do need a petition? Only six types: capital improvement, operating & maintenance, special circumstances/comparable rents, Proposition I rent history, utility passthrough, and Rule 1.21 — none of which is an annual or banked increase. sf.gov

Analysis So the practice is: report once a year into the Housing Inventory (by 1 March), confirm the licence shows for each tenant-occupied unit, then serve the statutory written notice on the tenant. Nothing is filed with the Rent Board about the increase itself. Full ten-step walk-through: License & process.

2. Step-by-step (condensed)

  1. Confirm the building is covered. Built 1940, two units, so it is on the pre-13 June 1979 side of the line and is not a separately-sold single-family home or condominium. sf.gov — rental laws. Building facts are the owner's statement — see flag F-7.
  2. Report into the Housing Inventory at portal.sfrb.org for both units; confirm a rent increase licence was generated for each tenant-occupied unit. sf.gov
  3. Fix each tenancy's anniversary date and rent history — Unit 1: 1 February (lease began 1 Feb 2025); Unit 2: 1 June (lease began 1 Jun 2019). sf.gov
  4. Add the percentages, do not compound. Multiply the summed percentage by the current base rent only. No rounding up. sf.gov
  5. Check the banking time test. A skipped increase is banked only after 24 full months without an increase on the current Banking page; a worked example on another official page uses 12. sf.gov — banking · sf.gov — example · flag F-1
  6. Choose the effective date — the rate that applies is the one in effect on the effective date, not the date of the notice. sf.gov
  7. Serve written notice 30 days ahead if the increase is 10% or less, 90 days if more, plus 5 days if mailed. Civ. Code § 827
  8. State the amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which part is banked. sf.gov · § 37.3(b)
  9. Keep proof of service and the rent-history file. A tenant may petition the Rent Board for free, with no time limit, to challenge an unlawful increase. sf.gov
  10. Give no reason tied to a protected characteristic, a complaint, a repair request or a new occupant. Gov. Code § 12955 · Civ. Code § 1942.5

6. Notice and reasons

  • Written notice is required; 30 days if the increase is 10% or less, 90 days if more, and 5 extra days if mailed. Civ. Code § 827
  • The notice must state the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which part is banked; § 37.3(b) also requires the portions attributable to annual/banked and to any operating-cost, comparable-rent or capital-improvement component to be identified. sf.gov · § 37.3(b)
  • No official source requires a justification for an annual or banked increase — the required contents are an exhaustive list on both the sf.gov page and § 37.3(b). Analysis
  • Reasons that must never be given: anything tied to a protected characteristic (Gov. Code § 12955; 42 U.S.C. § 3604), retaliation for repairs or complaints within 180 days (Civ. Code § 1942.5), or the arrival of an additional occupant (sf.gov).

7. One unit or both?

Neither the Rent Ordinance nor the cited state and federal statutes require identical increases across different tenancies; each tenancy's ceiling is computed from its own base rent, anniversary date and rent history. What is unlawful is a difference that is because of a protected characteristic or in retaliation. Gov. Code § 12955 · 42 U.S.C. § 3604 · Civ. Code § 1942.5

Analysis The defensible pattern is one written rule applied to both units on the same schedule, with each unit's rent history documented before service. Unit 1 at 3.0% and Unit 2 at 10.0% look different because the inputs differ by six skipped years, and that difference is documented. Raising one unit and not the other is not automatically unlawful, but it is the version that invites the question. Full matrix and stress test: Is it reasonable and normal?

8. What could not be verified

Twelve items are published on Flagged for review with their sources. The three that affect the numbers:

  • F-1 — two official pages disagree on banking. The Banking page requires 24 full months; a worked example on the “Learn about rent increases” page banks an increase after 12. Unit 1 passes the 24-month test on 1 Feb 2027 either way; Unit 2 passes on 1 Jun 2027 either way.
  • F-2 — Unit 2 rent history is unverified. 13.1% assumes no increase since 1 Jun 2019. Without the history the figure is conditional.
  • F-9 — 90-day timing. A >10% increase effective 1 Feb 2027 would have needed service by 3 Nov 2026 (29 Oct 2026 if mailed), so it is already out of reach.

Also blank, not guessed: any ZIP-level market rent benchmark (F-5) and the 2027–28 allowable rate, which the Rent Board has not yet published (F-3).
