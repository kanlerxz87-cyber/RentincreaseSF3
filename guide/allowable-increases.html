How the percentage is set each year

RuleOfficial textSource
Formula“On March 1st of each year, the Board shall publish the increase in the CPI for the preceding 12 months… A landlord may impose annually a rent increase which does not exceed a tenant's base rent by more than 60 percent of said published increase. In no event, however, shall the allowable annual increase be greater than seven percent.”§ 37.3(a)(1)
Current figure“For rent-controlled units, the annual allowable increase amount effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027 is 1.6%.” Announcement dated 14 January 2026.sf.gov
Basis of the calculation“To calculate the dollar amount of the 1.6% annual rent increase, multiply the tenant's base rent by .016.”571
Frequency“A landlord may increase the tenant's base rent once every 12 months.”571
Petition needed?“…without filing a petition at the Rent Board.”571
2027–28 rateBlank — not yet published as of 7 Nov 2026F-3

Analysis Average of the eight most recent published years (1.6, 1.4, 1.7, 3.6, 2.3, 0.7, 1.8, 2.6) = 1.96%. Ten-year average = 1.95%. These are arithmetic on the official rows, not a forecast and not a legal limit.

Banking rules (what makes a skipped year recoverable)

RuleOfficial textSource
Time test for banking“A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date.”sf.gov
Conflicting worked exampleA different official page banks an increase once “12 full months has elapsed since the skipped rent increase first became available”, and says an increase is “permanently lost” where 12 months have not elapsed.sf.gov · F-1
Is there a cap on banked amounts?“There is no limit on how much can be banked.” “There is no limit to the amount of rent increases the landlord can bank (back to April 1, 1982).”sf.gov
Do banked increases expire?“Banked increases do not expire during a tenancy.”sf.gov
When can they be imposed?“on a future anniversary date, or at another time when they are allowed to raise the rent (as long as at least 12 months have passed since the last increase).”sf.gov
Partial increases“If only part of the allowable increase is used, the rest is banked.”sf.gov
Arithmetic“Banked increases… cannot be compounded or prorated.” Add the percentages, then multiply by the current base rent.sf.gov
Approval“Banked increases do not have to be approved by the Rent Board, but must be calculated correctly.”sf.gov
New owner“New owners may use banked increases from prior owners.”sf.gov
New tenant“Banked increases do not carry over to a new tenant.”sf.gov
Start date“Banking has only been allowed since April 1, 1982. Skipped increases before that date cannot be banked.”sf.gov
Notification“Effective July 1, 2022 (or March 1, 2023 for condominiums and buildings with 1-9 residential units), a landlord must obtain a rent increase license before imposing annual allowable and/or banked rent increases.”sf.gov
Pending changeFile 261020 would cap combined banked and capital-improvement increases at 10% a year for three years. Status 7 Nov 2026: Pending Committee Action — not law.Legistar · F-10

Analysis This is why “the rent can go up 1.6%” is the wrong mental model for Unit 2. The annual allowance is a rate that accrues per year to a tenancy; skipping years does not delete it, it parks it — subject to the 24-month test and to whatever the Board of Supervisors enacts next.
