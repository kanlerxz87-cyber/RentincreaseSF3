The correct way to inform tenants

Every step below is taken from official San Francisco Rent Board guidance and the California Civil Code. Follow them in order.

Step-by-step procedure (annual / banked increases)

  1. Confirm the unit is covered by the Rent Ordinance (built on/before Jun 13, 1979 and not exempt). Source: sf.gov — rental laws.
  2. Report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory and obtain a rent increase license. Required for all owners; for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 residential units since March 1, 2023. “Your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice.” Deadline each year: March 1. Source: sf.gov — Housing Inventory.
  3. Compute the increase: current annual rate in effect on the effective date + banked percentages for skipped years (added, not compounded, no rounding up), applied to the tenant's base rent only. Once per 12 months. Source: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases, sf.gov — banked increases.
  4. Serve written notice in advance:
    • 30 days before the effective date if the increase is ≤ 10% of the rent charged in the prior 12 months (alone or combined);
    • 90 days if it is > 10%;
    • +5 days if mailed.
    Source: Cal. Civ. Code § 827(b); sf.gov — Learn about rent increases.
  5. Notice content: dollar amount of the increase; percentage; date it goes into effect; which part (and which years) is banked. Use the percentage in effect on the effective date, not the date of service. Source: sf.gov — banked increases.
  6. Do not charge for occupants: a landlord “cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a unit with the existing tenant” — such a charge is unlawful even if the lease says so. Source: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases.
  7. Keep records: lease, base rent, every notice, effective dates, the rate table used, and the license on the effective date. Tenants can petition the Rent Board alleging an illegal rent increase (Form 516) and can obtain free legal help, so documentation must be complete. Source: sf.gov — Rent Board Forms Center, sf.gov — Tenant and Landlord Resources.

Reasonable, recognized reasons for a rent increase

In San Francisco, a rent increase must fit one of the framework's recognized bases. “Reasonableness” is built into using the right basis with the right documentation.

Reason / basisWhat it allowsDocumentation / approval neededSource (official)
Annual allowable adjustment (CPI)The Rent Board percentage (currently 1.6%) once every 12 months, on base rent No petition; license + proper notice only sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
Banked (skipped) annual increasesAny un-imposed allowable percentages from prior years, added (not compounded) No petition; correct math + itemize in notice; license sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Operating & maintenance cost growthAdditional base-rent increase up to 7% when documented O&M expenses grew faster than the annual allowance (comparison of two calendar years or a 24-month period; debt service excluded) Rent Board petition (Form 530) BEFORE the notice; notice void if served first; imposed only on the anniversary date sf.gov — O&M petitions (Topic 322)
Capital improvementsAmortized passthrough of certified improvement costs (7/10/15/20-year schedules at the current imputed interest rates, 4.1%–4.8% for 2026–27) Petition first (certified costs); computed separately from annual increases sf.gov — Landlord Petitions (Topic 301), sf.gov — Current Rates
Utility / gas & electricity passthroughsPassthrough of utility cost increases (after 1 year of occupancy) Petition or Rent Board worksheet filed before notice sf.gov — Landlord Petitions (Topic 301)
Special circumstances (comparable units)Reset toward comparable rents — only on proof of extraordinary circumstances (e.g., initial rent set very low due to a special relationship/fraud); “The mere fact that a long-standing tenant is paying significantly less than market rent is not considered an extraordinary circumstance.” One time per tenancy; precludes all prior annual/banked/O&M increases Special Circumstances Petition (Form 531) first sf.gov — Special Circumstances (Topic 323)
New tenancy on a vacated unit (Costa-Hawkins)“There is no limit on how much rent a landlord may first charge a tenant when renting an empty unit that is covered by rent control” Only after an actual vacancy (all occupants leave); petition route: Form 537 / Rules § 6.14 sf.gov — Learn about rent increases, sf.gov — Forms Center
Not reasonable / not allowed (officially): increases without a license; increases above the allowable without a qualifying petition; rounding or compounding; more than once per 12 months; charging extra merely because occupants changed; short or missing notice; increases that differ because of a tenant's protected characteristics (see Legal Framework). Tenants may petition the Rent Board claiming an illegal rent increase — Forms Center.

Scrutiny environment (why documentation matters)

SF tenants can obtain free counseling (Rent Board 415-252-4600) and free legal representation (Eviction Defense Collaborative / Right to Counsel program and other tenant counselors listed by the city) — sf.gov — Tenant and Landlord Resources, sf.gov — Evictions. Any increase should be prepared as if it will be reviewed by a tenant-side lawyer: correct rate, correct math, correct notice, license in place, neutral documented rationale.
