Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. § 3604(b) “To discriminate against any person in the terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental of a dwelling … because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status, or national origin” is unlawful A rent increase is a “term” of rental — it must never vary because of a protected characteristic; handicap discrimination separately barred by § 3604(f) U.S. GPO — 42 U.S.C. § 3604

Federal rent control [ no federal rent-ceiling law exists to cite ] Federal law's role here is anti-discrimination (above), not rate-setting; rates come from local law U.S. GPO — 42 U.S.C. § 3604 (the federal provision cited in this study governs discrimination only)
Practical hierarchy for these units: the SF Rent Ordinance rate (1.6% + banked) is the controlling ceiling — it is stricter than the state cap (which does not apply) and there is no federal ceiling. State and federal law then govern how you notify (30/90 days) and fairness (no discrimination), while the Rent Ordinance governs how much and how often.
