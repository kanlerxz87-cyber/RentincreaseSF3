R09 Compliance test

Is the increase free of any discriminatory selection effect?

The FHA reaches disparate impact without intent. The test is whether the pattern of who receives an increase, and how much, tracks a protected characteristic rather than a lawful variable such as anniversary date or banked years.

R11 One unit or both

Neither the Rent Ordinance nor the FHA requires that two units receive the same increase.

No provision reviewed requires uniform treatment across units. The FHA prohibits discrimination because of a protected characteristic; it does not impose a most-favoured-tenant rule. The Rent Board computes the allowance per tenancy, from that tenancy's own base rent and anniversary date.

R13 One unit or both

The discrimination risk is highest when the treated group is small and the distinction is unexplained.

With two units, a differential can look like selection. The mitigation is a written record created before the notices: the same method applied to both, the same notice template, the same effective-date logic, and a short written basis for each number.

R14 One unit or both

Withholding a lawful increase from one unit is not itself unlawful, but it creates its own record problem.

A landlord may impose less than the allowance, and the unused portion banks (Ord. 37.3(a)(2); Rule 4.12). So declining to increase one unit does not forfeit the entitlement — it converts it into a banked amount that can be imposed later.

 Unused amount banks Verified Sec. 37.3 — Rent Limitations (full text)www.sf.gov · primary source
R15 One unit or both

Recommended course: apply the same method to both units, and record the calculation for each.

Serve each unit the allowance its own anniversary date and history supports; use one notice template; attach a one-page calculation showing base rent, rate, banked years and arithmetic; keep proof of service; and log a counseling call with the Rent Board beforehand.

R16 What a lawyer will look for

A reviewer can invalidate the whole increase on a single formal defect.

Ordinance 37.3(b)(5) and Rule 4.10(b): a non-conforming increase is null and void in its entirety, subject only to a good-faith adjustment of up to 0.5% of prior base rent. The defect does not have to be large.
