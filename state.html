S01 Notice period

Civil Code 827 requires 30 days' notice for an increase of 10% or less, and 90 days for an increase greater than 10%, measured against the lowest rent charged in the prior 12 months.

Sec. 827(b)(2)-(3): 30 days if the increase is 10 percent or less "either in and of itself or when combined with any other rent increases for the 12 months before the effective date"; 90 days if greater than 10 percent.

S02 Service

Notice must be delivered personally, or by mail following Code of Civil Procedure 1013.

Sec. 827(b)(1): "(A) By delivering a copy to the tenant personally. (B) By serving a copy by mail under the procedures prescribed in Section 1013 of the Code of Civil Procedure."

S03 State cap

The Tenant Protection Act caps increases at 5% plus the change in cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower, over any 12 months.

Sec. 1947.12(a)(1), measured against the lowest gross rent charged in the prior 12 months; no more than two increments in 12 months (a)(2).

 5% + CPI, max 10% Verified California Civil Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent capleginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source
S04 Local cap displacement

The state cap does not apply where a local ordinance restricts annual increases to less than the state cap.

Sec. 1947.12(d)(3): "Housing subject to rent or price control through a public entity's valid exercise of its police power ... that restricts annual increases in the rental rate to an amount less than that provided in subdivision (a)."

 SF cap of 1.6% controls Verified California Civil Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent capleginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source
S05 State cap table

The Attorney General publishes 8.8% for the San Francisco area for increases starting 8/1/2026 – 7/31/2027.

S06 Owner-occupied duplex

The state rent cap exempts a two-unit structure where the owner occupied one unit at the start of the tenancy and still does.

Sec. 1947.12(d)(6): "A property containing two separate dwelling units within a single structure in which the owner occupied one of the units as the owner's principal place of residence at the beginning of the tenancy, so long as the owner continues in occupancy..."

 Not applicable here Verified California Civil Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent capleginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source
S07 Costa-Hawkins

Costa-Hawkins allows an owner to set initial and subsequent rates for separately alienable units, subject to exceptions.

Sec. 1954.52(a)(3) applies to a dwelling "alienable separate from the title to any other dwelling unit", but not where the preceding tenancy was terminated on notice under 1946.1 or by a change in terms under 827.

S08 Vacancy decontrol

When a unit is truly vacant and no prior tenant remains, the owner may set the initial rent, and the cap then applies to later increases.

Sec. 1954.53(a); sec. 1947.12(b) is to the same effect for the state cap.

 Initial rate is free Verified California Civil Code § 1954.53 — initial rental rate / vacancy decontrolleginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source
S09 Retaliation

A rent increase within 180 days of a tenant's habitability complaint or tenant-organising activity is prohibited, and attorney's fees are available.

Sec. 1942.5(a) bars increasing rent within 180 days of a good-faith repair notice or complaint to an agency; (d) bars increases for retaliating against organising; (h) sets damages including punitive damages of $100-$2,000 per act; (i) provides attorney's fees to the prevailing party.

 180-day bar Verified California Civil Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibitedleginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source
S10 Just cause

After 12 months of tenancy an owner may not terminate without just cause stated in the notice.

Sec. 1946.2(a); if additional adult tenants are added before an existing tenant reaches 24 months, the 12-month clock requires all tenants to reach 12 months or one tenant to reach 24 months.

 Just cause required Verified California Civil Code § 1946.2 — just causeleginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source
S11 Translation

A residential lease over one month negotiated primarily in Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese or Korean requires a translated copy before signing.

Sec. 1632(b)(3) covers "a lease, sublease, rental contract or agreement ... for a period of longer than one month, covering a dwelling."

 5 covered languages Verified California Civil Code § 1632 — translation of rental agreement (Spanish/Chinese/Tagalog/Vietnamese/Korean)leginfo.legislature.ca.gov · primary source
S12 State civil rights

The Fair Employment and Housing Act bars discrimination in housing on 16 listed characteristics, including source of income and familial status.

Gov. Code 12955(a): race, color, religion, sex, gender, gender identity, gender expression, sexual orientation, marital status, national origin, ancestry, familial status, source of income, disability, veteran or military status, genetic information.
