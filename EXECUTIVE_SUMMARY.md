## 1. The question that comes first: do I have to inform the Rent Board and get a licence — as an individual owner, not a corporation?

**Yes. Owner type makes no difference.**

San Francisco requires **all** owners of residential property to report into the **Rent Board
Housing Inventory** each year. Reporting a *tenant-occupied* unit generates a **rent-increase
licence** automatically. That licence must be **in effect on the effective date** of any annual or
banked increase. If it is not, the increase **may be null and void** — "this may apply for multiple
years" — and the tenant may be entitled to a rent refund.

The ordinance distinguishes by **building size**, not by whether the owner is a person or a company:
10 or more units from **1 July 2022**; condominiums and buildings with **fewer than 10 units** from
**1 March 2023**. This is a two-unit building, so the March 2023 date applies.

What you do **not** have to do:

- you do not file the rent-increase notice with the Rent Board;
- you do not apply for approval of the increase;
- you do not petition for an annual or banked increase;
- you do not send the licence to the tenant — "The rent increase license does not need to be included
  with your rent increase notice."

The licence is a **precondition**, not an approval of the amount.

## 8. How large owners and individual owners differ

The ordinance, the allowance and the licence are identical for a two-unit building and a thousand-unit
portfolio. What differs is process.

## 9. Bottom line

1. **Yes, the licence is required**, and no owner type is exempt. File the Housing Inventory, confirm the
   licence covers the effective date, then serve notice. Reversing that order voids the increase.
2. **Unit 1:** the defensible step on 1 February 2027 is **3.0% = $119.85 → $4,114.85**, made of the
   unused 1.4% and the current 1.6%, on 30 days' notice. A 1.6%-only step of $63.92 is also lawful and
   leaves the 1.4% available.
3. **Unit 2:** $412.65 (13.1%) is the verified banked **balance**, needing 90 days' notice; the June 2027
   ceiling is **higher**, because the 2027-28 allowance is unpublished. A 10.0% step of $315.00 is lawful
   on 30 days' notice and stays inside the pending 10% cap.
4. **Do both units under one written policy**, each on its own anniversary, with the same method,
   template and documentation.
5. **Open items that block certainty, not the plan:** Unit 2's rent history, this building's licence
   status, whether Unit 1's 1.4% year was ever charged, and the unpublished 2027-28 rate.
