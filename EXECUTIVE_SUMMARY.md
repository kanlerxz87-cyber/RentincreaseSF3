# Executive summary

**Yes: an individual owner must report residential units to the San Francisco Rent Board Housing Inventory and have a current rent-increase license on file for an annual or banked increase to take effect.** Obtain it before serving notice as a prudent precaution; the Rent Board specifies the effective date as the critical deadline. A license does not prevent a tenant from challenging an increase. [Official Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory).

For covered units, the annual allowable component is **1.6%** from March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027. A conservative option is that amount or less, subject to actual coverage, lawful base rent, timing, lease, license and proper notice. On an assumed base rent of $3,995, 1.6% is $63.92 (illustrative total $4,058.92); on an assumed base rent of $3,150, it is $50.40 (illustrative total $3,200.40). Neither current base rent nor eligibility has been independently verified. [Rent Board rate and formula](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced).

Some banked increases or separate passthroughs may be lawful, but history is missing. No rule found requires both units to be raised; use neutral, consistent criteria and do not retaliate or discriminate. No attorney has reviewed these facts. [Banking](https://www.sf.gov/information/banked-rent-increases) · [Tenant petitions](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-petitions) · [Fair housing](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/housing/).
