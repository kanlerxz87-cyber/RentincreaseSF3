# Executive Summary — Rental Increases for Two San Francisco Units (Location Withheld)

**Forefront first: the rent-increase license. Private owner ≠ exempt.** San Francisco requires **every** owner of residential property — individuals and corporations alike — to report annually to the **Rent Board Housing Inventory** and hold a current **rent-increase license** on the effective date of any annual or banked rent increase. Without it, even a perfectly calculated 1.4% or 1.6% increase is **null and void** and can be overturned on a tenant petition.

> **Do I, as an individual owner (not a corporation), need to inform the Rent Board and get a license before notifying tenants?**
> **Yes.** The ordinance distinguishes by *building size*, not ownership form: **10+ residential units** (reporting since July 1, 2022) vs. **condos and 1–9 unit buildings** (since March 1, 2023). A 2-unit building is in the second group. Reporting a tenant-occupied unit automatically generates the license — there is no separate application — but the license must be **in effect on the effective date** of the increase, not just the notice date.

Verified against official sources on **7 November 2026**. Rates and pending legislation change; re-check before serving any notice. This is an information record, not legal advice.

## 0. Forefront: Step-by-step — what an individual owner must do before notifying tenants

|---|---|---|
| **1. Confirm coverage** | Building built 1940 → certificate of occupancy before June 13, 1979 → presumptively covered by SF Rent Ordinance (Admin. Code Ch. 37). | **2. File Housing Inventory** | Report **every** residential unit via the **Rent Board Portal** ([portal.sfrb.org](https://portal.sfrb.org/)) — due **March 1 every year**. Provide: owner/manager contact, business registration if any, approx. square footage in 250-sq-ft increments, bedrooms/bathrooms, occupancy/vacancy dates in past 12 months, base rent in $250 increments + utility info for tenant-occupied units. Under penalty of perjury. Update contact changes within 30 days. | [sf.gov — Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) |
| **3. Confirm the license** | After filing a tenant-occupied unit, a **rent-increase license is automatically generated** — no separate application. | **4. Calculate correctly** | Multiply **base rent only** (incl. housing services like parking/storage; excl. passthroughs/fluctuating charges) by the **allowable % in effect on the effective date**: **1.4%** (3/1/2025–2/28/2026) or **1.6%** (3/1/2026–2/28/2027). No rounding up. One increase per 12 months; anniversary date controls; banked amounts additive, not compounded. | [sf.gov — rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [Form 571 PDF](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) · [sf.gov — 1.6% announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced) |

Use rate in effect on *effective* date. | [sf.gov — Giving notice](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [Civ. Code §827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=827) · [CCP §1013](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1013) |

**Order matters: File → confirm license → serve notice.** Noticing first and filing after invalidates the increase. This is the most common reason lawful-percentage increases are overturned.

## 1. The applicable ceiling

Both units are in a 1940 two-unit building, i.e. certificate of occupancy before 13 June 1979, so the San Francisco Rent Ordinance (Administrative Code Chapter 37) presumptively applies and rent cannot be reset to market while a tenant from the original tenancy remains in possession.

| Rate period | Allowable annual increase | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3/1/2026 – 2/28/2027 | **1.6%** | [sf.gov — 1.6% announcement](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced) · [Form 571 PDF](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) |
| 3/1/2025 – 2/28/2026 | **1.4%** | [sf.gov — rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) |
| 3/1/2024 – 2/28/2025 | 1.7% | [Form 571 PDF](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf) |

Formula is 60% of prior year's Bay Area CPI change, capped at 7% (Admin. Code §37.3(a)(1)). Average of last eight published years: 1.96%.

The statewide Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482, Civ. Code §1947.12) does **not** set the ceiling here: §1947.12(d)(3) exempts housing already subject to a local ordinance that restricts increases to a lower amount. The SF-region AB 1482 cap would be **8.8%** for increases starting Aug 1, 2026–Jul 31, 2027 (5% + 3.8% CPI), but it does not govern these units. No federal statute caps rent; the federal rule that applies to increase *decisions* is the Fair Housing Act's ban on discriminatory terms ([42 U.S.C. §3604](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title42/html/USCODE-2023-title42-chap45-subchapI-sec3604.htm)).

Rules: percentages added, never compounded; on base rent only; nothing rounded up; one increase per 12 months; license prerequisite above.

The **1 April 2026 Unit 1 renewal**, at which a co-tenant joined a continuing original tenant, is **not** a vacancy and **cannot** support extra rent — SF prohibits increases solely because an additional occupant moved in, even where the lease allows a charge. ## 4. Noticing

Written notice is required ([Civ. Code §827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=827)): **30 days** if ≤10%, **90 days** if >10%, **+5 extra days if mailed** ([CCP §1013](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1013)). Notice must state dollar amount, percentage, effective date, which part is annual vs. banked, and dates the banked amount is based on. A **current rent-increase license must exist on the effective date** or the increase is unenforceable.

No legal requirement to give a *reason* for an annual or banked increase; reasons become mandatory only for capital-improvement or operating-and-maintenance petitions. If a reason is given, it must never reference a protected characteristic, a complaint, a repair request, or an additional occupant.

## 5. One unit or both?

Neither the ordinance nor fair-housing law requires equal dollar increases; raising one unit is not unlawful by itself. Unlawful treatment arises when a difference is tied to a protected characteristic ([Gov. Code §12955](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=12955); [42 U.S.C. §3604](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title42/html/USCODE-2023-title42-chap45-subchapI-sec3604.htm)) or retaliation ([Civ. Code §1942.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1942.5)). The defensible pattern is **one written rule applied to both units on the same schedule**, with each unit's rent history documented before service. Different outcomes from seven skipped years vs. two are explainable; different rules for similarly placed tenants invite inquiry.

**Recommendation:** Increase **both units** under the same policy. See [pages/process.html](pages/process.html) for the one-vs-both matrix.

## 6. How owners actually behave

Institutional owners apply the published annual increase portfolio-wide on a calendar, track banked years in systems, use standard notices, and keep licenses current via bulk Portal filings. Individual owners often skip years informally and lose the 24-month record. San Francisco banned algorithmic rent-setting devices for residential units in 2024 ([Admin. Code §37.10C](https://www.sf.gov/news--new-law-prohibits-algorithmic-devices-used-set-rents-san-francisco)), and DOJ has pursued the largest pricing vendor.

## 8. Corporate vs. individual — direct answer

**You must follow the same process as a corporation.** There is no individual-owner exception. The Rent Board does not have a lighter track for private landlords: same Housing Inventory filing, same March 1 deadline, same license requirement, same allowable percentages, same notice rules, same petition rights for tenants. The practical difference is not entitlement but *administration* — corporations run it as a compliance calendar; individuals who treat it casually are the ones whose increases are overturned. For a private owner of a 2-unit building, the cleanest path is to mirror that calendar: file Mar 1, confirm license, calendar each anniversary, use one property-wide written policy, and have a tenant-side-experienced attorney review the two notices.
