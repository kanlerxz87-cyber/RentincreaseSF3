## 1. Forefront: must an individual owner inform the Rent Board and obtain a licence before notifying tenants?

**Yes to reporting and the licence; no to any per-increase application. And the requirement is a City law, not a state law — see “State or local?” below.**

**And it is a *local* law, not a state one.** The duty to inform the Rent Board and to hold a
rent-increase licence comes from **San Francisco Ordinance No. 265-20**, effective
**18 January 2021**, which amended the City's Rent Ordinance in the Administrative Code. California
requires no licence and no filing with any agency to raise rent. The year **2023** — the one in the
brief — is when the City rule reached small owners: reporting began **1 March 2023** for
condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units (1 July 2022 for larger buildings), with
updates due every 1 March. →
[Ordinance 265-20 explained by the Rent Board](https://www.sf.gov/news--new-legislation-creates-housing-inventory-and-requires-owners-obtain-license-imposing) ·
[Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) ·
[the state cap, Civ. Code § 1947.12](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1947.12)

San Francisco requires **all owners of residential property** — individuals exactly as much as corporations — to report their units into the **Rent Board Housing Inventory**. For a tenant-occupied unit, the report **automatically generates a rent-increase licence**, and that licence must be **in effect on the effective date** of the increase. Without it the notice is **not enforceable**. Annual and banked increases need no approval petition. A separate notice-copy filing requirement was not independently established (FR-20); confirm it with the Rent Board.

### State or local? Which level of government requires each step

**The requirement the owner had heard about is local, not state.** The duty to inform the Rent Board (by reporting into the Housing Inventory) and to hold a rent-increase licence was created by **San Francisco Ordinance No. 265-20**, adopted by the Board of Supervisors in 2020 and **effective 18 January 2021**, which amended the City's Rent Ordinance (Administrative Code, Chapter 37). What dates to **2023** is not the passage of a new law but the **phase-in**: the City states that reporting "began March 1, 2023 for condominiums and units in buildings with fewer than 10 units" (10-or-more-unit buildings began 1 July 2022). No California statute requires a landlord to notify a rent board or hold such a licence.

### Step by step (full version with dates on the site: `pages/process.html`)

1. **Confirm coverage.** Units built on or before 13 June 1979 generally have rent control and eviction protection, unless the unit is a single-family dwelling or most condominiums. The owner reports rent control and a 1940 building, but the building size, certificate of occupancy and exceptions remain unverified. → [sf.gov — SF rental laws](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-san-francisco-rental-laws) · counsellors **415-252-4600**
2. **Report into the Housing Inventory, every year by 1 March.** Portal account; per unit: owner/manager business contact, business registration if any, square footage in 250 sq ft increments, bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy start date, base rent in $250 increments and utility information. Under penalty of perjury; free; no monetary penalty for late filing. To add the property to a Portal account you need "the PIN number on your Annual Notice" (311 resets it); what you report is public. → [sf.gov — Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) · [portal.sfrb.org](https://portal.sfrb.org/)
3. **Confirm the licence is current on your planned effective date** and keep a screenshot with the date. → [sf.gov — Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory)
4. **Calculate on base rent only** — including housing services such as parking or storage, excluding passthroughs and fluctuating charges — using the percentage **in effect on the effective date**. No rounding up. For an effective date on or before 28 February 2027 that is **1.6%**. → [sf.gov — Learn about rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [Form 571](https://www.sf.gov/documents/34344/571_Allowable_Annual_Increases_26-27_1.pdf)
5. **Add any banked amounts additively, never compounded.** No cap back to 1 April 1982; they do not expire during the tenancy and do not carry to a new tenant. → [sf.gov — Banked rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/information--banked-rent-increases) · [sf.gov — multiple years](https://www.sf.gov/information/annual-rent-increase-multiple-years)
6. **Check the bar before the timing.** Civ. Code § 1942.4 forbids issuing a rent-increase notice while written official notice of substandard conditions stands unabated more than 35 days without good cause — check the inspection record for both units first. → [Civ. Code § 1942.4](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1942.4)
7. **Check timing.** First increase 12 months after the lease began; then once every 12 months; a later effective date becomes the new anniversary date. Avoid retaliation; Civil Code § 1942.5(a) addresses specified retaliatory acts within 180 days subject to its conditions, and other retaliation protections also apply. No extra charge because an occupant joined. → [sf.gov — rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [Civ. Code § 1942.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1942.5)
8. **Serve written notice:** 30 days if the increase is 10% or less of the rent charged in the prior 12 months (alone or combined), 90 days if more; add 5 days if mailed within California. State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, annual and banked portions and dates underlying banked amounts. → [Civ. Code § 827](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=827) · [CCP § 1013](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1013)
9. **Keep the file:** notice, proof of service, licence screenshot, calculation, rent history. Re-report by 1 March and update contact details within 30 days. → [sf.gov — Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory)
10. **If a tenant petitions:** free Tenant Petition (Form 516C, box C) for an unlawful rent increase or a lawful-rent determination, with no filing deadline, heard by a Rent Board Administrative Law Judge. → [sf.gov — unlawful rent increase](https://www.sf.gov/report-unlawful-rent-increase-or-request-review-your-rent-history) · [sf.gov — tenant petitions](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-petitions)

**Notice-date arithmetic (from 13 November 2026).** A 13.1% Unit B increase designed to take effect 1 February 2027 needed service by 3 November 2026 — that window has closed. Service on 10 or 11 November 2026 would have reached 8 or 9 February 2027, and both dates have now passed. As at **13 November 2026**: a notice personally served today reaches **11 February 2027** (90 days) at the earliest, or **13 December 2026** for a 30-day increase; by mail within California the same service reaches **16 February 2027** (95 days) or **18 December 2026** (35 days). Later service moves the date out again — recalculate from the actual service date. ## 3. What changed on 9 November 2026

The Rent Board added a notice to its rent-increase and banking pages on **9 November 2026**: the Board of Supervisors is considering **File No. 261020**, which would temporarily limit the combined amount of **banked rent increases and capital improvement rent increases** to **10% per year** for rent-controlled units, for three years. The Rent Board states that, if enacted as currently proposed, it would apply to banked increases **noticed on or after 15 September 2026**, and that the legislation "has not been enacted and is subject to change". Legistar shows the file as **Pending Committee Action** (introduced 15 September 2026, referred to the Land Use and Transportation Committee).

Practical effect: the illustrative **13.1%** total contains **11.5% banked**, exceeding the proposed combined banked-and-capital limit of 10%; the **1.6% annual** component is distinct. A **10.0%** total has at most 10% banked, assuming no capital-improvement increase; confirm eligibility, remaining bank and final law.

## 4. Reasonable and normal increases

There is **no official definition** of a "reasonable" or "normal" rent increase in San Francisco, and **no official statistic** for what owners typically charge. What exists is a **lawful maximum**, which is the Rent Board percentage plus properly banked amounts.

## 7. Would it survive a tenant lawyer?

A San Francisco tenant can file a free Tenant Petition (Form 516C, box C) for an unlawful rent increase or a determination of the lawful rent, with **no filing deadline**, heard by a Rent Board Administrative Law Judge; the process is designed so nobody needs a lawyer. The site sets out a **16-point test** covering coverage, percentage version, base-rent basis, rounding, banking, the 12-month interval, anniversary effects, licence on the effective date, Housing Inventory currency, 30/90-day period, proof of service, notice contents, retaliation, protected characteristics and the explainability of any difference between units.

The arithmetic is illustrative; no actual notice, service proof, rent ledger, licence or lawyer review was provided, so the checks cannot be marked as passed. They are not clearance to serve notice: the owner must first confirm Unit B's complete rent history, the licence status, and Unit A's anniversary date, then re-check the pending legislation and retaliation facts.

Sources: [sf.gov — unlawful rent increase](https://www.sf.gov/report-unlawful-rent-increase-or-request-review-your-rent-history) · [sf.gov — tenant petitions](https://www.sf.gov/tenant-petitions) · [CRD — housing](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/housing/)

## 8. One unit or both?

**No official source requires raising both units, and none says a selective increase is unlawful in itself.** FEHA prohibits discrimination *because of* a protected characteristic (race, colour, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, marital status, national origin, ancestry, familial status, source of income, disability, veteran or military status, genetic information); the federal Fair Housing Act covers race, colour, religion, sex, familial status, national origin and disability; the Civil Code separately prohibits retaliatory increases, with specific conditions and protections that can extend beyond 180 days. The Rent Board does **not** hear discrimination or retaliation claims — those go to the Civil Rights Department, HUD or court.

The reported base rents and lease dates differ. Whether skipped years differ requires the complete rent histories; different dollar amounts or percentages are not automatically discriminatory. Helpful documentation includes a written, dated, objective rationale per unit, the same method applied to both, a check of the 180-day window, and evidence of the licence. The site publishes this as an **absence of authority** (FR-12) rather than as a positive rule.
