P01 Institutional pricing method

A large single-family rental REIT states that it sets rents using a pricing tool built on publicly available data plus property-specific factors.

Invitation Homes 2025 Form 10-K: associates set rates "based on a tailored pricing tool that analyzes publicly available data to reflect local market conditions, including a competitive analysis of market rents for institutional single-family rental properties, as well as the size, fit and finish, and location of the home, the number of applications received, and the number of days a home has been available on the market."

P05 Individual owner method

An individual San Francisco owner's lawful method is to apply the published percentage, hold a licence, serve the statutory notice, and document the base rent.

The Rent Board sets out a complete procedure: confirm coverage; report into the Housing Inventory to obtain the rent increase licence; compute 1.6% (or 1.6% plus banked percentages) on base rent with no rounding up; serve 30 days' notice (90 if over 10%) stating dollar amount, percentage and effective date; and identify which portion is annual and which is banked.

P06 Which reasons are needed

For an annual or banked increase within the allowance, no justification or reason beyond the ordinance formula is required.

The Rent Board describes the annual increase as imposable "without filing a petition" (doc 571) and requires only the percentage and dollar figures, not a reason. Reasons and cost documentation become mandatory only for petition-based increases, which must be filed "on the forms provided by the Rent Board" and "must include supporting documentation".

P07 Reasons that are not allowed

Several commonly offered justifications are unlawful in San Francisco.

Not permitted: an increase because a roommate or family member moved in (Ord. 37.3(a)(11)); an increase in bad faith to induce the tenant to leave (Ord. 37.10B(a)(5)); an increase within 180 days of a habitability complaint (Civ. Code 1942.5); an increase calculated with revenue-management software (Ord. 37.10C); an increase that has not been computed on base rent only, or that has been rounded up.

P08 Reasons that are supportable

The defensible basis for these units is the published allowance plus documented banked percentages, tied to the tenancy's own anniversary date.

Because the allowance is fixed by the Board and applied to each tenancy's own base rent and anniversary date, the reason for the increase is the ordinance, and the explanation is arithmetic. That explanation is auditable from public sources.

P09 Notice delivery record

Keep proof of service and a copy of the notice; the tenant can request a full rent history review at no cost and with no deadline.

Service must be personal or by mail under CCP 1013 (Civ. Code 827(b)(1)). The Rent Board accepts a 516C petition at any time, free, and Form C asks for the complete rent history, receipts and cancelled cheques.
