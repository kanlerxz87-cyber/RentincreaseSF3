How owners handle increases

What large institutional owners do, what individual owners do, and which parts are documented in official sources.

Two different operating models, one set of rules

Institutional owners and individual owners face the same ordinance. What differs is process: scale, standardisation and the presence of pricing software and counsel. The table separates what is documented in official sources from what is general practice (labelled as such).

What that means for these two units

An institutional owner would almost certainly do three things that an individual owner often skips: file the Housing Inventory report every year, run the arithmetic from a documented rent roll rather than memory, and break out the banked portion year by year in the notice. Those three steps are precisely what a tenant petition attacks. S6 S14 S11

What an institutional owner would not do, and what is now illegal in San Francisco, is set these rents with algorithmic pricing software, or coordinate pricing with other owners. S23 S24

Where an individual owner should spend the effort

  1. Rebuild the rent roll from documents, not memory. S11
  2. Keep the licence current - an unlicensed increase is unenforceable however correct the maths. S6
  3. Use one template that shows the banked breakdown and the rate period. S14
  4. Serve early: 90 days is easy to miss and impossible to cure retroactively. S15
  5. Do not delegate the calculation to a spreadsheet formula that compounds or rounds. S2 S3
