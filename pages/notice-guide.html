How to notify a tenant, and on what grounds

San Francisco requires more than a correct number. The notice must be written, served with the right period, state the right content, identify each component separately, and be backed by a current rent increase license. A defect in any of these can void the entire increase even if the arithmetic was right.

The procedure

The procedure, in order. Do not serve a notice until every gate is cleared.
# Step What the official rule requires Source Status
1 Confirm rent-control coverage Units built on or before June 13, 1979 are generally covered. Verify the construction date on the San Francisco Property Information Map and confirm the unit is not separately alienable. Source: S7 · S21 Unverified – flagged
2 Obtain the complete rent history The base rent for every year since the tenancy began, every prior notice served, and every prior anniversary date. The burden of proving the correct base rent rests on the owner in a Form 354 petition. Source: S18 · S14 Unverified – flagged
3 Confirm the rent increase license is current A license must be on file on the effective date. If it is not, the entire annual or banked increase is null and void and the tenant may seek a refund. File or refresh the Housing Inventory at the Rent Board Portal; the deadline is March 1 but late filing is accepted with no monetary penalty. Source: S8 · S9 · S6 Unverified – flagged
4 Confirm no disqualifying condition No serious health, safety, fire or building code violations cited and unabated for six months or more before a vacancy (relevant to any market-reset theory); no pending tenant petition; no reduction in housing services. Source: S25 · S26 · S6 · S14 Verified
5 Compute the increase on base rent Base rent includes housing services such as parking and storage; it excludes temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges. Sum the matured banked percentages, add the annual rate in effect on the effective date, multiply by base rent, and truncate to the cent. Never round up. Source: S3 · S16 · S5 Verified
6 Confirm at least 12 months have passed Since the lease began, or since the last increase. The first increase is available 12 months after the lease began. Source: S3 · S16 Verified
7 Determine the notice period 30 days if the total increase is 10% or less of the rent charged at any time in the prior 12 months; 90 days if more than 10%, alone or combined with any other increase in that period. Add 5 days if the notice is mailed. Source: S23 · S3 Verified
8 Draft the notice with the mandatory contents See the required-contents table below. Omitting the banked-amount disclosure does not by itself void the increase, but omitting the dollar amount, percentage or effective date can. Source: S6 · S5 · S3 Verified
9 Serve it in writing and keep proof Personal delivery, or mail under Code of Civil Procedure Sec. 1013. Retain the notice, the proof of service and the calculation worksheet. Source: S23 · S16 Verified
10 Do not use an algorithmic pricing device Sec. 37.10C prohibits the use of algorithmic devices to set rents or manage occupancy levels for residential units in San Francisco. Source: S6 Verified
11 Do not raise rent because of occupancy Sec. 37.3(a)(11)(A) bars an increase based on an additional occupant even where the lease permits it; the Rent Board bars an increase based on a departing tenant’s replacement. Source: S6 · S3 · S11 Verified
12 Pay the standing obligations Rent Board fee of $59.00 per dwelling unit by March 1, and security-deposit interest of 4.2% for 2026-27. Source: S10 · S1 Unverified – flagged
13 If anything above the AGA is claimed, petition first A landlord who seeks any increase beyond the permitted list must petition for a Rent Board arbitration hearing before serving the notice. The tenant need not pay until the Board approves; approval is then retroactive to the effective date in the valid notice. Source: S6 · S13 Verified

The consequence of getting this wrong

Rent Ordinance Sec. 37.3(b)(5): ‘Nonconforming Rent Increases. Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void.’ Rules and Regulations Sec. 4.10(b) preserves the increase only where the amount requested is no more than the allowable annual and banked increase, and allows an Administrative Law Judge to readjust rather than void a good-faith overage within half of one percent of the prior base rent. Source: S6 · S5

Grounds that will fail

Grounds that are NOT legitimate
Claim Why it fails Source
“The market supports more” while an original occupant remains Vacancy decontrol reaches only a genuinely new tenancy. Sec. 1954.53(d)(3) expressly excludes partial changes in occupancy where an occupant under the agreement with the owner remains in lawful possession. Source: S25 · S12 · S11
“A new occupant moved in” Sec. 37.3(a)(11)(A) prohibits it ‘notwithstanding a rental agreement or lease that specifically permits’ it. Such a charge is an unlawful rent increase; any agreement to pay it is void and unenforceable and refundable on petition. Source: S6 · S3 · S11
“A roommate was replaced” ‘The landlord may not increase the rent for the unit based on the replacement of a departing tenant or the addition of new occupants, even if agreed to by the tenant.’ Source: S11
“Costs went up” without a certified petition Only the enumerated grounds apply; anything above them requires a Sec. 37.8 arbitration petition served before the notice. An O&M increase must be certified by the Board and cannot exceed 7%. Source: S6 · S13
“The state allows 8.8%” AB 1482 exempts housing under a local ordinance with a lower cap. San Francisco’s 1.6% is lower, so the state cap does not apply and cannot be used to justify a higher local increase. Source: S24
“Property taxes went up” General obligation bond and water revenue bond increases follow the property tax rate change and require the Board’s worksheet; there is no general tax passthrough. Source: S6 · S13 · S5
“The lease says we can” The Ordinance overrides the lease. Sec. 37.3(a)(11)(A) applies notwithstanding a contrary lease term, and a rental agreement cannot waive or circumvent the tenant’s rights. Source: S6 · S11
“The pricing software recommends it” Sec. 37.10C prohibits the sale or use of algorithmic devices to set rents or manage occupancy levels for residential units in San Francisco. Source: S6
“The tenant refused to renew” or any retaliatory motive Retaliation for exercising rights, requesting an accommodation or filing a complaint is expressly unlawful under California law, and the Rent Board expressly does not adjudicate it — it goes to the Civil Rights Department or court. Source: S33 · S14 · S34

Where a notice goes wrong most often

  1. No license on the effective date. Null and void in its entirety. Source: S8 · S9
  2. Serving on the wrong anniversary date. Permanently forfeits any unmatured skipped rate and moves the anniversary. Source: S3
  3. Using the rate in effect on the service date instead of the effective date. Source: S3
  4. Rounding up to the next dollar. Source: S3 · S17
  5. Applying the percentage to a rent that includes a passthrough. Source: S3 · S16
  6. Compounding banked percentages instead of adding them. Source: S5
  7. Omitting the banked-portion disclosure, or omitting the dollar amount, percentage or effective date altogether. Source: S6 · S5
  8. Serving 30 days when the total exceeds 10%. Source: S23
  9. Tying the increase to an occupant change. Source: S6 · S11
  10. Using an algorithmic pricing tool. Source: S6
