Rules & caps — local, state and federal

What actually limits these two units, layer by layer, and the complete official rate table back to 1982.

Local — San Francisco Rent Ordinance

RuleWhat it providesOfficial source
CoverageMany residential units built on or before 13 June 1979 have both rent control and eviction protection. Exclusions include most single-family homes and condominiums, units built after that date, substantially rehabilitated units and units regulated by another agency.sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws
Annual allowance1.6% for 1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027; the Rent Board recalculates it each year on 1 March, based on 60% of the rise in Bay Area CPI.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
FrequencyOnce every 12 months; the first increase may be imposed 12 months after the lease began; the effective date then becomes the tenant's anniversary date.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Base for the calculationBase rent only, including housing services such as parking or storage; excluding temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges. No rounding up.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
BankingSkipped increases may be banked subject to the Rent Board's conditions, added together (never compounded), unlimited back to 1 April 1982, non-expiring during the tenancy, transferable from a prior owner, never transferable to a new tenant.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years
Licence preconditionA rent-increase licence must be held on the effective date of any annual or banked increase (1 March 2023 onwards for 1–9 unit buildings and condominiums). An increase taking effect while unlicensed may be null and void (even across multiple years) and may entitle the tenant to a refund.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal)
Rent Board Fee (separate)Annual per-unit fee — $59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026–27, due 1 March (5% monthly late penalty up to 15%; 50% = $29.50 recoverable from tenants in occupancy as of 1 November; 2027 exemption deadline 14 December 2026). Separate from the free Housing Inventory report.sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee · sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Notice30 days' written notice; 90 days if the increase is more than 10%; +5 days if mailed; the notice must state the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and which portion reflects the annual increase and/or any banked amount (§ 37.3(b)(1)); nonconforming increases are null and void (§ 37.3(b)(5)).sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Banked rent increases · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal)
Additional occupants & partial turnoverUnder § 37.3(a)(11)(A), a landlord cannot charge more rent solely because an additional occupant joined an existing tenancy, notwithstanding any lease clause. Under § 37.3(d)(2)(B) and Rule § 6.14, unlimited market resets do not apply to partial occupancy changes while any original occupant remains in lawful possession (and Costa-Hawkins never applies to a co-tenant with a landlord agreement).SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal) · sf.gov — Rent increases under Section 6.14 and Costa-Hawkins · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Ordinance subsections (§ 37.3)Codified at Administrative Code § 37.3: § 37.3(a)(1) annual CPI formula (60% of CPI, 7% formula ceiling, § 37.15 licence); § 37.3(a)(2) banking since 1 Apr 1982; § 37.3(a)(8) arbitration petition above annual/banked; § 37.3(a)(10) voucher payment-standard rules; § 37.3(a)(11) additional occupants; § 37.3(b)(1)&(5) written breakdown & null-and-void rule; § 37.3(d) Costa-Hawkins & partial occupancy changes.SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal)
Beyond the annual amountThere are different petition and worksheet rules: capital improvement, operating and maintenance, and special-circumstances increases require petitions before notice. Bond passthroughs require a worksheet but no petition; certain utility worksheets must be filed before notice.sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
Pending (not law)File #261020 would cap combined banked + capital-improvement increases at 10% per year for three years; status Pending Committee Action. The Rent Board's 9 Nov 2026 notice says it would apply to banked increases noticed on or after 15 Sep 2026 if enacted.sfgov.legistar.com — File #261020, cap on rent increases for rent-controlled units · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco

State — California

RuleWhat it providesOfficial source
Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482) cap5% + change in cost of living, capped at 10% per 12 months; for the San Francisco area the published maximum is 8.8% for increases starting 1 Aug 2026 – 31 Jul 2027.Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap · California Attorney General — Limits on rent increases
Does AB 1482 govern these units?No. The statute does not apply to housing subject to a local rent control ordinance that restricts annual increases to less than the state amount. San Francisco's 1.6% is less than 8.8%, so the local cap controls.Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap
New tenancyFor a new tenancy in which no prior tenant remains in possession, the owner may set the initial rental rate; the cap applies to later increases.Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap · Cal. Civ. Code § 1954.53 — Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act
Costa-HawkinsAn owner may establish the initial rental rate for a dwelling, subject to listed exceptions (including subsidised tenancies and terminations aimed at evading rent control).Cal. Civ. Code § 1954.53 — Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act
Notice to change terms30 days if the increase is 10% or less of the rent charged at any time in the prior 12 months (alone or combined); 90 days if more; delivery in person or by mail under CCP § 1013.Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
Service by mailAny statutory notice period is extended by 5 calendar days when both mailing and address are in California; 10 days if either is outside California.Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension
RetaliationRetaliation after a qualifying habitability complaint is prohibited subject to statutory conditions; § 1942.5(a) addresses a 180-day period. The separate tenant-rights and organising protection in § 1942.5(d) is not limited to that window.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited

Federal

RuleWhat it providesOfficial source
Federal fair housingThe Fair Housing Act prohibits discriminatory rental terms. Federal subsidy or program rules may impose additional restrictions; subsidy status is unverified.42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Fair Housing Act (govinfo, U.S. Code 2023)
Fair Housing ActUnlawful to discriminate in the terms, conditions or privileges of a rental because of race, colour, religion, sex, familial status, national origin or disability — which covers how a rent increase is applied.42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Fair Housing Act (govinfo, U.S. Code 2023)
State fair housing overlayFEHA is broader: it adds ancestry, marital status, source of income (including a Section 8 voucher), gender identity and expression, military status, genetic information and age, and prohibits harassment.Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination · California Civil Rights Department — Housing discrimination
Where complaints goThe Rent Board does not adjudicate harassment, discrimination or retaliation; those go to the Civil Rights Department or HUD / the courts.sf.gov — Tenant petitions · California Civil Rights Department — Housing discrimination
