How big corporations and individual owners handle increases

The legal ceiling is identical for both. What differs is the machinery around it: entity form changes which exemptions are available, portfolio scale changes which reporting deadlines applied and when, and corporate pricing practice is now expressly unlawful in San Francisco. Everything below is sourced from SEC filings by a large institutional multifamily owner with a San Francisco Bay Area portfolio, or from sf.gov and California statute.

The single most important difference: algorithmic rent-setting is banned in San Francisco

Rent Ordinance Sec. 37.10C, ‘Use and Sale of Algorithmic Devices Prohibited’ (Ordinance 224-24, effective October 14, 2024) prohibits the sale or use of algorithmic devices to set rents or to manage occupancy levels for residential units. The commercial revenue-management software that is standard practice in institutional multifamily is therefore unlawful to use in San Francisco, and it is unlawful for an individual owner too. Any increase on these units must be justified by the published Rent Board rate and the tenant’s own tenancy history — not by a pricing model. Source: S6

Practical read-across for these two units

What institutional practice does and does not justify

Institutional owners do not get a different percentage, and an individual owner does not get a discount. What the corporate record shows is a discipline, not a higher ceiling: a single written policy, applied on the same cycle to every unit, documented per unit, and reviewed against the local cap before service. That discipline is exactly what a tenant lawyer probes for, and it is free to adopt.

Two corporate habits must NOT be imported. First, portfolio-level growth targets — a target is not a lawful ground, and the only lawful grounds are the closed list in Notice procedure & reasons. Second, algorithmic or model-driven pricing, which Sec. 37.10C prohibits outright in San Francisco. Source: S6 · S13
