Reasonable & normal increases

What “reasonable” and “normal” can and cannot be measured against, using only official sources — including the parts where no official answer exists.

The honest headline

No official source defines a “reasonable” or “normal” rent increase for a rent-controlled San Francisco unit. There is a lawful maximum, which is the Rent Board percentage plus any properly banked amounts. There is no official benchmark of what owners actually charge, no ZIP-code or neighbourhood rate, and no published citywide statistic comparing corporate with individual owners. Other authorized increases or passthroughs have their own petition and worksheet rules; some do not require an approval petition.

QuestionVerified answerSource
Is there an official definition of a “reasonable” increase?No. The Rent Board publishes a maximum allowable percentage and the rules for using it; it does not publish a reasonableness standard for annual increases.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal)
Is there an official “normal” or average increase?No published figure. Left blank rather than estimated (FR-08).sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Is there a different rate for a particular ZIP code?No. The allowable percentage is set once per year and applies citywide; there is no ZIP-level or neighbourhood variant.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
What is the practical benchmark then?The lawful ceiling itself: 1.6% for dates through 28 February 2027, plus banked amounts. It is a ceiling, not proof an increase is typical, fair, or lawful in a particular case; notice, licence, retaliation and other rules also apply.sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF) · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
