The evidence-based answer to ‘what is normal’

A normal annual increase in San Francisco is the published allowable increase and nothing more: 1.6% right now, against a long-run average of 2.36% since 1982 and 1.96% since 2019. A reasonable increase is one that (i) does not exceed the published rate plus properly matured banked amounts, (ii) is calculated on base rent only, (iii) is served with the correct notice period and the mandatory content, (iv) is supported by a current rent increase license, and (v) is not motivated by a protected characteristic, by retaliation, or by a wish to displace. California courts have held that rent regulation must still allow an owner a fair rate of return, which is the constitutional floor on the other side of the ledger. Source: S1 · S2 · S3 · S32
