How increases are actually handled: corporations vs individual owners

Only what official sources say. Where no official evidence exists, the row is left blank and flagged.

Large / corporate owners

PracticeOfficial evidenceSource
Setting rent at turnoverThe Rent Board states there is “no limit on how much rent a landlord may first charge a tenant when renting an empty unit that is covered by rent control”; state law likewise lets an owner set the initial rate for a new tenancy where no prior tenant remains.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap · Cal. Civ. Code § 1954.53 — Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act
Renewal pricing with shared data / algorithmsSan Francisco prohibits the sale or use of “algorithmic devices” to set rents or manage occupancy for residential units (Rent Ordinance § 37.10C, effective 14 October 2024); a tenant or the City Attorney may sue.sf.gov — New law prohibits algorithmic devices used to set rents in San Francisco
Federal antitrust exposureThe U.S. Department of Justice sued six of the largest landlords (operating more than 1.3 million units) over sharing competitively sensitive data and using common pricing algorithms, and filed a proposed consent decree with one of them.U.S. DOJ — suit against six large landlords over algorithmic pricing (7 Jan 2025)
Whether corporate owners take the annual allowance or rely on turnoverBlank — no official dataset reports the frequency or size of increases by owner type. Flagged (FR-09).sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Professional management of the licensing stepHousing Inventory reports require an owner or property-manager business contact, and changes must be updated within 30 days — the mechanism larger operators use to keep licences current.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory

Practical takeaway: the well-documented corporate advantage is turnover — resetting to market between tenancies — plus compliance infrastructure. It is not an ability to raise an existing tenant's rent above the ordinance.

Individual owners

PracticeOfficial evidenceSource
Take the annual allowance, once every 12 months“A landlord can only increase a tenant's rent by a certain percentage once every 12 months”; no petition is required for the annual amount.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
Bank skipped yearsSkipped increases may be saved and added later, subject to the banking conditions; no Rent Board approval, but the calculation must be right.sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Report and licenceOne free Housing Inventory report per unit per year; the licence follows automatically; no separate application.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · Rent Board Portal (Housing Inventory / licence look-up)
Free official helpRent Board counsellors answer by phone on weekdays; the arbitration process for a tenant petition is designed so that no one needs an attorney.sf.gov — Rent Board counselling line 415-252-4600 · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history
Outside help for larger increasesPetition-based increases (capital improvement, operating and maintenance, comparable rents) require forms and supporting documentation; the Rent Board advises talking to a counsellor before filing a comparable-rent petition.sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs · sf.gov — Special circumstances petitions based on rents for comparable units
Whether individual owners raise rent less often than corporationsBlank — no official data. Flagged (FR-09).sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco

Where the two converge

Both corporate managers and individual owners work from the same ceiling: the annual percentage, plus properly banked amounts, plus any petition-based passthrough. The tools differ — larger owners automate renewals and lean on turnover, individual owners rely on the annual cycle and banking — but the legal test applied to a served notice is identical, and a tenant may challenge any of them with the same free petition.
