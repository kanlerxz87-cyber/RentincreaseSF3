One unit or both units?

The discrimination question, answered from the statutes and the enforcement routes — and separated from what the Rent Board does and does not decide.

Answer

There is no legal requirement to raise rent on both units, and no official guidance says a selective increase is unlawful in itself. What the law prohibits is treating a tenant differently because of a protected characteristic, or increasing rent as retaliation. Because the two units have different base rents, different lease dates and different rent histories, their lawful maximums differ anyway — so the same percentage or the same dollar increase is not automatically required. The control that matters is a written, dated, objective rationale per unit, applied consistently.

Documentation protocol (recommended, not a legal requirement)

  1. Write one page per unit. Lease start, base rent, every increase or skip, the licence status on the planned effective date, and the arithmetic.
  2. Use one method for both. Same notice template, same source (Form 571), same calculation sheet, whatever the amount.
  3. Record the objective reason for the date. “Anniversary falls on 1 February 2027” and “six skipped annual increases 2020–2025” are objective; “the tenant is difficult” is not.
  4. Check the 180-day window. Search the maintenance log and any complaints before setting an effective date.
  5. Keep the licence evidence. Screenshot of the Portal showing the licence current on the effective date.
  6. If either tenant asks why the other's increase differs, answer with the calculation sheet — base rent and skipped years, not the tenant.
