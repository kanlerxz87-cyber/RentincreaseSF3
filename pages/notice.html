Notice, service & reasons

Exactly what the notice must say, how it must be delivered, and which reasons are lawful — with model wording you can adapt.

Required contents

ElementRequirementOfficial source
Dollar amount of the increaseState the amount in dollars.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
PercentageState the percentage; for banked amounts the percentages are added, not compounded.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Effective dateState the date the increase takes effect; it becomes the new anniversary date.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Banked breakdownIf any part is banked, state in writing the annual and banked portions and the dates underlying the banked amount. Rule § 4.12(b) specifies that omission of the banked dates does not itself make the increase null and void.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal) · sf.gov — Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Part IV §§ 4.10 and 4.12
Correct rate versionUse the percentage in effect on the effective date, not the date the notice is served.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
LicenceThe licence must exist, but it does not have to be attached to the notice.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Signature / identificationBlank — not specified in the Rent Board's published notice guidance. Flagged (FR-11).sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco

Delivery and timing

MethodLead timeOfficial source
Personal delivery, increase 10% or lessAt least 30 days before the effective date.Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
Personal delivery, increase more than 10%At least 90 days before the effective date (alone or combined with other increases in the prior 12 months).Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
Mail within CaliforniaAdd 5 calendar days to the notice period, i.e. 35 or 95 days.Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension · Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
Mail outside CaliforniaAdd 10 calendar days.Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension
Express / overnightAdd 2 court days (statutory overnight-delivery extension).Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension
Proof of serviceKeep the delivery receipt or the certificate/notation of mailing; the statute requires the mailed copy to record the date and place of mailing or carry an affidavit/certificate.Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension

Model notice (adapt; verify amounts before serving)

NOTICE OF RENT INCREASE
Date: [date served]
To: [Tenant name(s)], [unit]
From: [Owner name and contact for habitability issues]

Under San Francisco Administrative Code § 37.3 and California Civil Code § 827, your monthly base rent will increase as follows.

• Current base rent: $[amount]
• Amount of increase: $[amount]
• Percentage of increase: [x]% consisting of [y]% annual allowable increase (allowable rate for the period covering the effective date) and [z]% banked increase based on [list each applicable anniversary date and rate]
• New base rent: $[amount]
• Effective date: [date]

The increase is 10% or less / more than 10% of the rent charged during the 12 months before the effective date, so [30 / 90] days' written notice is given. [If served by mail: an additional 5 days is provided under Code of Civil Procedure § 1013.]

The unit's rent is controlled by the San Francisco Rent Ordinance. Signed: [Owner]

This wording follows the elements the Rent Board lists for an annual or banked increase. It is a drafting aid, not legal advice: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Banked rent increases · Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal) · sf.gov — Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Part IV §§ 4.10 and 4.12

Before the notice — five things that can make a lawful percentage unenforceable

Pre-conditionWhat to checkOfficial source
Rent-increase licenceA current licence must be on file for the unit on the effective date; without it the notice “will not be enforceable”, and the tenant may get a refund.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory
Unabated habitability violationsCalifornia bars a rent-increase notice where the dwelling lacks the standards of Civil Code § 1941.1 or has been declared substandard, and a public officer has notified the owner in writing, and the condition has stood unabated for more than 35 days without good cause, and the tenant did not cause it. Remedy: actual damages plus $100–$5,000, and attorney's fees to the prevailing party.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.4 — no rent-increase notice while substandard conditions are unabated
RetaliationAn increase motivated by a tenant's complaint, repair request or organising is prohibited, with statutory conditions and timing rules; the protection is not limited to a 180-day window.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited
Protected characteristicsThe amount must not vary because of a protected characteristic; different treatment of the two units is defensible only on documented, unit-specific facts.Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination · 42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Fair Housing Act (govinfo, U.S. Code 2023)
Extra rent for an added occupantCharging more simply because a roommate moved in is an unlawful rent increase even if the lease allows it. Unit A added an occupant on 1 April 2026 — do not price that in.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco

This is a checklist of official rules, not a clearance: nothing on this site can certify that a particular notice is unchallengeable.

Reasons — what is required, permitted and prohibited

SituationPositionOfficial source
Do I have to give a reason for an annual increase?No. The annual and banked increases may be imposed without a petition and without any stated justification; the notice elements are amount, percentage, date and banked part.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
Reasons that are lawfulThe lawful allowance published by the Rent Board; recovery of previously skipped (banked) annual increases; and, separately by petition, documented capital improvements, operating and maintenance costs, or comparable-rent circumstances.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
Reasons that are unlawfulRetaliation for a repair request, habitability complaint or tenant organising (with distinct statutory conditions and time rules); any difference based on a protected characteristic; charging more because an additional occupant moved in.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited · Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
“Market rent” as a reasonNot a basis for an annual increase. The Rent Board states a long-standing tenant paying less than market is not, by itself, an extraordinary circumstance justifying a comparable-rent increase.sf.gov — Special circumstances petitions based on rents for comparable units
“My costs went up” as a reasonOnly through an operating-and-maintenance petition with documentation — not as an add-on to the annual notice.sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
“I want both units to be the same”Not a lawful basis in itself; equalising rents is achieved only through each unit's own lawful allowance and banking.SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal) · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
