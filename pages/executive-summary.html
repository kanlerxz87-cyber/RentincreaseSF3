Forefront finding — the Rent Board notification and licence question

Annual and banked increases do not require a Rent Board approval petition. Any separate notice-copy filing requirement remains unverified (FR-20). You do have to report the units into the Housing Inventory and hold a current rent-increase licence on the effective date — and that obligation applies to you as an individual owner exactly as it would to a corporation.

State or local? Which level of government asks for each step

Direct answer to the question you asked: the requirement to inform the Rent Board and to hold a rent-increase licence before you notify a tenant of an increase is a local San Francisco law, not a state law. It is Ordinance No. 265-20, adopted by the Board of Supervisors in 2020 and effective 18 January 2021, which amended the City's Rent Ordinance (San Francisco Administrative Code, Chapter 37) to create the Housing Inventory and the licence that comes out of it. The 2023 date you read about is the date that duty reached your kind of building: the City's own page says reporting “began March 1, 2023 for condominiums and units in buildings with fewer than 10 units” (10-or-more-unit buildings started 1 July 2022). California law imposes no equivalent filing or licence.

Why the confusion is understandable: the City's page uses the phrase “a new law”, and the licence sits inside a City ordinance, so it reads like a state statute. The operative text is in the San Francisco Administrative Code, and the enforcement body is the Rent Board — both municipal. What California did in 2023 was different: the current text of Civil Code § 1947.12 was “repealed … and added by Stats. 2023, Ch. 290, Sec. 4. (SB 567)”, effective 1 January 2024, operative 1 April 2024, and it “shall remain in effect until January 1, 2030”. That statute caps the amount and cross-references § 827 for notice; it creates no filing with, or licence from, any rent board. The state's own text says it is “not intended to expand or limit the authority of local governments to establish local policies regulating rents” (§ 1947.12(m)(2)).

5. What to do next

  1. Confirm the licence. Log into the Rent Board Portal, check the Housing Inventory entry for both units, and confirm a licence is current for the planned effective date. Source: sf.gov — Housing Inventory · portal.sfrb.org
  2. Confirm Unit B's rent history. Leases, notices and rent ledger 2019–2026. Without it the 13.1% figure stays conditional. Source: sf.gov — banked increases
  3. Consider the options. The annual-only 1.6% is a smaller illustrative option for each unit through 28 February 2027. Any banked amount, including Unit A at 3.0% or Unit B at 10.0% or 13.1%, needs a verified ledger, correct anniversary, current licence and review of pending legislation; no amount is guaranteed to pass review. Source: sf.gov — rent increases · File #261020
  4. Serve notice in time. 30 days for ≤10%, 90 days for >10%, +5 if mailed. Worked dates: Licence & process. Source: Civ. Code §827 · CCP §1013
  5. Keep the file. Notice, proof of service, licence screenshot, calculation sheet, and the reason each unit's amount differs.
