Forefront: Rent Board notification & license — individual owners included

Short answer: Yes. You are an individual owner, not a corporation, and you still must report to the Rent Board Housing Inventory and hold a current rent-increase license before any annual or banked increase takes effect. The ordinance makes no distinction between an individual and a corporation — it applies to all owners of residential property in San Francisco. The only distinction is building size: 10+ units (reporting since July 1, 2022) vs. 1–9 units and condos (since March 1, 2023). Your 2-unit building falls in the second group.

Do this order: file → confirm license → serve notice. Noticing first and filing after invalidates the increase.

Recommendation: Adopt one written, property-wide policy — e.g., \"each covered unit is increased annually by the SF Rent Board allowable percentage plus documented banked increases, applied on its anniversary date\" — and apply it to both units. Document each unit's rent history before service. If you raise only one, document the objective reason in writing and keep it with the property file.

Bottom line & action checklist

Yes — a small, lawful increase is available for both units, and it is normal. The SF allowable annual increase (1.4%/1.6%) is the measure of \"reasonable\" for rent-controlled units. Applying it plus documented banking, on each unit's anniversary, with a current license and compliant written notice, is exactly what careful owners — corporate and individual — do.
