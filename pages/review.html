The tenant-lawyer stress test

Sixteen checks a tenant-side reviewer would run against a served notice. This checklist is not a legal opinion or a guarantee: a tenant can still challenge an increase, and additional facts and rules may matter.

Why this test is the right standard

A San Francisco tenant can challenge any increase by filing a Tenant Petition (Form 516C, box C) asking the Rent Board to find the increase unlawful or to determine the lawful rent. Filing is free, there is no filing deadline, and the matter can proceed to an arbitration hearing before a Rent Board Administrative Law Judge; the process is designed so that no one needs an attorney. Separately, discrimination and retaliation claims go to the Civil Rights Department, HUD or court rather than the Rent Board.

Sources: sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history · sf.gov — Tenant petitions · California Civil Rights Department — Housing discrimination

The sixteen checks

#What the reviewer asksHow this file answers itRule
1Is the unit covered by the Rent Ordinance?The 1940 construction year and rent-control status are owner-reported. Building unit count, unit type, and exemptions require confirmation.sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws
2Is the increase within the annual allowable percentage?1.6% for an effective date through 28 February 2027; the published figure, not an older year's.sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
3Is the percentage the one in effect on the effective date?Notice served in Dec/Jan must still quote 1.6%, because the increase takes effect before 1 March 2027.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
4Was the calculation done on base rent only?Exclude passthroughs and fluctuating charges; include parking/storage if part of base rent.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
5Was anything rounded up?No rounding to the nearest dollar.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
6Is the banked amount properly established?Each skipped year identified with its percentage; added, not compounded; the banking conditions met.sf.gov — Banked rent increases
7Has at least 12 months passed since the last increase or the lease start?Unit A: 1 Feb 2027. Unit B: any date after 1 June 2020, on the reported history.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
8Would the increase create a new anniversary date, and is that acceptable?Any effective date becomes the new anniversary date; a later increase may be lost if the date shifts.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
9Was a rent-increase licence in force on the effective date?Check the Rent Board Portal and keep a screenshot with the date.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
10Was the Housing Inventory report filed for the correct year?Deadline 1 March each year; late filing is allowed but must precede the effective date.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
11Is the notice period correct (30 or 90 days)?10% or less ⇒ 30 days; more than 10% ⇒ 90 days; +5 if mailed within California.Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
12Was service documented?Personal delivery receipt, or a mailed copy with the date and place of mailing / certificate of mailing.Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension
13Does the notice state amount, percentage, date and banked part?All four elements present.sf.gov — Banked rent increases
14Is there any retaliation issue?Review complaints, repairs and tenant activity for potential retaliation; the statutory 180-day provision has specific conditions.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited
15Is there any protected-characteristic issue?The amount must not vary because of race, colour, religion, sex, familial status, national origin, disability, source of income or the other FEHA grounds.Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination
16Is the differential between the two units explainable from the file?Different base rents and different lease dates explain the different dollar amounts; the method is identical.42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Fair Housing Act (govinfo, U.S. Code 2023)

What would actually get an increase overturned

Failure modeConsequenceSource
No licence on the effective dateThe notice is not enforceable, may be null and void (potentially across multiple years), and exposes the owner to a Tenant Summary Petition (Form 518) before the effective date or an Unlawful Rent Increase Petition / refund claim (Form 516C) after.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal)
Percentage above the allowance (or banked amount wrongly computed)Unlawful rent increase; the lawful rent can be determined and overpayments addressed in the petition process.sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history
Too little noticeThe increase cannot take effect on the stated date; the notice is defective for that date.Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
Increase within 180 days of a repair request or complaintPotential retaliation; assess the statutory conditions and circumstances, including the 180-day provision.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited
