Open questions — what must be answered before a decision is defensible

The brief asked for a clear list of the questions, the information they need and the answers available now. This page is that list. 14 questions block a decision; 14 are answered from official sources; 10 documents have to be collected; 8 actions will find the missing information; 4 questions have no official answer and stay blank.

Status key. Owner record means no public page can answer it — it comes out of your files. Open item means the official sources do not settle it and it is published on Flagged for review. Verified means the answer below was read off the linked official page on the research date.

A. The blocking questions

Serve nothing until every row here is either answered or deliberately accepted as an open risk. Each row states the consequence of leaving it blank.

Q-01Has the Housing Inventory been reported for both units, and is a rent-increase licence in effect on the planned effective date?Without a licence on the effective date the increase is not enforceable however correct the percentage is; the notice may be null and void and the tenant may be owed a refund.Owner record — not verifiable from any public pageRent Board Portal: search the address or Assessor's Parcel Number and open each licence year; keep a dated screenshot for both units.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · Rent Board Portal (Housing Inventory / licence look-up)
Q-02Was Unit B's rent increased at any time since 1 June 2019?It decides whether the lawful ceiling is 1.6% or a banked total above 10%, which also flips the notice period from 30 to 90 days.Owner record — published as an open flag (FR-01)Leases, every rent-increase notice, the rent ledger and cancelled cheques 2019–2026; the Rent Board can also determine the lawful rent on petition.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history
Q-03Is each tenancy month-to-month, or still inside a fixed-term lease on the intended effective date?The 30/90-day change-of-terms notice in Civil Code § 827 is the mechanism for a periodic tenancy; inside a fixed term the rent can only change as the lease itself allows.Owner record — lease document requiredThe current signed lease or rental agreement, including any renewal or addendum.Cal. Q-04What exactly is the base rent for each unit, and does it include housing services such as parking, storage or utilities?The percentage is applied to base rent only; passthroughs and fluctuating charges are excluded, and the increase may not be rounded up to the next dollar.Owner record — one figure requiredThe rent stated in the current agreement less any separately identified charges.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Q-05Does the building's record show residential units that pre-date 13 June 1979, and is the unit a single-family dwelling?Coverage is the premise of every other figure on this site. A 1940 building is generally covered, but the owner's statement has not been checked against a City record.City record — not yet retrievedThe City's own instruction is to look up the building on the Property Information Map linked from the rental-laws page; the assessor record also gives the unit count.sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws
Q-06Are the units separately alienable (condominium or co-op) and is the owner an individual rather than a corporation, REIT, LLC or partnership?That combination can exempt a unit from the state rent cap — but the exemption is conditional on giving the tenant the statute's written notice — and separately alienable units are also outside the City ordinance only in defined circumstances.City record plus title documentThe deed or condominium map, the current lease (to check whether the statutory exemption sentence was ever given), plus a Rent Board counsellor or a determination on petition if the answer is unclear.Cal. Q-07What is each unit's anniversary date — the date the last increase actually took effect?It fixes the earliest lawful effective date and which year's percentage applies; getting it wrong loses a banked increase or voids the notice.Owner record, then counsellor confirmation (FR-02)Date of the last effective increase; for Unit A the choice between 1 February and 1 April 2027 should be confirmed in writing.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Q-08Has either household complained, requested repairs, asserted a habitability problem, or organised in the last year?An increase following protected conduct invites a retaliation claim; the Rent Board does not hear retaliation, so the exposure lands in court.Owner record — communications file requiredEvery written request, work order and reply since the last increase, with dates.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited · sf.gov — Tenant petitions
Q-09In Unit A, is the occupant who joined in 2026 a co-tenant or a subtenant, and was a written Section 6.14 notice served within about 60 days of the change?It decides whether any future market-rate increase is available at all when an original occupant leaves, and it is also why no extra rent could be charged for the new occupant.Owner record plus legal analysis (FR-21)The 2026 agreement, rent-acceptance conduct, and any Section 6.14 notice; a Section 6.14 determination may be requested from the Rent Board.sf.gov — Rent increases under Section 6.14 and Costa-Hawkins · sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
Q-10Does either household hold a Housing Choice Voucher or another rent subsidy?A subsidy adds a rent-reasonableness determination by the housing authority, different treatment of increases over 10%, and a Rent Board Fee exemption route.Owner record — lease file and housing authorityThe voucher tenancy addendum and the housing authority's most recent rent determination.24 CFR § 982.507 — Rent to owner: reasonable rent (Housing Choice Voucher) · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal) · sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee
Q-11Is the annual Rent Board Fee paid and current for both units, and were owner contact details kept updated?A separate duty from the licence: $59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026–27 due 1 March, with 5% monthly penalties capped at 15%; contact changes must be updated within 30 days.Owner record — portal accountPayment confirmations in the Rent Board Portal and the current owner/manager contact entry.sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee · sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Q-12Are you also planning a passthrough (capital improvement, utility, or operating and maintenance expense)?Those six categories require a petition filed with the Rent Board *before* the notice is served, and the tenant does not have to pay until it is approved — a different track from the annual increase.Owner decision, then petitionRent Board landlord petitions; filing is free.sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
Q-13Does the draft notice state the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and which part is banked — and does it use the percentage in force on the effective date?The Rent Board lists those as the contents of a compliant notice; the breakdown of annual versus banked amounts and the dates behind them are set by rule.Drafting step — checkable nowCompare the draft against the published notice contents before serving.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Q-14Has File #261020 moved since 13 November 2026, and would it apply to a notice served now?If enacted as proposed it would cap combined banked and capital-improvement increases at 10% a year for three years, reaching banked notices served on or after 15 September 2026.Legislative status — re-check before servingThe Bill file page and the notice the Rent Board posts on its rent-increase pages.sfgov.legistar.com — File #261020, cap on rent increases for rent-controlled units · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco

B. What research has already settled

QuestionAnswer as it stands ConfidenceOfficial source
Is the duty to inform the Rent Board and hold a licence a state law or a local law?Local. It is a San Francisco ordinance — Ordinance No. 265-20, effective 18 January 2021, which added the Housing Inventory and the rent-increase licence to the City's Rent Ordinance. No California statute requires a landlord to notify a rent board or hold a licence to raise rent.Verifiedsf.gov — Ordinance 265-20: Housing Inventory and rent-increase licence · sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Then why does everyone quote 2023?Because that is when it reached small owners: reporting began 1 July 2022 for units in buildings of 10 or more units, and 1 March 2023 for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units, with updates due every 1 March.Verifiedsf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Do I need the licence because I am an individual owner rather than a corporation?The rule is written by building size, not by the owner's legal form: all owners of residential property must report, and owners with tenants receive a licence after the submission. The state statute, by contrast, does care about legal form for separately alienable units.Verifiedsf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap
Is there a licence application, a fee, or an approval process?No separate application for the licence — it is generated automatically once the report is filed, reporting is free, and the licence does not have to be attached to the tenant's notice. Annual and banked increases need no petition.Verifiedsf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
Can I be told I must file the increase notice with the Rent Board?No official page in the register requires a copy of an ordinary annual or banked increase notice to be filed; the open question is published rather than answered (FR-20). Petition-based increases are a different matter.Open itemsf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
What is the maximum increase available right now?1.6% for an increase taking effect between 1 March 2026 and 28 February 2027, plus any properly banked amounts. The 2027–28 percentage has not been announced.Verifiedsf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
Does state law add anything on top, or require any filing?No filing and no licence — the state has no rent board. The state cap is 5% plus regional cost-of-living up to 10% (8.8% for the San Francisco area for increases starting 1 August 2026 – 31 July 2027) and the statute expressly does not apply where a local ordinance allows a smaller increase. What the statute does add is a tenant-facing duty for owners who claim an exemption: a separately alienable unit owned by a non-corporate owner is exempt only if the tenant was given the statute's written exemption notice, and a two-unit structure the owner has occupied as a principal residence since the start of the tenancy is exempt outright.VerifiedCalifornia Attorney General — Limits on rent increases · Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap
How much notice, and how is it served?30 days where the increase (alone or with another in the prior 12 months) is 10% or less; 90 days where it exceeds 10%; five extra days when served by mail in California.VerifiedCal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy · Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension
What happens if the tenant challenges it?A tenant may file an unlawful-rent-increase petition (Form 516C with Form C rent history), or a Tenant Summary Petition before the increase takes effect, and there is no time limit for filing the rent-history review. Hearings are before an Administrative Law Judge and no lawyer is required.Verifiedsf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history · sf.gov — Tenant petitions
Must I raise both units, or neither?No official source requires uniform increases. Fair-housing law prohibits different treatment because of a protected characteristic, and the Rent Board does not adjudicate discrimination at all — those claims go to the California Civil Rights Department or HUD.VerifiedCal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination · 42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Fair Housing Act (govinfo, U.S. Code 2023) · sf.gov — Tenant petitions
Can I charge more because a new occupant joined Unit A in 2026?No. Charging additional rent merely because an additional roommate moved in is an unlawful rent increase even if the lease allows it.Verifiedsf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal)
What counts as a reasonable or normal increase under local, state or federal law?None of the three publishes a 'normal' figure. The local rule is a ceiling, not a benchmark; no official body publishes an average or typical increase, and none publishes a ZIP-level rate.No official sourcesf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Can a state law stop me serving the notice at all, even at a lawful percentage?Yes — Civil Code § 1942.4. A landlord may not issue a notice of a rent increase while all four conditions hold: the dwelling falls below the standards of § 1941.1 or Health & Safety Code §§ 17920.10/17920.3, an appropriate public officer has notified the owner in writing after inspection, the condition has stood unabated more than 35 days without good cause, and the tenant did not cause it. Remedy: actual damages, $100–$5,000, and attorney's fees. Check the building's inspection record before drafting; published as FR-37's companion correction.Verified 13 Nov 2026Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.4 — no rent-increase notice while substandard conditions are unabated
Does the City expect a business registration number for a two-unit rental?Blank — unresolved. The Housing Inventory asks for “the business registration number for the unit, if any”; no official source read on 13 Nov 2026 settles the question for an individual owner of a small residential rental, so the field is left blank rather than answered by inference (FR-37). Ask the Rent Board and the Treasurer & Tax Collector and keep the answer.Opensf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory

D. How to discover the missing information

Each action is a route to a fact, not a workaround. Free counselling exists precisely for the coverage and anniversary questions.

ActionWhat it resolves Official channel
Book the free Rent Board counselling line and ask three questions in writing: coverage, anniversary date, and the banked balance for each unit.Q-02, Q-07, and the Unit A anniversary flag FR-02 — the counsellor is the only party who can read the file, not just the rules.sf.gov — Rent Board counselling line 415-252-4600
Search both units on the Rent Board Portal and export or screenshot the licence record for every year.Q-01 and Q-11; the same search is what a tenant is told to use to check you.Rent Board Portal (Housing Inventory / licence look-up)
Reconstruct each unit's rent history in a one-line-per-year table, leaving unknown years blank instead of estimated.Q-02 and Q-04; a blank row is reviewable, an estimated row is not.sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history
File the Housing Inventory report now if any year is missing, then re-check the licence status after submission.Q-01; filing after the 1 March deadline is allowed, but it cannot retroactively licence an increase that already took effect.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Ask the Rent Board, by petition, to determine the lawful rent for Unit B if the ledger cannot be reconstructed.Q-02 — a determination converts a contested banked balance into a record; filing is free and needs the complete rent history you do have.sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
Read the four texts that describe banking side by side before choosing a Unit A or Unit B number.The 24-month page text, the 12-month worked example, Rules § 4.12 and the multi-year page; the divergence is published as FR-03.sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years
Check the Bill file status again within 48 hours of serving anything above 10%.Q-14 — proposed law can change mid-process and the Rent Board posts a notice when it does.sfgov.legistar.com — File #261020, cap on rent increases for rent-controlled units
Have a California landlord-tenant lawyer review the drafted notice, the ledger and the licence record before service.Everything the site cannot verify: actual service, motive, and the tenancy documents.sf.gov — Tenant petitions

G. The do-not-serve gate

  1. Licence confirmed for each unit, current on the intended effective date, with a dated record kept. Source: sf.gov — Housing Inventory
  2. Rent history reconstructed for each unit, with unknown years left blank. Source: sf.gov — banked increases
  3. Coverage confirmed against the building record, not against the owner's recollection. Source: sf.gov — rental laws
  4. Notice drafted with the amount, percentage, effective date and banked breakdown, checked against the percentage in force on the effective date. Source: sf.gov — rent increases
  5. Legislative status re-checked within 48 hours of service if the total exceeds 10%. Source: File #261020
  6. Records filed — notice, proof of service, calculation, licence record, and the dated reason the two units differ.
