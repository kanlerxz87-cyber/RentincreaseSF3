A. Answered — the information we already have (11 questions)

IDQuestionAnswer (verified)Official source
Q-01Must an individual owner (not a corporation) inform the Rent Board and hold a licence before notifying tenants of an increase?Yes. All owners of residential property must report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory; a tenant-occupied report generates a rent-increase licence, and the licence must be in effect on the effective date of the increase. The duty turns on building size, not on the owner's legal form.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Q-02Is the “since 2023” duty to inform the Rent Board a state or a local law?Local. San Francisco Ordinance No. 265-20 (effective 18 January 2021) created the Housing Inventory and licence; reporting for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units began 1 March 2023. State law (AB 1482, Civ. Code § 1947.12) caps increases and Civ. Code § 827 sets notice periods — neither requires a Rent Board filing. No federal law imposes one.sf.gov — Ordinance 265-20: Housing Inventory and rent-increase licence
Q-03What is the current allowable annual increase?1.6% for effective dates 1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027 (60% of Bay Area CPI), multiplied against base rent only, no rounding up.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Q-04Do annual or banked increases need a Rent Board approval petition?No. “Landlords are not required to file a petition with the City to impose these types of rent increases.” Other increases (capital improvement, operating and maintenance, etc.) have their own petition procedures.sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years
Q-05How much notice must a tenant get?30 days if the increase is 10% or less (measured against the rent charged at any time in the previous 12 months, alone or combined), 90 days if more than 10%; +5 days if mailed within California.Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
Q-06How do banked increases work?Skipped annual increases may be added later: added, not compounded; no cap; allowed only since 1 April 1982; no expiry during a tenancy; the banked part and its dates belong in the notice. Two official texts disagree on the waiting test (12 vs 24 months) — both are published on the Flagged page (FR-03).sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Q-07What is a “reasonable and normal” increase here?The lawful ceiling for a rent-controlled unit is the citywide Rent Board percentage plus properly banked amounts. The Rent Board publishes one citywide figure — no ZIP-level or neighbourhood benchmark exists. State AB 1482 (8.8% in this region for 1 Aug 2026 – 31 Jul 2027) does not govern where the local cap is lower.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Q-08Should both units be increased, or only one, to avoid discrimination exposure?No official source requires uniform increases. Fair-housing law prohibits different treatment because of a protected characteristic — the control is a documented, non-protected reason per unit. Raising both on the same lawful formula is the lower-risk pattern; the Rent Board does not hear discrimination claims (Civil Rights Department or HUD do).Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination
Q-09What does the tenant side cost, and what can they file?Tenants file an unlawful-rent-increase petition (Form 516C, Box C) free of charge, with no filing deadline, before an Administrative Law Judge; a Tenant Summary Petition (Form 518) is available while an increase has not yet taken effect. The hearing is designed so no attorney is required — but tenants can and do obtain free legal help, so an increase must survive that scrutiny.sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history
Q-10Is there a separate annual fee besides the free Housing Inventory report?Yes. The Rent Board Fee is $59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026–27, due 1 March, with a 5% monthly late penalty up to 15%; 50% ($29.50) is recoverable from qualifying tenants; the 2027 exemption window closes 14 December 2026. Neither obligation satisfies the other.sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee
Q-11Has anything changed in the law that would block a large banked increase today?Not yet. File #261020 (10%/yr cap on combined banked + capital-improvement increases, if enacted as proposed applying to banked increases noticed on or after 15 September 2026) was re-checked on 13 November 2026: status Pending Committee Action, no final action — not law.sfgov.legistar.com — File #261020, cap on rent increases for rent-controlled units

Q-12Are both units actually covered by rent control?Every calculation on this site assumes coverage. A 1940 construction year is owner-reported; a government building record has not been pulled.Unverified — owner statement onlysf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws
Q-13How many units are in the building?The brief describes two rental units; the total building count is not stated. It decides which reporting start date applied (10+ units: 1 July 2022; 1–9 units: 1 March 2023) — though the licence duty itself applies either way.Unverified — FR-18sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Q-14Is the Housing Inventory filed, and is the licence current on the planned effective date?Without a licence on the effective date the increase “will not be enforceable” and “may be null and void (this may apply for multiple years)”, with refund exposure. This is the single largest legal risk in the whole process.Unverified — FR-04sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory
Q-15What is Unit B's complete rent history from June 2019 to today?The 11.5% banked / 13.1% combined figures assume no increase was ever imposed. One prior increase changes the banked total, the base rent and the timing.Unverified — FR-01sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Q-16Which anniversary applies to Unit A — 1 February or 1 April 2026?The tenancy began 1 February 2025 and a new co-tenant joined 1 April 2026 at the same rent. The official rule starts the clock 12 months after the lease began, but the Rent Board pages do not address a mid-tenancy renewal with a new occupant. Both dates are computed; the earliest effective date differs.Open — FR-02sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Q-17Is either unit subsidised (voucher / regulated rent)?Federal voucher rules and the Rent Ordinance's partial exemption can change the lawful amount and the notice.Unverified — FR-13SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal)
Q-18Do the leases run to a fixed term that has not expired?Civil Code § 827 describes periodic tenancies. Nothing here establishes that an increase may be imposed mid-term under a fixed-term lease; both leases must be read.Unverified — FR-25Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
Q-19Does a copy of the rent-increase notice have to be filed with the Rent Board?Ordinary annual/banked increases need no approval petition, and the licence need not accompany the notice — but the cited pages do not answer whether a separate notice-copy filing exists.Unverified — FR-20sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Q-20Which effective date will actually be chosen (anniversary vs later)?The date decides the governing percentage (the 1.6% runs only to 28 February 2027), the banked set, and the 30/90-day clock. The 2027–28 percentage is blank until the Rent Board announces it.Decision pendingsf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
Q-21Will File #261020 be enacted before the notice is served?If enacted as proposed it would apply to banked increases noticed on or after 15 September 2026 — which would change what a lawful large banked increase looks like. Status must be re-checked immediately before service.Pending — not lawsfgov.legistar.com — File #261020, cap on rent increases for rent-controlled units
Q-22Is the 2026–27 Rent Board Fee paid (or an exemption on file)?The fee is separate from the licence; arrears accrue 5%/month up to 15% and are referred to the Bureau of Delinquent Revenue after 1 June.Unverified — owner recordsf.gov — The Rent Board Fee
