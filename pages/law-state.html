California — state law

Three state statutes matter here. Costa-Hawkins (Civil Code Sec. 1954.50 et seq.) sets the outer limits of local rent control and governs when an owner may reprice a unit. The Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (AB 1482, Civil Code Sec. 1947.12) sets a statewide cap — but it does not apply to these units. Civil Code Sec. 827 sets the notice periods, which San Francisco’s local pages mirror.

The decisive provision for Unit A

Cal. Civ. Code Sec. 1954.53(d)(3) — a partial change in occupancy does NOT permit a market reset

Sec. 1954.53(d)(2) allows an owner to increase the rent by any amount allowed by the section to a lawful sublessee or assignee who did not reside at the unit before January 1, 1996, but only ‘if the original occupant or occupants who took possession of the dwelling or unit pursuant to the rental agreement with the owner no longer permanently reside there’.

Sec. 1954.53(d)(3) then removes that power in exactly Unit A’s situation: ‘This subdivision does not apply to partial changes in occupancy of a dwelling or unit where one or more of the occupants of the premises, pursuant to the agreement with the owner provided for above, remains an occupant in lawful possession of the dwelling or unit…’

Sec. 1954.53(b) reinforces the result: an owner’s right to set an initial rate ‘applies to, and includes, renewal of the initial hiring by the same tenant… for the entire period of their occupancy at the rental rate established for the initial hiring’.

One original tenant of Unit A remains in possession. Therefore no market-rate reset is available, and the lawful ceiling stays tied to the $3,995.00 base rent with a February 1 anniversary date. Source: S25

Just cause — context

Sec. 1946.2 and Chapter 37 Sec. 37.9

AB 1482 also created a statewide just-cause requirement at Civil Code Sec. 1946.2. For these units the operative just-cause regime is the local one: sf.gov lists the grounds under the Rent Ordinance, including non-payment, breach of the rental agreement, nuisance, owner or relative move-in, condominium conversion, capital improvement work that makes the unit unlivable, and removal of all units from the rental market under the state Ellis Act. Just cause is not directly engaged by a lawful rent increase, but it matters to the red-team analysis: an increase imposed in order to manufacture a non-payment or breach ground is exposed as harassment under Sec. 37.10B and as circumvention under Sec. 37.9F. Source: S7 · S27 · S6
