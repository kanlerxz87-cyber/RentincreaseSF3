Notice, deadlines and reasons

The notice is the only part of the process a tenant sees first. Its required contents are few, so most failures are omissions — or a reason that should never have been written down.

Reasons: required, permitted, forbidden

No reason is required for an annual or banked increase; several reasons are never acceptable
QuestionAnswerSource
Must I give a reason?No. The required contents of a notice for an annual or banked increase are the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, the banked portion, and the §37.3(b) component split. Nothing in the published guidance requires a justification for the allowance itself.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF · S.F. Admin. Code §37.3 (American Legal Publishing)
When is a justification mandatory?Only for increases that require a landlord petition — capital improvements, operating and maintenance costs, comparable rents, certain passthroughs — where the landlord must file with the Rent Board before serving notice. None of those is proposed here.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF
What is the safest thing to state?The arithmetic itself: which percentage is the current annual allowance, and which is a banked year. It is verifiable from the Rent Board's published table, which is exactly what a reviewer will check.sf.gov — Form 571, Allowable Annual Increases 26–27 (PDF) · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Never: a protected characteristicA reason or differential treatment touching race, colour, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, marital status, national origin, ancestry, familial status, source of income, disability, veteran or military status or genetic information is unlawful.Gov. Code §12955 — FEHA housing discrimination (leginfo) · 42 U.S.C. §3604 (govinfo)
Never: a repair request or complaintWithin 180 days of a good-faith repair request or tenantability complaint the rent may not be increased in the circumstances set out in the statute, and retaliation may never be the purpose.Civ. Code §1942.5 (leginfo)
Never: the new occupant"A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a unit with the existing tenant. This is an unlawful rent increase, even if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge."sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF
Never: "market rate"Market rent can be set for a new tenancy where no prior tenant remains in possession. It cannot justify an increase on a tenant in occupancy in a covered unit.Civ. Code §1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act (leginfo) · Civ. Code §1954.53 — Costa-Hawkins (leginfo)
Careful: the last original occupantA different, narrower rule allows an unlimited increase for certain remaining occupants once the last original occupant permanently vacates — only for subtenants, and only if a proper §6.14 notice was served in time. A co-tenant is not subject to that increase. This does not apply while an original tenant still lives in Unit 1.sf.gov — Rent increases under §6.14 and Costa-Hawkins · Civ. Code §1954.53 — Costa-Hawkins (leginfo)

A compliant notice, in plain form

This is a template for the owner's file, not a Rent Board form and not legal advice. Fill every bracket; do not add persuasive language, and do not reference the occupants, a complaint or the market.

NOTICE OF RENT INCREASE Date of service: [date] To: [tenant name(s)] Unit: [unit identifier] Property: [street address] 1. Your current base rent is ${current_rent} per month. 2. Effective [effective date], your base rent will increase by [X]% — a dollar increase of ${dollar_increase} per month — to a new base rent of ${new_rent} per month. 3. The increase consists of: [A]% annual allowable increase for the rate year [rate year] [B]% banked increase carried forward from [years] 4. The percentages are added and applied once to the base rent. No passthrough or fluctuating charge is included in this calculation. 5. Please keep this notice with your rent records. [Owner or manager name and contact details]

Elements drawn from: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF · sf.gov — Banked rent increases · Civ. Code §827 (leginfo) · S.F. Admin. Code §37.3 (American Legal Publishing)

Service and proof

  • Personal delivery to the tenant, or mail under Code Civ. Proc. §1013. If mailed within California, the notice period gains five days — so a 30-day notice should be mailed at least 35 days before the effective date.
  • Keep the notice copy, the service record (delivery receipt, or certified-mail tracking), and the licence page for the relevant year in one file per unit.
  • If the increase starts later than the anniversary, the later date becomes the new anniversary and the next increase must wait at least 12 months.

Sources: Civ. Code §827 (leginfo) · Code Civ. Proc. §1013 (leginfo) · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF
