Units and arithmetic

Each tenancy has its own ceiling, built from the allowances that accrued on its own anniversary and were not used. Nothing here is a market rent: a rent-controlled tenancy's lawful rent is a sum of published percentages.

Which percentage applies: the "rate year" rule

The Rent Board publishes each allowance as a period beginning 1 March, and the percentage in effect on the effective date is the one that applies. So an increase effective on or after 1 March 2026 and before 1 March 2027 uses the 1.6% row; one effective on or after 1 March 2027 uses the next row, which has not been published. In shorthand on this site, "rate year 2026" means 1 Mar 2026 – 28 Feb 2027 and "rate year 2027" means 1 Mar 2027 – 29 Feb 2028. Both flats' anniversaries (1 February and 1 June) fall inside the same rate year as their calendar date, so there is no offset to apply.

Sources: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF · sf.gov — Form 571, Allowable Annual Increases 26–27 (PDF) · sf.gov — Current Rates (rent increase, relocation, deposit)

Two arithmetic traps

  1. The 2027-28 rate is not published. Any notice with an effective date after 28 February 2027 must use the allowance in effect on that later date F-11. Repeating 1.6% beyond its rate year is the error; the Rent Board's own guidance says to "use the percentage that will be in effect on the date of the increase, not the percentage in effect on the date they serve the notice." Source: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF
  2. Percentages are added, then applied once. Compounding either unit produces a higher, unlawful number: Unit 1 would be $4,115.74 instead of $4,114.85, and Unit 2 would be $3,585.72. Source: sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years
