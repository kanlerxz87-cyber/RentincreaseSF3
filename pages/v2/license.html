Licence and process

The obligation that comes before any number: report into the Housing Inventory, obtain the rent-increase licence, and make sure it is in force on the date the increase takes effect.

Direct answer

Yes. As an individual owner of a two-unit building you must (a) report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory every year, and (b) hold a rent-increase licence that is in effect on the effective date of the increase. Being an individual rather than a corporation is irrelevant: the ordinance classifies by building size, and the "1–9 units" track began on 1 March 2023. There is no separate application for the licence and no fee for it, and you do not send it to the tenant or file your notice with the Rent Board.

Sources: sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF

Filing walkthrough

  1. Open an account. Use the Rent Board Portal (Rent Board Portal). The PIN on the Annual Notice adds the property to the account; 311 can reset a PIN. The Portal is also where the fee is paid.
  2. Report each unit. Owner-occupied, non-owner occupied and vacant units are asked different questions. For the two rented flats: owner or manager contact details, business registration number if any, approximate square footage in 250 sq ft increments, bedrooms, bathrooms, the date the current occupancy began, and the base rent in $250 increments plus whether utilities are included. Everything is submitted under penalty of perjury, and the answers are public. Approximate answers are acceptable if made honestly.
  3. File by 1 March. Late filing is possible, but it does not repair an increase that already took effect without a licence.
  4. Read back the licence. After filing, check the property in the Portal, open the document icon for the non-owner-occupied unit, and confirm the licence period covers the intended effective date. Previous years can be viewed too.
  5. Keep the copy with the notice, the proof of service and the calculation.

Source: sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · Rent Board Portal
