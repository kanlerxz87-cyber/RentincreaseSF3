San Francisco — local law

The San Francisco Residential Rent Stabilization and Arbitration Ordinance (Administrative Code Chapter 37) and the Rent Board’s Rules and Regulations are the operative law for both units. They are stricter than state law and therefore control. This page sets out every local rule that bears on a rent increase, with the official source for each.

The annual allowable increase

The annual allowable increase (AGA)
Item Value Official source Status
Current AGA 1.6% for increases effective March 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027 Source: S1 · S2 · S3 Verified
Prior AGA 1.4% for March 1, 2025 – February 28, 2026 Source: S1 · S2 Verified
How it is set 60% of the increase in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers in the Bay Area; published by the Rent Board each year, effective March 1. Source: S3 · S6 Verified
Statutory ceiling In no event may the allowable annual increase be greater than 7%. Source: S6 Verified
Petition needed? No. A landlord is not required to file a petition to impose an annual allowable increase. Source: S17 · S4 Verified
Frequency Once every 12 months. Source: S3 · S16 Verified
Applied to Base rent only. Includes housing services such as parking and storage; excludes temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges. Source: S3 · S16 Verified
Rounding Increases cannot be rounded up to the nearest dollar. Source: S3 · S17 Verified
Which year’s rate Use the percentage that will be in effect on the effective date of the increase, not the percentage in effect when the notice is served. Source: S3 Verified
Initial rent on a vacant unit No limit. There is no limit on what a landlord may first charge for an empty rent-controlled unit; Rules & Regs Sec. 4.15 confirms the Ordinance does not regulate initial rent levels for a new tenancy. Source: S3 · S5 Verified

Banking — recovering increases that were never imposed

Banking of skipped increases
Item Official rule Source Status
Right to bank A landlord who refrains from imposing an annual increase, or any part of it, may accumulate it and impose it on or after the tenant’s subsequent rent increase anniversary date. Source: S5 · S4 · S6 Verified
Maturity ‘A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable.’ (Rules & Regs Sec. 4.12(a) — the controlling text.) Source: S5 Verified
Conflicting statement on sf.gov The sf.gov Banked rent increases page says a skipped increase banks only if ‘at least 24 full months’ pass — and then contradicts itself in its own worked example. See flag F3. Source: S4 Conflict
Combination Banked percentages are added together and combined with the current annual increase; they are not compounded and not rounded up. Source: S5 · S4 Verified
Cap on the total There is no limit on how much can be banked, back to April 1, 1982. Source: S4 Verified
Expiry Banked increases do not expire during a tenancy. Source: S4 Verified
Partial increases A landlord does not have to impose the maximum. If only part is used, the rest stays banked and may be imposed after at least 12 months, or on a future anniversary date. Source: S4 Verified
Change of owner New owners may use banked increases accumulated by prior owners. Source: S4 Verified
New tenant Banked increases do not carry over to a new tenant. Source: S4 Verified
Forfeiture risk Changing the anniversary date permanently forfeits any skipped rate that had not yet matured. Source: S3 Verified
Disclosure duty To impose an accumulated increase the landlord must inform the tenant which portion is banked and the dates on which it is based. Failure to include that information does not by itself render the increase null and void. Source: S5 · S4 Verified
Good-faith overage If a banked increase exceeds the limit by no more than one-half of one percent of the prior base rent and was given in good faith, an Administrative Law Judge readjusts the base rent rather than voiding the increase. Source: S5 Verified
Flag F3 — two official sf.gov pages disagree

The Banked rent increases page states a skipped increase banks only after ‘at least 24 full months’. The controlling regulation, Rules and Regulations Sec. 4.12(a), states ‘a full 12 months’, and the same sf.gov page’s own worked example banks a rate that had been available for only 12 months. This project applies the regulation (12 months) and reproduces all four official worked examples exactly under that rule. Because two official pages conflict, confirm with a Rent Board counselor on 415-252-4600 before serving a banked-increase notice. Source: S5 · S4 · S3

The rent increase license

The rent increase license (Rent Ordinance Sec. 37.15)
Item Official rule Source Status
Who must report All owners of residential property, most even if they are not landlords. Source: S8 Verified
What it produces Owners who report a tenant-occupied unit automatically receive a rent increase license, which allows them to impose annual allowable and banked increases. Source: S8 Verified
When it must exist A current license must be on file before any annual or banked rent increase goes into effect. Without it the notice is not enforceable. Source: S8 · S9 Verified
Consequence of no license A rent increase that goes into effect while the unit is unlicensed may be null and void — and this may apply for multiple years. The tenant may be entitled to a refund. Source: S9 Verified
Deadline March 1 every year. Late submission is still accepted and the Rent Board assesses no monetary penalty for filing late — but the increase is unenforceable until a license exists. Source: S8 Verified
Where Rent Board Portal, portal.sfrb.org. Reporting is free and available 24/7. Source: S8 · S22 Verified
Operative dates July 1, 2022 for non-condominium buildings with 10 or more units; reporting began March 1, 2023 for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units. An official Rent Board worksheet gives different dates (July 1, 2020 / March 1, 2021) — see flag F5. Source: S8 · S13 Conflict
Privacy consequence The information entered in the Housing Inventory submission is public, including approximate square footage, bedroom and bathroom counts, occupancy history and base rent in $250 increments. Source: S8 Verified
Tenant visibility A tenant may search the Rent Board Portal to check whether the landlord holds a current license, and may file a Tenant Summary Petition (Form 361) if a notice took effect while unlicensed. Source: S8 · S9 · S14 Verified
Status for these units — blank, unverified — — not supplied and not published anywhere readable by this project. See flag F4. Source: S8 Unverified – flagged

Prohibited bases for an increase

Bases on which an increase is PROHIBITED
Prohibited basis Official rule Source
A new or additional occupant ‘A landlord may not impose increases solely because a tenant has added an additional occupant to an existing tenancy… The prohibition… shall apply notwithstanding a rental agreement or lease that specifically permits a rent increase for additional occupants.’ (Sec. 37.3(a)(11)(A)) Such a charge is an unlawful rent increase; any agreement to pay it is void and unenforceable, and a tenant may petition for a refund. Source: S6 · S3 · S11
Replacement of a departing roommate ‘The landlord may not increase the rent for the unit based on the replacement of a departing tenant or the addition of new occupants, even if agreed to by the tenant.’ Source: S11
Algorithmic rent-setting Sec. 37.10C, ‘Use and Sale of Algorithmic Devices Prohibited’ (Ordinance 224-24, effective October 14, 2024), prohibits the sale or use of algorithmic devices to set rents or manage occupancy levels for residential units. Source: S6
Harassment or forcing a tenant out Sec. 37.10B (Tenant Harassment) and Sec. 37.9F (Circumvention of Tenant Protections) are part of Chapter 37. An increase imposed for the purpose of compelling a tenant to leave is exposed under these provisions and under the fair-housing statutes. Source: S6 · S33 · S34
Severing a housing service without a rent reduction Garage and parking facilities, driveways, storage spaces, laundry rooms, decks, patios or gardens supplied with a unit may not be severed without just cause, and any severance, substantial reduction or removal of a housing service must be offset by a corresponding reduction in rent. Source: S6
An increase without a license Null and void in its entirety. See the license table above. Source: S8 · S9
A non-conforming notice ‘Nonconforming Rent Increases. Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this section shall be null and void.’ (Sec. 37.3(b)(5)) Rules & Regs Sec. 4.10(b) is to the same effect, with the half-percent good-faith exception. Source: S6 · S5
