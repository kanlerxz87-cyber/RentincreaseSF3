Correct way to inform tenants (verified steps)

StepRequirementSource
1. Confirm coverage & rateConfirm the unit is rent-controlled and use the current Rent Board allowable increase (1.4% / 1.6%).sf.gov
2. Calculate on base rentMultiply base rent by the allowable %; include housing services, exclude passthroughs; do not round up.sf.gov
3. Wait 12 monthsOnly one increase in any 12-month period.sf.gov
4. Serve written notice30-day written notice (90 days only if combined increases in the year exceed 10%).sf.gov; Civ. Code §827
5. Apply uniformlyServe the same lawful increase on both units to avoid discrimination exposure.sf.gov

Reasonable reasons & who does what

QuestionAnswerSource
Reasonable reasons for an increaseUnder SF rent control the lawful reason is simply the annual allowable increase (CPI-based). Larger increases only via Rent Board petition (capital improvements, operating costs).sf.gov
How large corporations vs. individual owners proceedBoth must follow the same Rent Board cap and notice rules; neither may exceed the allowable increase without a petition. Individual owners additionally risk AB 1482-style scrutiny if the unit were not locally covered.sf.gov AB 1482
Raise one unit or both?Both. Uniform, simultaneous, same-percentage increases are normal business practice and avoid discrimination inquiries; differential increases are a fair-housing risk (SF Human Rights Commission handles discrimination cases).sf.gov; hud.gov
Tenant review / free legal helpTenants can file a free Tenant Petition (incl. invalid rent-increase notice) and obtain free eviction/housing legal help: Eviction Defense Collaborative 415-659-9184; Rent Board counseling 415-252-4600.sf.gov petitions; sf.gov evictions; sf.gov Rent Board
