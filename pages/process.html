Step 0 (before everything): Housing Inventory & license — individual owners included

All owners must do this — individuals and corporations alike. Before you can impose any annual or banked increase on a rent-controlled unit, you must have a current rent-increase license on file. You get it by reporting to the Rent Board Housing Inventory. Without it, the increase is null and void even if the math is perfect.

StepRequirementOfficial source
A Who files?All owners of residential property in SF — individual persons, not just entities. 2-unit building = \"1–9 units\" track: reporting began March 1, 2023, updates every March 1.sf.gov — Housing Inventory
B How?File online via Rent Board Portal: owner/manager contact, business registration if any, approx. sq ft (250-sq-ft increments), bedrooms/bathrooms, occupancy/vacancy dates in past 12 months, base rent in $250 increments + utility info for tenant-occupied units. Under penalty of perjury. Update contact changes within 30 days.sf.gov — Housing Inventory
C What you getReporting a tenant-occupied unit automatically generates a rent-increase license — no separate application. Verify in Portal that license will be in effect on the effective date of the increase (not just the notice date).sf.gov — Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Banked increases
D If you skip itIncrease is null and void; tenant may get refund via Unlawful Rent Increase Petition; Board may notify tenants of noncompliance. Late filing helps going forward but does not cure a prior effective date without a license.sf.gov — Notice of Noncompliance

Correct way to inform tenants — verified steps (after license confirmed)

StepRequirementSource
1 Confirm coverage & rateConfirm unit is rent-controlled; use the allowable % in effect on the effective date: 1.4% (3/1/2025–2/28/2026) or 1.6% (3/1/2026–2/28/2027). See Form 571 for history.sf.gov — rent increases · Form 571 PDF · sf.gov — 1.6% announcement
2 Calculate on base rent onlyMultiply base rent (includes housing services like parking/storage; excludes passthroughs/fluctuating charges) by the allowable %. Do not round up. Example: $3,995 × 0.016 = $63.92. Banked amounts are additive, never compounded.sf.gov — Calculate / base rent only · sf.gov — banking (additive)
3 Respect 12-month rule & anniversaryOnly one increase per 12 months. First increase allowed 12 months after lease start; thereafter on the anniversary date (date of last increase). Changing the date may forfeit a banked year (see 12-vs-24-month note on Flagged).sf.gov — Timing & anniversary
4 Handle banking correctlyA skipped increase is only banked if 24 full months without any increase have passed (per current banking page). No cap on total banked (back to Apr 1, 1982); never expires during tenancy. Use Form 571 percentages for each skipped year.sf.gov — Banked increases · Form 571 PDF
5 Draft written noticeWritten notice only. Must state: dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and for banked increases — which part is banked and from which years. No reason is legally required for an annual/banked increase.sf.gov — Giving notice · sf.gov — Banked (notice contents)
6 Serve with correct lead time30-day written notice if increase ≤10%; 90-day if >10% alone or combined with another increase in same 12 months. +5 days if mailed within California (CCP §1013). Personal delivery or posting/sub-mailing also must follow statute.sf.gov — Giving notice · Civ. Code §827 · CCP §1013
7 Apply uniformlyServe the same lawful formula on both units (each on its own anniversary) to avoid discrimination exposure. Keep copies + proof of service (receipt / certified-mail tracking) with the property file and license confirmation.sf.gov — evictions / HRC

Model wording (adapt, have attorney review): \"Pursuant to SF Rent Ordinance §37.3 and Rent Board Form 571, your base rent will increase by [X]% ($[Y]), consisting of [Z]% annual allowable for [rate year] [plus [W]% banked from [years]], effective [date]. Your current license is on file with the Rent Board.\"

Reasonable reasons for the increase

QuestionVerified answerSource
What counts as a reasonable reason for an annual/banked increase?The lawful reason is simply \"annual allowable increase (CPI-based) per SF Rent Ordinance §37.3(a)(1) and Rent Board Form 571, plus banked unused increases per §37.3(a)(2)\" — no further justification is required.sf.gov — rent increases · sf.gov — banking
When ARE reasons required?Only for Landlord Petitions beyond the annual/banked amount (e.g., capital improvements, substantial rehabilitation, operating & maintenance) — which require Rent Board approval via petition (Forms 540–600 series). Not applicable to these two standard increases.sf.gov — Landlord petitions & passthroughs
What reason should I NOT give?Never reference a tenant's protected characteristic, complaint, repair request, or additional occupant — that invites retaliation/discrimination claims. The Apr 2026 occupancy addition in Unit 1 is not a lawful basis for extra rent, even if the lease purports to allow it.sf.gov — additional occupants (unlawful) · Civ. Code §1942.5 · Gov. Code §12955

How large corporations vs. individual owners actually proceed

Owner typePracticeSource
Large corporations / REITs / managed portfoliosApply the published annual % portfolio-wide on a fixed calendar; banking tracked in property-management software; standardized notices; licenses kept current via bulk Housing Inventory filing; algorithmic pricing now banned in SF.sf.gov — Housing Inventory (bulk uploads for 5+ units) · sf.gov — algorithmic devices ban (Admin §37.10C)
Individual owners (like this case)Often skip years informally, lose banking documentation, miss March 1 filing window, miscalculate base rent vs. passthroughs, or attempt \"additional occupant\" charges — the most common reasons valid-percentage increases are overturned.sf.gov — rent increases
Bottom lineSame ceiling, same paperwork. The difference is process discipline, not entitlement. Adopt the corporate calendar: file Mar 1, confirm license, calendar each anniversary, use one written policy for the property.sf.gov · sf.gov — Housing Inventory

Tenant scrutiny — would this pass a SF tenant lawyer?

SF tenants can obtain free counseling and representation; any increase you serve should be able to survive their review:

ProtectionWhat the tenant can doSource
Tenant Petition — unlawful rent increaseFile a free petition challenging an increase that exceeds the allowable/banked amount or was served without a license, improper notice, or wrong base rent.sf.gov — Tenant Petitions · sf.gov — banking (license note)
Free legal helpEviction Defense Collaborative 415-659-9184 (Tenant Right to Counsel intake); Rent Board counseling 415-252-4600; SF Tenants Union; Bay Area Legal Aid; La Raza Centro Legal.sf.gov — evictions & free help · sf.gov — Tenant Petitions
Retaliation protectionA rent increase within 180 days of a tenant exercising protected rights (complaint, organizing) is presumed retaliatory.Civ. Code §1942.5
Discrimination protectionTreatment that disadvantages a tenant based on protected characteristics (race, religion, sex, disability, familial status, source of income, etc.) is unlawful. Differential increases without objective justification invite HRC complaints.sf.gov — HRC referral · Gov. Code §12955 · hud.gov

One-unit vs. both-unit increase

OptionLawfulnessTenant-lawyer viewSource
Both units — same formula, each on its anniversaryLawfulLowest risk / most defensible. Uniform application defeats any discrimination inference; seen as normal business practice.sf.gov · sf.gov
One unit onlyNot per se unlawfulHigher scrutiny. Lawful only with documented, objective, non-protected-class reason (e.g., one tenancy already at higher effective rent, different anniversary histories). Recommendation: Raise both units under one written property-wide policy. If you raise only one, put the objective reason in writing and keep it with the property file. See also Executive Summary and Flagged for Review.
