Correct way to inform tenants (verified steps)

StepRequirementSource
1. Report to the Rent Board & get a rent increase licenseRequired for every owner, including individual (non-corporate) owners. Report each tenant-occupied unit into the Rent Board Housing Inventory (portal.sfrb.org); a license is then generated automatically. A current license must be on file before any annual or banked rent increase goes into effect. Buildings with 1–9 units are covered for increases effective on or after March 1, 2023. The license does not need to be included with the tenant notice.sf.gov — Housing Inventory · sf.gov — rent increases · sf.gov — Ordinance 265-20
2. Confirm coverage & rateConfirm the unit is rent-controlled and use the current Rent Board allowable increase (1.4% / 1.6%) for the increase's effective date.sf.gov · sf.gov — SF rental laws
3. Calculate on base rentMultiply base rent by the allowable %; include housing services, exclude passthroughs; do not round up. Banked years are added, never compounded.sf.gov · sf.gov — banked increases
4. Wait 12 monthsOnly one increase in any 12-month period; the date of the last increase becomes the anniversary date.sf.gov
5. Serve written notice30-day written notice (90 days if the increase, alone or combined with others in the prior 12 months, exceeds 10%); +5 days if mailed. Notice must state the dollar amount, the percentage, and the effective date — and itemize any banked portion with its dates.sf.gov; Civ. Code §827; sf.gov — banked increases
6. Increase takes effectOnly after the notice period runs and a current rent increase license is on file on the effective date.sf.gov — Housing Inventory
7. Apply uniformlyServe the same lawful increase policy on both units to avoid discrimination exposure.sf.gov · hud.gov

Reasonable reasons & who does what

QuestionAnswerSource
Reasonable reasons for an increaseUnder SF rent control the lawful reason is simply the annual allowable increase (CPI-based) plus any properly banked years. Larger increases only via Rent Board petition (capital improvements, operating and maintenance). No reason is legally required for an annual/banked increase; if a reason is given it must never reference a protected characteristic, a complaint, a repair request, or an additional occupant.sf.gov
How large corporations vs. individual owners proceedBoth must follow the same Rent Board cap, notice rules, and license requirement; neither may exceed the allowable increase without a petition. SF banned algorithmic rent-setting devices for residential units in 2024 (Admin. Code §37.10C).sf.gov AB 1482 · sf.gov — §37.10C
Raise one unit or both?Both, under the same written policy. Uniform, simultaneous, same-percentage increases are normal business practice and avoid discrimination inquiries; differential increases are a fair-housing risk (SF Human Rights Commission handles discrimination cases).sf.gov · hud.gov
Tenant review / free legal helpTenants can file a free Tenant Petition (incl. invalid notice of rent increase) and obtain free eviction/housing legal help: Eviction Defense Collaborative 415-659-9184; Rent Board counseling 415-252-4600.sf.gov petitions; sf.gov evictions; sf.gov Rent Board
