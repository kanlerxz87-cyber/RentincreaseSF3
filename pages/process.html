Licence & process — step by step

Written for someone who has never raised rent through the Rent Board. Each step states what to do, what the official source says, and what to keep.

The whole process in one sentence

Report both units in the Rent Board Housing Inventory → check that a rent-increase licence is current on your chosen effective date → calculate the annual percentage in effect on that date (+ any banked amounts) on base rent only → serve the correct written notice with the right lead time → keep proof. Annual and banked increases do not require a petition for approval. The cited guidance does not explicitly resolve whether a notice copy must be filed; confirm that question with the Rent Board.

Step-by-step

  1. Check that the units are covered. Units built on or before 13 June 1979 generally have rent control and eviction protection, unless the unit is a single-family dwelling or most condominiums. The construction year and rent-control status are owner-reported; the total number of units and applicable exemptions are unknown. Confirm the City property record and consult a Rent Board counsellor.
    Source: sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws · sf.gov — Rent Board counselling line 415-252-4600
  2. Report into the Housing Inventory — every year, by 1 March. Use the Rent Board Portal, create an account, and report each unit: owner/manager business contact, business registration if any, approximate square footage in 250 sq ft increments, bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy start date and base rent in $250 increments (plus whether utilities are included). Submissions are made under penalty of perjury. Reporting is free and there is no monetary penalty for filing after the deadline; owners who cannot access the online Portal may submit paper filings by email (rentboard.inventory@sfgov.org) or at the Rent Board office, though paper processing is slower. (Note: the annual Rent Board Fee — $59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026–27, due 1 March — is a separate obligation from the free Housing Inventory report.)
    Source: sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee · Rent Board Portal (Housing Inventory / licence look-up)
  3. Confirm the licence — on the effective date, not the notice date. A licence is generated automatically for non-owner-occupied units after the report. Check it in the Portal. The Rent Board's exact test is that the licence be held “on the effective date of the notice”; without it the notice “will not be enforceable”. The licence does not have to be sent with the notice. The Rent Board's noncompliance page states the practical stakes: a notice that takes effect while the unit is unlicensed “may be null and void (this may apply for multiple years)” and the tenant “may be entitled to a rent refund”.
    Source: sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory
  4. Calculate on base rent only, using the percentage in effect on the effective date. Base rent includes housing services such as parking or storage and excludes temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges. Do not round up. Use the percentage that will be in effect on the date of the increase, not the date the notice is served. For an increase effective from 1 March 2026 through 28 February 2027 that is 1.6%. Review fixed-term lease provisions with counsel; the statutory notice procedure cited here covers periodic tenancies.
    Source: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
  5. Add any banked amounts — additively, never compounded. A skipped increase is banked when the Rent Board's conditions are met; banked percentages are added together and then multiplied by the current base rent. There is no cap on banked amounts back to 1 April 1982, they do not expire during the tenancy, and they cannot be carried to a new tenant.
    Source: sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years
  6. Check the timing conditions before you commit. The first annual increase can be imposed 12 months after the lease began; after that, no more than once every 12 months on the tenant's anniversary date; if you increase later than the anniversary, the effective date becomes the new anniversary date and a later annual increase may be lost. Avoid retaliatory increases. A recent habitability complaint or repair request calls for particular caution; protections can extend beyond 180 days. Consult counsel. Do not charge extra because a new occupant moved in.
    Source: sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited
  7. Serve the written notice in time. 30 days' written notice if the increase is 10% or less of the rent charged at any time in the previous 12 months (alone or combined with other increases); 90 days if more than 10%. Personal delivery, or mail under Code of Civil Procedure § 1013 — if mailed, add 5 calendar days. State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and, for a banked increase, which part is banked and the dates underlying it.
    Source: Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy · Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension · sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Part IV §§ 4.10 and 4.12
  8. Keep the file and diarise next year. Keep the notice, proof of service, a screenshot of the current licence, the calculation and the rent history. Update owner/manager contact details within 30 days of any change and re-report by 1 March. The date of the increase becomes the new anniversary date.
    Source: sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
  9. If a tenant petitions, respond with the file. Before an increase takes effect, a tenant who sees no licence in the Portal may file a Tenant Summary Petition (Form 518); after an increase takes effect, a tenant may file a Tenant Petition (Form 516C, box C) for an unlawful rent increase or a determination of lawful rent — free, with no filing deadline — heard by a Rent Board Administrative Law Judge without needing a lawyer.
    Source: sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history · sf.gov — Tenant petitions
