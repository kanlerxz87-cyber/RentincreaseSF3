F2
High		 Source conflict Third-party sites publish a 2026-27 San Francisco allowable increase of 1.7%; the official rate is 1.6%

The SF Rent Board's own Current Rates page and document 571 both state 1.6% for 3/1/2026-2/28/2027 (and 1.4% for 3/1/2025-2/28/2026). At least one widely circulated third-party 'SF rent control calculator' states the current AGA is 1.7% for the same period while also stating the previous period was 1.4% - an internally inconsistent combination, since the official 1.7% belongs to 3/1/2024-2/28/2025. Another third-party guide states the 2025-26 rate was 1.4% and separately that a 90-day notice applies 'for increases of 10% or more', whereas the statute sets the long-notice trigger at greater than 10%.

Action taken: 1.6% adopted throughout. No third-party figure is used anywhere on this site. If any document you hold shows 1.7% for the 2026-27 period, treat it as incorrect.

F3
High		 Official source conflict Two sf.gov pages state DIFFERENT banking maturity rules (12 months vs 24 months)

The sf.gov page Banked rent increases states: 'A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost.' The sf.gov page Learn about rent increases states in its own worked example that a skipped increase failed to bank 'because a full 12 months has not elapsed'. The Banked rent increases page then contradicts itself: its own worked example banks the 1.7% that had been available for only 12 months (moved in 8/1/2022, first increase 8/1/2025, banking both 8/1/2023 at 3.6% and 8/1/2024 at 1.7%).

Action taken: Resolved in favour of the controlling regulation: Rent Board Rules and Regulations Sec. 4.12(a), as published by the Rent Board on sf.gov, reads 'A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable.' All calculations here use 12 months. The 24-month sentence appears to be an error on that page. Because two official pages disagree, confirm the maturity rule with a Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) before serving any banked-increase notice. The calculator in this repository reproduces all four official worked examples exactly under the 12-month rule, which is strong corroboration.

High		 Compliance gate Rent increase license status is UNKNOWN for both units - no increase is enforceable without it

Under Rent Ordinance Sec. 37.15 and the Rent Board Housing Inventory rules, a current rent increase license must be on file on the EFFECTIVE DATE of the increase or the entire annual or banked increase is null and void, regardless of whether the arithmetic was correct. The license status of these two units was not supplied and is not published anywhere this project can read.

Action taken: Left blank on the unit dossiers - not inferred. Before serving any notice: file or refresh the Housing Inventory at portal.sfrb.org (deadline March 1 each year, late filing is permitted and carries no monetary penalty) and confirm the license covers the effective date. Note the submitted information is PUBLIC and includes base rent in $250 increments.

The Housing Inventory page states the requirement became operative July 1, 2022 for non-condo buildings with 10 or more units, with reporting beginning March 1, 2023 for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units. High		 Legal fact gap Unit A had a change in occupants - whether a market-rate reset is available turns on facts not supplied

Unit A's original tenancy (began 2025-02-01) had three tenants; the continuing tenancy from 2026-04-01 has two, one of whom is an original tenant and one of whom is new. Two different legal routes could in principle permit a market-rate reset, and both are unavailable on the facts as stated: (i) Cal. Civ. Code Sec. 1954.53(d)(3) expressly does NOT apply 'to partial changes in occupancy... where one or more of the occupants... remains an occupant in lawful possession'; and (ii) Rules & Regs Sec. 6.14 permits an unlimited increase only when the LAST original occupant vacates AND a timely written 6.14 notice was served on each subsequent occupant. Whether the new occupant is a co-tenant (never subject to a Costa-Hawkins reset) or a subtenant, and whether any 6.14 notice was ever served, were not supplied.

Action taken: Analysed on the facts as stated: an original occupant remains, so NO market-rate reset is available and the lawful ceiling stays tied to the $3,995.00 base rent with a February 1 anniversary. The unresolved sub-questions are recorded here rather than assumed. Do not serve a Sec. 6.14 or Costa-Hawkins notice on these facts without a written legal opinion.

Low		 Verification gap No federal statute sets a rent-increase ceiling for private unsubsidised housing - a negative finding

The federal maximum allowable rent increase is left BLANK. This is a negative finding: the Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. Sec. 3601 et seq.) regulates the MANNER in which rent may be set, not its amount, and federal rent limits attach only to federally assisted housing (for example Section 8 and HUD-insured programmes) or to programme-specific rules. A negative finding cannot be proved by citing a document that states it.

Action taken: Published as 'No federal ceiling - not applicable', with the Fair Housing Act cited for what federal law DOES require. Recorded here so the blank cell is understood as deliberate, not as an omission.
