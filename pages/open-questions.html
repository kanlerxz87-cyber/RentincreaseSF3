Known vs unknown — the information register

A decision about a rent increase is only as good as the facts behind it. This page is the information register: the 11 things already established, the 11 pieces of information still missing, and the official route that would close each gap — with the order to resolve them in. Verified 13 November 2026.

How this page relates to the two others. The question-by-question decision gate — what blocks a notice, what settles it, how each answer moves the number, and the do-not-serve test — is on the Open questions page. The paths you can choose between are on the Suggestions & decision path page. This page holds the underlying inventory of what is known and what is not.
Every row below links the official rule that governs the point. Owner-supplied facts are labelled as such: a row's source link shows the rule the fact is measured against, not a government record of the fact.

3. The order to resolve them in

  1. Close the two blocking unknowns first. A licence check in the Rent Board Portal and a Unit B rent ledger cost nothing and decide whether any of the percentages matter. Until both are done, every figure on this site is conditional.
  2. Then fix the date. Coverage and the anniversary date decide when an increase may first take effect; the percentage only decides how much.
  3. Then choose the amount from the paths on the suggestions page, using the percentage in force on the intended effective date.
  4. Then check the reason and the record. Retaliation and protected-characteristic exposure are tested on the surrounding facts, not on the arithmetic.
  5. Then serve, and keep the file — notice, proof of service, licence screenshot, calculation and rent history.
  6. Re-check the pending legislation on the service date itself. File #261020 is not law; if it is enacted the banked portion of a catch-up may be limited.
