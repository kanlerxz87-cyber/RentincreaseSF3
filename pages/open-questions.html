The rule this page enforces

Not ready to decide. Every question in section 2 turns on a record that sits outside this project — the City property record, the Rent Board Portal, the leases and ledger for Unit B, the code-notice file, the subsidy file, the fee account and the complaint timeline — except Q4, which needs the Rent Board's own reading of a mid-tenancy renewal. Choosing a percentage before those are in hand means choosing on assumptions, and the assumptions are the first things a tenant-side reviewer tests. The two that most often invalidate an otherwise correct calculation are the licence (Q2) and the rent history (Q3).

1. Already settled — answers you do not need to re-research

QuestionAnswer, as the official sources state itSource
Is the duty to inform the Rent Board and hold a licence a state or a local law?Local. Created by San Francisco Ordinance No. 265-20 (adopted 2020, effective 18 January 2021), which amended the City's Rent Ordinance; reporting reached condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units on 1 March 2023. No California statute creates it.sf.gov — Ordinance 265-20: Housing Inventory and rent-increase licence · sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Does being an individual owner rather than a corporation change the duty?No. The rule is written by owner category, not entity form: “All owners of residential property are required to report … Most owners are required to do this even if you are not a landlord.”sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Is there a separate licence application, hearing or fee?No. The licence is generated automatically for non-owner-occupied units once the report is filed; reporting is free, and the licence “does not need to be included with your rent increase notice”.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Does an ordinary annual or banked increase need Rent Board approval?No petition is required. Approval petitions exist for other increase types — capital improvements, rehabilitation, hardship, comparable rents — not for the annual or banked amount.sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
What is the maximum percentage right now?1.6% for an increase effective 1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027 (60% of Bay Area CPI). Prior year 1.4%; the full 1982–2027 table is published as document 571.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF)
Does the state cap give me more room?No. California allows 5% + CPI up to 10% (8.8% for the San Francisco area for increases starting 1 August 2026), but Civil Code § 1947.12(d)(3) excludes housing already restricted by a local ordinance to a lower amount, and the Attorney General's table says the local rule then applies.California Attorney General — Limits on rent increases · Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap
What must the notice say, and how far ahead?Written notice, 30 days if the increase is 10% or less of the rent charged in the prior 12 months (alone or combined), 90 days if more; +5 calendar days if mailed within California. The Rent Board's elements are the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and which part is banked.Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy · Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1013 — service by mail extension · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Must I state a reason?Not for an annual or banked increase. The published notice elements do not include a justification, and no petition is filed. The prohibition runs the other way: the increase may not be retaliatory, may not turn on a protected characteristic, and may not be charged merely because an occupant moved in.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited · Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination
May I raise one unit and not the other?Nothing in the ordinance or fair-housing law requires uniform increases (published as an absence of authority, FR-12). What is prohibited is different treatment because of a protected characteristic; the Rent Board does not hear discrimination claims — the Civil Rights Department and HUD do.Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination · 42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Fair Housing Act (govinfo, U.S. Code 2023) · sf.gov — Tenant petitions
Is there a published “normal” or ZIP-level increase for this area?No. The Rent Board publishes one citywide maximum and no average, typical or ZIP-level figure; those cells stay blank on this site (FR-08, FR-09, FR-10).sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF) · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
What can a tenant do if they object?Before the increase takes effect, a Tenant Summary Petition (Form 518) — for example where no licence is on record; after it takes effect, a Tenant Petition (Form 516C, box C) for an unlawful rent increase, which has no filing deadline and is heard by an Administrative Law Judge without a lawyer.sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history · sf.gov — Tenant petitions
Is there a law changing these rules in the near future?Pending, not law. File No. 261020 would cap banked plus capital-improvement increases at 10% per year for three years; status on 13 November 2026 is Pending Committee Action, last recorded action 7 October 2026. The Rent Board's 9 November 2026 notice says it would reach banked increases noticed on or after 15 September 2026 if enacted as proposed.sfgov.legistar.com — File #261020, cap on rent increases for rent-controlled units · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco

2. Blocking questions — close each one in writing before choosing

“Blocking” means the answer can change whether an increase is enforceable at all, not merely how large it is. The answer column is left blank deliberately: these are owner-record questions, and this site does not fill them in.

Q1Are these two units actually under the Rent Ordinance?Everything else assumes it. If a unit is exempt, the ceiling, the banking arithmetic and the licence question all change — and a wrongly assumed exemption is itself a tenant claim.Owner statement: a 1940 building with two rental units. Rent control applies to units with a certificate of occupancy on or before 13 June 1979, excluding single-family dwellings and most condominiums.The City property record: certificate-of-occupancy date, legal unit count, whether either unit is a condominium or separately alienable, and whether any conversion was permitted.Pull the assessor/building record and ask a Rent Board counsellor to confirm coverage; the answer is documentary, not arguable.sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws · sf.gov — Rent Board counselling line 415-252-4600
Q2Is a rent-increase licence in effect for each unit on the intended effective date?Without it the notice “will not be enforceable”, and the Board's noncompliance page says such an increase “may be null and void (this may apply for multiple years)” with a possible rent refund.Nothing on file with this project. No filing history was supplied.The Housing Inventory submission for the current year and the licence record for each unit, checked on the effective date.Search the address or parcel number in the Rent Board Portal and keep a dated screenshot; file the report first if no record exists.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · Rent Board Portal (Housing Inventory / licence look-up)
Q3What is Unit B's complete increase history since 1 June 2019, and its current base rent?The 11.5% banked and 13.1% combined figures exist only if no increase was ever imposed. One increase in any year changes the banked balance, the base rent to multiply, and the anniversary date.A 1 June 2019 lease start and a $3,150 starting rent, plus the owner's statement that the unit is rent controlled.Every signed notice, proof of service and ledger entry from 2019 to 2026, and the rent actually charged today.Owner records; if incomplete, the Rent Board's rent-history review (Request to Review Your Rent History) produces the officially recorded lawful rent.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history
Q4Which date is Unit A's anniversary — 1 February 2027 or 1 April 2027 — and is a fixed term running on that date?It sets the earliest lawful effective date, the percentage available, and whether 1.4% can be banked at all. A renewal that added an occupant on 1 April 2026 at the same rent is not addressed by the published rules.Lease start 1 February 2025; continuation 1 April 2026 at $3,995. The rule: the first annual increase may be imposed 12 months after the date the tenant's lease began, and the effective date then becomes the new anniversary.A counsellor's or ALJ-level answer on whether the 2026 renewal reset the clock, plus the signed renewal's term dates.Rent Board counselling line, in writing if possible; keep the answer with the file. This is FR-02 on the flagged page.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy
Q5Is there an open habitability or code notice for either unit?California bars a rent-increase notice while substandard conditions that an official has flagged in writing have stood unabated for more than 35 days without good cause; violating it exposes actual damages, $100–$5,000 in special damages and attorney's fees.Nothing supplied either way.Any Notice of Violation or inspection record for the building, plus proof of abatement and its date.Check the department records and the tenant correspondence file before the notice is drafted, not after.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.4 — no rent-increase notice while substandard conditions are unabated
Q6Does either household receive a rent subsidy (Housing Choice Voucher or a regulated program)?Subsidy can change who reviews the amount — a housing authority applies its own rent-reasonableness standard — and can change the unit's exemption analysis and the Rent Board Fee position.Nothing supplied.Whether a housing assistance payments contract exists for either unit, and the current contract rent.Owner's own paperwork plus the housing authority; the federal rule for the payment standard is published in 24 CFR § 982.507.24 CFR § 982.507 — Rent to owner: reasonable rent (Housing Choice Voucher) · sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws
Q7Is the Rent Board Fee paid and current for both units?It is a separate obligation from the licence, and unpaid fees escalate (5% per month to a 15% cap) and can be referred as delinquent. Paying it does not create a licence, and the licence does not pay it.No filing or payment record supplied. The 2026–27 fee is $59.00 per dwelling unit, due 1 March; the exemption request window for the 2027 fee closes 14 December 2026.The portal account's fee status for both units for 2026–27 and 2027–28.Rent Board Portal; the fee page also lists the recoverable half ($29.50) for tenants in occupancy as of 1 November.sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee · sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Q8Is there any protected-characteristic or retaliation exposure between the two households?These are the two grounds on which a tenant-side review actually attacks an increase, and neither can be answered from the numbers.Nothing but the absence of information. No complaint history, no correspondence, no dates of repair requests.A dated timeline for each unit: complaints, repair requests, code notices, lease-renewal talks, and the dates of any increase notice, for both units.Owner records assembled into one timeline per unit, then reviewed by counsel before service.Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited · Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination

Suggested order of work

  1. Documents first, numbers second. Pull the two leases, the renewal, every notice and the ledger for Unit B; download the assessor record; screenshot the Portal.
  2. Close Q1 and Q2 (coverage, licence). Until both are yes, no notice should be drafted.
  3. Close Q3 and Q4 (rent history, anniversary). These two decide the percentage and the date together.
  4. Clear Q5–Q8 (habitability notices, subsidy, fee status, complaint and contact timeline). Each is a documented yes/no.
  5. Then pick from the lawful options on the units page, and write one sentence of reason per unit for the file.
  6. Then serve, following the notice page and the planner on the process page, and keep the proof.
