Increase scenarios

Every scenario below is lawful on its face — each is computed only from published Rent Board rates and the controlling regulation. They differ in how much of the entitlement is taken, when, and what is forfeited by moving off the anniversary date. The calculator that produced these figures is asserted against all four of the Rent Board’s own published worked examples before it writes any output.

Notice period 30 days Total is 10% or less
Serve on or before January 2, 2027 (December 28, 2026 if mailed) Civ. Code Sec. 827(b)
Assessment

Lawful and lower-friction. The official rules are explicit: ‘A landlord does not have to impose the maximum allowed increase. If only part of the allowable increase is used, the rest is banked.’ A smaller increase is also less likely to draw a petition. The trade-off is cash now versus cash later, and the 1.4% remains available indefinitely during this tenancy. Source: S4

One open question, flagged: the official material does not state how a remainder that stays banked interacts with a base rent that has since risen. Because Rule 4.12 provides that banked increases ‘shall not be compounded’, the safest reading is that the retained percentage is applied later to base rent without compounding — but that reading is not stated in an official document, so it is not published as a computed figure anywhere on this site. Confirm with a Rent Board counselor before relying on a multi-year phase-in.

Unit B — the phase-in question

A 13.1% increase is lawful but it is not ‘normal’

Nothing in the Ordinance caps a properly banked total: the official page states there is no limit on how much can be banked back to April 1, 1982, and that banked increases do not expire during a tenancy. So 13.1% plus the then-current annual rate is lawful. But it is a one-step recovery of seven years of forbearance, it requires a 90-day notice, and it is the single largest discretionary decision in this file.

The rules expressly permit taking less: ‘A landlord does not have to impose the maximum allowed increase. If only part of the allowable increase is used, the rest is banked.’ A phase-in therefore has a clear official basis. What the official material does not state is how a retained remainder interacts with a base rent that has already risen, and Rule 4.12 forbids compounding banked increases. Because that interaction is not documented in an official source, no phased dollar figures are published on this site. Obtain a Rent Board counselor’s confirmation (415-252-4600) or a written legal opinion before serving a phase-in notice. Source: S4 · S5 · S3
