Tenant-lawyer stress test

The fifteen checks a tenant-side advocate or a Rent Board Administrative Law Judge would run against these increases, and the consequence of each failure.

The test being applied
San Francisco tenants have access to free Rent Board counseling and, in eviction proceedings, a City-funded right to counsel, and any tenant may file an unlawful-rent-increase petition at no charge and with no time limit. S10 S11 S12 S25 The checklist below is what such a review would look for, in the order it would look for it.

Fifteen checks

#Question a reviewer asksWhat a compliant file showsSource
C1Is the unit covered by the Rent Ordinance?Building on or before 13 June 1979, multi-unit residential, not a single-family home or exempt condo; no substantial-rehabilitation or subsidy exemption.S5 S13
C2Is there a current rent increase license on the effective date?Housing Inventory report filed; license visible in the Rent Board portal on the effective date of the increase.S6
C3Is the percentage within the published allowable increase?The rate in effect on the effective date is applied to base rent; the notice cites the Rent Board document 571 rate period.S1 S4
C4Is the arithmetic exact - no compounding, no rounding up?Percentages added, then applied once to base rent; cents kept; no rounding to the nearest dollar.S2 S3 S8
C5Are banked years correctly dated and within the 24-month rule?At least 24 full months without any increase; each banked year identified with its date and rate; only years that could have been imposed after 1 April 1982.S3 S9
C6Has at least 12 months passed since the last increase?Compare the effective date with the prior increase (or lease start) and keep a copy of the prior notice.S2 S9
C7Is the notice period long enough?30 days for 10% or less; 90 days for more than 10%; 5 extra days if mailed. Start counting from the day after service.S15 S2
C8Does the notice contain every required element?Dollar amount, percentage, effective date, which part is annual and which is banked, the dates of the banked years, and how the increase was calculated.S13 S14 S3
C9Is service documented?Personal delivery or mail per CCP 1013; keep the proof of service affidavit and a copy of the notice.S15
C10Is any part of the increase for an additional occupant?None. A co-tenant moving in cannot generate rent, even under a lease clause permitting it.S2 S13
C11Is the increase free of retaliation?No complaint, repair request, inspection, petition or organising activity in the 180 days before service - or, if there was, a documented independent reason and consistent treatment.S18
C12Is the increase free of discrimination?Same rule applied to both units; no protected characteristic correlates with the difference; written criteria recorded before the increase.S19 S20
C13Are other charges kept separate?Passthroughs (water bond, general obligation bond, Rent Board fee share, utility) are separate line items with their own requirements, not folded into the annual increase.S2 S14
C14Would the file survive a tenant petition?Lease, prior notices, proof of service, license, rate printout, and the calculation worksheet are all in one folder (the Rent Board requires no fee and imposes no time limit on petitions).S10 S11
C15Is the tenant told how to question the increase?Notice or covering letter states that the tenant may contact the Rent Board (415-252-4600) and may file a petition at no charge.S10 S11

What happens if a check fails

DefectConsequenceSource
No current rent increase licence on the effective dateThe increase is not enforceable; the tenant can be refunded the difference through a petition.S6
Arithmetic error, compounding, or rounding upThe base rent is readjusted to the correct amount; good-faith overages of half a percent may be corrected rather than voided.S14
Notice too short for the size of the increaseThe increase is not effective as noticed; a new notice with the correct period is needed.S15 S14
Banked amount not identified, or its dates not givenA notice defect; Rule 4.12(b) says it does not by itself void the increase, but it invites a petition.S14
Increase attributable to an additional occupantUnlawful rent increase - prohibited outright, even if the lease allows it.S2 S13
Increase during or after protected activityRetaliation exposure under Civil Code 1942.5, with a 180-day presumption.S18
Different terms tied to a protected characteristicDiscrimination exposure under Government Code 12955 and 42 U.S.C. 3604.S19 S20

The file to keep, per unit

  • Original lease and every renewal, including the 1 April 2026 Unit A lease.
  • Every prior rent-increase notice, with proof of service.
  • Housing Inventory submission confirmation and the rent increase licence.
  • The Rent Board rate printout (document 571) for each year used. S4
  • The calculation worksheet showing percentages added and the final cent amount.
  • The written rule applied to both units, dated before service.
  • Any complaint, repair request or petition history, for the retaliation check. S18
