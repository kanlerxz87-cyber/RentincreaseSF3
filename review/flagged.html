Two conflicting official statements, in full

"24 months"

If a landlord does not impose an allowed rent increase, they may be able to "bank" it and use it later. A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase. If less than 24 months pass, the skipped increase is lost and cannot be imposed at a later date.

sf.gov, Banked rent increases S3

Consistent with the "irregular periods" guidance, which allows adding earlier years only at 24 months or more. S9

"12 months"

A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable.

Rent Board Rules and Regulations, Rule 4.12(a), as published on sf.gov S14

And a worked example on the main rent-increase page applies a 12-month test. S2

The site uses the 24-month reading because it is the dedicated banking page plus the timing page, and because it is the more conservative test. Under either reading, the 1.6%-only option for Unit A and the 10% option for Unit B are lawful. Unit B's full 13.1% is lawful under both readings for an effective date after 1 June 2026.

Also worth a manual look

  • The Rent Board's publicly searchable Housing Inventory record for the property (licence status and reported occupancy date). S6
  • The Planning Property Information Map entry for the parcel (year built, unit count). S5
  • Legistar File 261020, up to and including the final enactment record. S22
  • Any tenant petition already on file - the Rent Board does not charge for filing and imposes no time limit. S11
