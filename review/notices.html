Informing tenants

What to serve, when to serve it, exactly what the notice must say, and what is safe - and unsafe - to give as a reason.

The four things that make a notice enforceable

1. A current licenceHousing Inventory report filed; licence live on the effective date. S6
2. The right amountPublished annual rate plus properly banked years, on base rent, unrounded. S1 S3
3. The right intervalAt least 12 months since the last increase, and 24 months without an increase for banking. S2 S3
4. The right notice30 days for 10% or less, 90 days for more, served personally or by mail, with the required content. S15 S14

Required contents of the notice

ElementWhat to includeSource
Dollar amount of the increaseNew base rent and the dollar increase per month, in figures and words.S2 S13
Percentage of the increaseThe percentage applied, and the rate period it comes from (for example 1.6% for 3/1/2026-2/28/2027).S1 S2
Effective dateThe first day the new rent is due, at least 12 months after the last increase or lease start.S2 S9
Which part is annual and which part is bankedBreak out the current annual portion and each banked year separately.S14 S3
Dates the banked amount is based onThe anniversary date of each banked year and its percentage.S14 S3
Calculation on base rent onlyShow base rent x percentage; exclude passthroughs and fluctuating charges.S2
No rounding upKeep the exact cent amount.S2 S3
Tenant's right to question the increaseRent Board counseling line and the no-fee tenant petition route.S10 S11

Civil Code 827 governs the length and service of the notice; Rent Ordinance 37.3(b) and Rent Board Rule 4.10 add the content requirements for annual and banked increases. S15 S13 S14

Reasonable reasons - what the law does and does not require

The ordinance authorises the annual and banked increase by formula. Neither the ordinance text nor the Rent Board guidance requires the owner to give a reason, and the Rent Board's worked examples contain no justification step. S13 S2 S8 Reasons become mandatory only when the owner seeks more than the annual allowance - for example a capital-improvement or operating-and-maintenance petition, which is decided on evidence by an Administrative Law Judge.
