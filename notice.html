SAN FRANCISCO · RENT STABILIZATION

Notice & safeguards

A practical review sequence, not a ready-to-serve legal notice.

Research reviewed November 6, 2026 · Rates may change

TopicFinding / reviewOfficial source
1 • VerifyConfirm coverage, complete rent ledger, current lawful base rent, lease term, last effective increase and Rent Increase License before choosing an effective date.Official guidance ↗
2 • CalculateUse the rate in effect on the proposed effective date, apply it to lawful base rent only, and do not round up. Separate any properly established banked amount.Official guidance ↗
3 • Provide noticeFor covered periodic tenancies, generally provide written notice at least 30 days for increases of 10% or less and 90 days for over 10%, considering increases over the preceding 12 months. Longer applicable terms may control.Official guidance ↗
4 • ServiceThe Rent Board says add five days when mailed. Verify the lawful method and applicable service rules for the circumstances, including any longer period, before serving.Official guidance ↗
5 • DiscloseInclude amount, percentage, effective date and the annual and any banked portions; identify banked dates as applicable. Preserve proof of service and calculation.Official guidance ↗
6 • Independent reviewAsk a qualified San Francisco housing lawyer to review the facts and draft notice; the Rent Board offers general counseling, and city-listed tenant organizations provide counseling. Free eviction legal help does not imply automatic free attorney review of every rent notice.Official guidance ↗

Educational research, not legal advice. Private facts are user-reported and unverified. Consult the Rent Board and independent counsel before action.
