Notice, deadlines and reasons

The notice is the only part of this process a tenant's lawyer sees first. It is also the part with the fewest formal requirements — which is why noticing correctly is mostly about what you leave out.

What the notice must say

ElementOfficial requirementSource
Dollar amount of the increase“The rent increase notice should include the: Dollar amount of the increase”sf.gov
Percentage of the increase“…Percentage amount of the increase”sf.gov
Effective date“…Date the increase will go into effect”sf.gov
Which part is banked“…Which part of the increase is banked”sf.gov
Portion attributable to each component§ 37.3(b): the landlord “shall inform the tenant, in writing” which portion reflects the annual increase and/or a banked amount, and which portion reflects operating and maintenance costs, comparable rents, capital improvements, rehabilitation, energy measures or an RAP loan.§ 37.3(b)
Which rate to quote“The landlord should use the percentage that will be in effect on the date of the increase, not the percentage in effect on the date they serve the notice.”sf.gov
The license itself“The rent increase license does not need to be included with your rent increase notice.”sf.gov
Written form“30-day written notice of the proposed rent increase / 90-day written notice if the increase… is more than 10%”sf.gov · § 827

A notice that does not conform with § 37.3 is “null and void” under that section, and a tenant can file a free summary petition based on receipt of an invalid notice of rent increase. § 37.3 · sf.gov — Tenant Petitions

Reasons: required, permitted, forbidden

QuestionAnswerSource
Must I give a reason for an annual or banked increase?No official source imposes that duty: the required contents are an exhaustive list ($ amount, %, effective date, which part is banked, and the § 37.3(b) components).sf.gov · § 37.3(b)
When is a justification mandatory?Only for increases that need a petition — capital improvement, operating and maintenance, comparable rents, Proposition I history, utility passthroughs — and the landlord must file before serving the notice.sf.gov
May I explain the arithmetic?Yes, and it is the safest thing to state: “1.4% banked from the 2026 anniversary plus 1.6% current annual allowance.”sf.gov
Never, under any wordingAnything tied to race, color, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, marital status, national origin, ancestry, familial status, source of income, disability, veteran or military status, or genetic information.Gov. Code § 12955 · 42 U.S.C. § 3604
Never: repairs or complaintsA rent increase is prohibited within 180 days of a good-faith repair request or a tenantability complaint, and at any time when the purpose is retaliation for organising or for exercising rights.Civ. Code § 1942.5
Never: the new occupant“A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a unit with the existing tenant. This is an unlawful rent increase, even if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge.”sf.gov
Never: “market rate” as a justificationMarket rent can only be set when a unit is vacant and no original tenant remains; it cannot justify an increase on a tenant in occupancy.sf.gov · § 1947.12(b)

Analysis The defensible reason for these two increases is procedural, and it is the same reason for both units: the Rent Board's published annual allowance plus the allowance that accrued unused in this tenancy. It needs no market argument, and it is the only reason that can be verified from a public document.

Model notice (template — placeholder text)

Use the actual figures and dates. This template contains every element the sources above require and nothing else. It is not a form published by the Rent Board and it is not legal advice.

NOTICE OF RENT INCREASE
[Date of service]

To: [Tenant name(s)]
Address: [street address, unit]

This notice is given under Civil Code section 827 and San Francisco Administrative Code
section 37.3.

1. Your current base rent is $[current].  Effective [effective date], your new base rent
   will be $[new].

2. The increase is $[amount], which is [percent]% of your current base rent.

3. Of that increase:
   - [percent]% ($[amount]) is the annual allowable increase published by the San Francisco
     Rent Board for the period [rate period], applied to the anniversary date of
     [anniversary date].
   - [percent]% ($[amount]) is a previously unused (banked) annual increase from the
     anniversary date(s) of [dates], each at the rate then in effect.

4. No portion of this increase is for operating and maintenance costs, comparable rents,
   capital improvements, rehabilitation, energy conservation measures, or an RAP loan.

5. Pay rent as before: [payment method and address].  If you believe this increase is
   unlawful, you may file a tenant petition with the San Francisco Rent Board. There is no
   charge. Rent Board: 25 Van Ness Avenue, Suite 700, San Francisco CA 94102,
   415-252-4600, rentboard@sfgov.org.

[Owner name and signature]
[Contact information for habitability issues]

If a tenant challenges the increase

What happensDetailSource
Free petition, no deadline“There is no charge for filing the petition.” “There is no time limit for filing a petition.”sf.gov
What the tenant must attachDocuments showing the date and amount of each increase — original lease, notices, receipts, money orders or cancelled checks — i.e. the same history the owner should already hold.sf.gov
HearingUsually within a few months, before a Rent Board Administrative Law Judge; the tenant does not need an attorney; the landlord may be represented.sf.gov
Free legal help for tenantsEviction Defense Collaborative, 415-659-9184, legal@evictiondefense.org, for free legal help; tenant counselling organisations are listed by the City.sf.gov
Matters the Rent Board will not decide“we do not adjudicate issues that are not related to the amount of rent charged, such as harassment, discrimination and retaliation” — those go to court or another forum.sf.gov

Analysis The hearing is won or lost on the rent history, not on the notice. That makes the single most valuable preparation step before serving anything the creation of one file per unit: lease, every previous notice, every proof of service, and the Housing Inventory confirmation and license printout.
