Sources, method and repository review

No figure on this site comes from a blog, a broker page, a news article or a language model. Each one was read in the official source listed below on 7 November 2026, and the passage that supports it is quoted on the page where it is used.

How the verification was done

  1. Open the primary document, not a summary. Every source below was opened in full; where a page quoted a statute, the statute was opened separately and compared.
  2. Require two independent official confirmations for any number that changes what a tenant pays. The 1.6% rate is confirmed by the announcement, the “Learn about rent increases” page and document 571; the 30/90-day notice periods by sf.gov and by Civil Code § 827; the state 8.8% figure by the Attorney General and by the Rent Board.
  3. Recompute every dollar figure to the cent from the official percentages and base rents, additively, with no rounding up. The ledgers on Units & math are the output of that calculation.
  4. Leave a value blank when no official source states it. Four such blanks exist here: ZIP-level market rent (F-5), the 2027–28 allowance (F-3), the federal rent ceiling (none exists), and the subsidy/licence status of the two units (F-8, F-11).
  5. Publish disagreements rather than resolving them silently. The 12-month versus 24-month banking conflict is published as F-1, and the mislabelled worked examples as F-12.
  6. Check the site mechanically. A script in the repository (tools/verify_edition.py) checks every internal link and anchor, confirms that every data row carries a link to an official domain or is explicitly marked blank/analysis, and scans every page for tenant identifiers, the street address and the ZIP code.

This repository already contained four published editions of the same analysis when this edition was built. Each was read, and the figures were re-derived from the sources above rather than copied. The findings:
