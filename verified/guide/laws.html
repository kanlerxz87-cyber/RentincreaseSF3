Local, state and federal law

Three layers touch the decision. Only one of them sets the number. The other two determine what you may not do while setting it.

Local — San Francisco Administrative Code chapter 37

Coverage “Many residential units built on or before June 13, 1979 have both rent control and eviction protection.” Units built later, most single-family homes and condominiums, substantially rehabilitated units and units regulated by another agency are limited or exempt. Building built 1940, two units, not a separately sold house or condominium → covered sf.gov
Annual ceiling 60% of the published CPI change, never more than 7%; the Board publishes it every 1 March. 1.6% for 1 Mar 2026 – 28 Feb 2027. Sets the ceiling for both units § 37.3(a)(1) · sf.gov
License to increase “A landlord who has reported the required information about their rental unit to the Rent Board as set forth in Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose annually a rent increase…” Must exist on the effective date — see the license page § 37.3(a)(1) · sf.gov
Notice contents The landlord must identify in writing which portion of an increase is the annual/banked amount and which portion is operating costs, comparable rents, capital improvements, rehabilitation, energy measures or an RAP loan. Applies to both notices § 37.3(b)
Additional occupants “A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a unit with the existing tenant. This is an unlawful rent increase, even if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge.” Blocks any extra charge for the 1 April 2026 co-tenant on Unit 1 sf.gov
Non-conforming increases “Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this Section shall be null and void.” The downside of getting a notice wrong is the whole increase, not a correction § 37.3
Algorithmic pricing Unlawful to use an “algorithmic device” (revenue management software using non-public competitor data) to set rents or occupancy levels in San Francisco; up to $1,000 per violation, with a private right of action for tenants. No pricing software may be used to choose either figure § 37.10C

State — California

RuleWhat it providesEffect on these unitsSource
Tenant Protection Act (AB 1482), Civ. Code § 1947.12 An owner “shall not, over the course of any 12-month period, increase the gross rental rate… more than 5 percent plus the percentage change in the cost of living, or 10 percent, whichever is lower”, and no more than two increments in 12 months. Would allow 8.8% in this region — but it is displaced here § 1947.12 · oag.ca.gov
Why the state cap does not bind § 1947.12(d)(3) exempts “housing subject to rent or price control through a public entity's valid exercise of its police power… that restricts annual increases in the rental rate to an amount less than that provided in subdivision (a).” 1.6% is less than 8.8%, so the local rule governs § 1947.12(d)(3) · sf.gov
State figure for this region “The maximum rent increase for units under AB 1482 from August 1, 2026 to July 31, 2027 is 8.8% in San Francisco. This does NOT apply for units under the rent increase limitations of the San Francisco Rent Ordinance.” Context only — it is not the ceiling here sf.gov · oag.ca.gov
Notice periods, Civ. Code § 827 30 days if the increase is “10 percent or less”; 90 days if “greater than 10 percent”, measured against the rent charged at any time in the previous 12 months and combined with any other increases in that window. 30 days for Unit 1; 30 or 90 for Unit 2 depending on the amount § 827(b)
Retaliation, Civ. Code § 1942.5 No rent increase, service reduction or eviction “within 180 days” of a good-faith repair request, tenantability complaint, agency complaint, inspection or citation, and no increase for the purpose of retaliating for organising. Check the last 180 days of each unit's repair/complaint history before serving § 1942.5
Housing discrimination, Gov. Code § 12955 Unlawful for an owner to “discriminate against or harass any person because of” a listed protected characteristic, including “source of income” and “familial status”. Controls why the two units may differ, never how much § 12955
Costa-Hawkins Local rent control cannot be applied to housing that is “alienable separate from the title to any other dwelling unit” in the circumstances set out in the statute; the City lists “most single-family homes and condos” as outside rent control. The two flats here are not separately alienable, so no exemption applies § 1954.52 · sf.gov
New tenancy pricing “For a new tenancy in which no tenant from the prior tenancy remains in lawful possession… the owner may establish the initial rental rate” outside the cap; the City states there is “no limit on how much rent a landlord may first charge a tenant when renting an empty unit that is covered by rent control.” Neither unit is vacant — this is not available now § 1947.12(b) · sf.gov

Federal — and what is genuinely not there

RuleWhat it providesEffect on these unitsSource
Fair Housing Act, 42 U.S.C. § 3604 Unlawful “to discriminate against any person in the terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental of a dwelling… because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status, or national origin.” A different rent for a reason that is a protected characteristic is a federal violation 42 U.S.C. § 3604(b)
Where to complain HUD accepts housing discrimination complaints; California's Civil Rights Department enforces the state statute. Both routes are open to a tenant regardless of the Rent Board hud.gov · calcivilrights.ca.gov
Federal rent ceiling Blank — no federal statute in the sources reviewed caps residential rent The ceiling is local; the federal layer constrains the reasons Scope note · 42 U.S.C. ch. 45
Subsidised tenancies Where rent is set by another agency the unit can be exempt; for Housing Choice Voucher and HOPWA tenancies the annual limits apply only if base rent exceeds the Housing Authority's payment standard, and passthroughs are not permitted at all. Unknown for these units — F-11 sf.gov

Analysis The federal layer is why “what is reasonable” is not a pure arithmetic question. Two identical units may lawfully be raised by different amounts, but only for reasons that are not protected characteristics and not retaliatory. That is the whole of the federal test for a rent increase that is otherwise within the local cap.

Eviction protection is a separate question — do not conflate it with the rent ceiling

San Francisco units built on or before 13 June 1979 generally have both rent control and just-cause eviction protection, and “a landlord must have ‘just cause’ to evict their tenants.” A rent increase is not one of the just-cause grounds, and a tenant who disputes an increase has a free route to a hearing at the Rent Board rather than a court. sf.gov — Learn about San Francisco rental laws · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase

This page does not analyse eviction or just cause, which are outside the scope of the questions asked. It records only that the two regimes are administered separately.
