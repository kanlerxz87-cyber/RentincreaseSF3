Is the increase “reasonable and normal”?

This page separates three things that get mixed together: what the law permits, what the market would bear, and what a reviewer would call normal practice. Only the first has an official number.

1. The only official definition of a “normal” increase

QuestionAnswerKind of answerSource
What is the normal annual increase in San Francisco? 1.6% for the rate year 1 Mar 2026 – 28 Feb 2027; 1.4% the year before; 1.7%, 3.6%, 2.3%, 0.7%, 1.8%, 2.6% in the six years before that. Verified doc 571
May an owner charge more than that? Only through a Rent Board petition for a specific cost category — capital improvement, operating and maintenance, comparable rents, Proposition I history, utility passthrough or Rule 1.21. Verified sf.gov
Is banked rent “normal”? Yes — it is expressly part of the ordinance's design: “A landlord that does not increase a tenant's rent on their anniversary date, can save (or ‘bank’) the increase and add it later.” Verified sf.gov · sf.gov
What is the normal increase at this address, or in this ZIP code? Blank — no official source publishes rent-increase levels at ZIP or neighbourhood level Unverified F-5
Can a long-standing below-market rent justify a catch-up on its own? No. “The mere fact that a long-standing tenant is paying significantly less than market rent is not considered an extraordinary circumstance” — a comparable-rent increase needs a Special Circumstances petition and proof of “fraud, mental incompetence or other extraordinary circumstances unrelated to market conditions”. Verified sf.gov
What rent would a comparable unit achieve? Blank — comparable-rent evidence is only relevant inside a Rent Board petition, and none has been filed Not applicable sf.gov

Analysis For a covered unit, “reasonable and normal” is not a market judgement. It is the answer to one question: what has this tenancy accrued? Unit 1 accrued 3.0% in two years; Unit 2 accrued 13.1% in seven. Both are normal; neither is a market rent.

2. How institutional owners and individual owners actually behave

The honest answer is partly a blank: no official source publishes a comparison of rent-increase behaviour by owner size. What the official record does show is mechanism, and it is worth knowing because it is the thing a tenant's advocate will have in mind.

Finding in the official recordWhat it showsRelevance hereSource
San Francisco prohibited algorithmic rent-setting devices in 2024, and its findings describe how large operators priced Owners “pool their data and pricing decisions using such software. By some estimates, this includes owners of as much as 70% of all rental housing in San Francisco.” Use and sale are now unlawful, with civil penalties up to $1,000 per violation and a tenant right of action. Portfolio-wide, data-driven increases by large owners are the pattern the City legislated against — not a model to copy § 37.10C · Ordinance 224-24
Federal antitrust action against six of the largest landlords The amended complaint alleges Greystar, LivCor, Camden, Cushman & Wakefield/Pinnacle, Willow Bridge and Cortland “participated in a scheme to set their rents using each other's competitively sensitive information through common pricing algorithms”; together they operated over 1.3 million units in 43 states. Confirms that large-owner practice is not a safe benchmark of “normal” justice.gov
No size exemption anywhere in the compliance rules The cap, the license, the notice periods and the fair-housing rules apply identically; the only size-specific rule is that buildings with fewer than 10 units started Housing Inventory reporting on 1 March 2023 rather than 1 July 2022. An individual owner is held to exactly the same standard as a corporation sf.gov · § 37.3
The City's listed help for small landlords is counselling and mediation, not software Rent Board counselling for landlords and tenants (415-252-4600), the Bar Association of San Francisco's conflict intervention service, and the San Francisco Apartment Association are the landlord-side resources the City publishes. The supported path for this owner is a phone call and a written file sf.gov · sf.gov

Analysis The practical difference is administrative, not legal: an institution takes the published allowance every year on a calendar and therefore never accumulates a bank; an individual owner skips years and accumulates one. That is precisely what has happened here — and it is why Unit 2's lawful increase is large.

3. One unit or both?

PropositionStatusBasis
Rent-controlled ceilings are calculated tenancy by tenancy Verified The allowance accrues to the tenancy's anniversary date and base rent; banked amounts belong to that tenancy and “do not carry over to a new tenant”. sf.gov
Identical increases across units are legally required No — no such requirement appears in any source reviewed § 37.3, § 1947.12, § 827 and § 12955 each regulate the amount, the notice, or the reason — none requires parity. § 37.3 · § 12955
A difference is unlawful when it is because of a protected characteristic Verified State and federal housing discrimination statutes, including “source of income” and “familial status”. Gov. Code § 12955 · 42 U.S.C. § 3604
A difference is unlawful when it is retaliatory Verified 180-day prohibition after repair requests and tenantability complaints; no increases for organising. Civ. Code § 1942.5
A difference is unlawful when it is a charge for a new occupant Verified “This is an unlawful rent increase, even if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge.” sf.gov
Raising only one unit is, by itself, unlawful No — nothing in the sources makes it unlawful Analysis It is lawful if the reason is documented inputs (rent history, anniversary, amount chosen), not a protected characteristic or retaliation.
The recommendation. Raise both, on one written rule, on each unit's own anniversary. Unit 1 at 3.0% and Unit 2 at 10.0% look different to a reader, but they are the same rule: “the published annual allowance plus the allowance this tenancy accrued”. Give both notices the same form, the same explanatory sentence and the same date discipline. That single sentence is the answer to “why is my neighbour paying less?” — and it is true, which is what makes it survive review.

If only one unit is to be raised, document the reason in the file before serving anything, and check the last 180 days for repair requests or complaints in the unit that is not being raised as well as the one that is.

5. Written owner policy (one paragraph, applied to both units)

“The owner imposes the annual allowable increase published by the San Francisco Rent Board on each tenancy's rent increase anniversary date, together with any annual allowance that accrued unused in that tenancy and remained available under the Rent Board's banking rules. The increase is calculated on base rent only, is not compounded, and is not rounded up. A written notice stating the amount, the percentage, the effective date and the banked portion is served within the period required by Civil Code section 827, and the unit is reported in the Rent Board Housing Inventory with a current rent increase license before the increase takes effect. No increase is based on the number of occupants, on a request for repairs, or on any characteristic protected by law.”

Every sentence in that paragraph maps to a source on the laws page or the license page. Adopting it as written, dated and signed is what turns two separate decisions into one rule.
