L01 Coverage

Rent control applies to most residential units built on or before June 13, 1979.

Rent Board: "Many residential units built on or before June 13, 1979 have both rent control and eviction protection." Units built after that date, most single-family homes and condos, substantially rehabilitated units and certain subsidized units do not have rent control.

L02 Allowable increase

The allowable annual rent increase for 3/1/2026 – 2/28/2027 is 1.6% of base rent.

Rent Board document 571: "Effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027, the allowable annual increase amount is 1.6%." Multiply base rent by .016.

L03 Allowable increase

The prior period allowance was 1.4% (3/1/2025 – 2/28/2026).

Rent Board document 571 table: 3/1/25 – 2/28/26 = 1.4%. 3/1/24 – 2/28/25 = 1.7%. 3/1/23 – 2/29/24 = 3.6%.

 1.4% Verified 571 — Allowable Annual Rent Increases (3/1/26 – 2/28/27)www.sf.gov · primary source
L04 Formula

The annual allowance is 60% of the published CPI increase, and can never exceed 7%.

Rent Ordinance sec. 37.3(a)(1): a licensed landlord "shall have a license to impose annually a rent increase which does not exceed a tenant's base rent by more than 60% of said published increase. In no event, however, shall the allowable annual increase be greater than 7%."

 60% of CPI, 7% ceiling Verified Sec. 37.3 — Rent Limitations (full text)www.sf.gov · primary source
L05 Frequency

Rent may be increased only once every 12 months, measured from the anniversary date.

Rent Board: "a landlord can only increase a tenant's rent by a certain percentage once every 12 months." The first annual increase may be imposed 12 months after the lease began.

 Once per 12 months Verified Learn about rent increases in San Franciscowww.sf.gov · official summary
L06 Base rent

The increase is computed on base rent only, and may not be rounded up.

"Landlords must calculate the rent increase on the tenant's base rent only. This includes any housing services like parking or storage. It does not include any temporary passthroughs or fluctuating charges. Rent increases cannot be rounded up to the nearest dollar."

 Base rent only; no rounding up Verified Learn about rent increases in San Franciscowww.sf.gov · official summary
L07 License prerequisite

A landlord must hold a Rent Board rent increase license before an annual or banked increase takes effect.

Ordinance sec. 37.15(e): a landlord who has substantially complied with Housing Inventory reporting "shall receive a license to impose rent increases"; if not, the licence is suspended and "a tenant shall not be obligated to pay the increased rent for months during the period of suspension."

 License required Verified Sec. 37.15 — Reporting Obligations; Licensing (full text)www.sf.gov · primary source
L08 Notice period

30 days' written notice is required; 90 days if the increase is more than 10%; add 5 days if the notice is mailed.

Rent Board: "30-day written notice" and "90-day written notice if the increase (either by itself or combined with another increase in the same year) is more than 10%." "If the notice is mailed, the landlord must give the tenant 5 more days."

 30 days / 90 days above 10% Verified Learn about rent increases in San Franciscowww.sf.gov · official summary
L09 Notice content

The notice must state the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date, and which part is banked.

Rent Board lists the required contents and adds: "The landlord should use the percentage that will be in effect on the date of the increase, not the percentage in effect on the date they serve the notice."

 4 required elements Verified Learn about rent increases in San Franciscowww.sf.gov · official summary
L10 Consequence of defect

An increase that does not comply is null and void in its entirety.

Rule 4.10(b): "Any rent increase which does not conform with the provisions of this Section shall render the entire rent increase null and void", except where the overage is no more than 0.5% of prior base rent and given in good faith.

 Null and void Verified Rules and Regulations Part IV — Rent Increases Not Requiring Approval (HTML, dated Dec 13, 2022)www.sf.gov · primary source
L11 Banking

Unused annual increases may be accumulated and imposed later, are not compounded, and are not rounded up.

Ordinance sec. 37.3(a)(2) and Rule 4.12: a landlord who refrains from imposing an increase may accumulate it; only increases available on or after April 1, 1982 may be banked; "Banked increases shall not be compounded and shall not be rounded up."

 Unlimited bank since 4/1/1982 Verified 625 — Complete Rent Board Rules and Regulations (amended 1/13/2026)www.sf.gov · primary source
L12 Banking arithmetic

Banked percentages are added together, then applied once to current base rent.

Rent Board worked example: tenant moved in 8/1/2022 at $1,300; landlord first raises rent on 8/1/2025. Banked 3.6% + 1.7% = 5.3%, plus 1.4% annual = 6.7%; $1,300 x .067 = $87.10.

 Add percentages, then apply Verified Banked rent increaseswww.sf.gov · official summary
L13 Banking threshold — CONFLICT

Two official Rent Board pages state different waiting periods before a skipped increase may be banked.

Rule 4.12 (the adopted regulation, doc 625, amended through 1/13/2026): "A full 12 months must have elapsed from the date that an annual rent increase, or a portion thereof, could have been imposed before this banking section becomes applicable." The Rent Board's plain-language page says: "A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase."

 12 months vs 24 months UnverifiedFlag 625 — Complete Rent Board Rules and Regulations (amended 1/13/2026)www.sf.gov · primary source
L14 Multi-year increase

Where no increase has been imposed for two or more years, the allowable percentages for the intervening years may be added.

Rent Board: "landlords may increase rent by adding up the allowable increases during that time period"; work 8.2% = 2.2 + 1.6 + 2.6 + 1.8. "Increases cannot be compounded." No petition is required.

 Add, do not compound Verified Annual rent increase for multiple yearswww.sf.gov · official summary
L15 Additional occupants

Adding a roommate or occupant is not a lawful basis for a rent increase.

Rent Board: "A landlord cannot charge additional rent simply because a new (or additional) roommate has moved into a unit with the existing tenant. This is an unlawful rent increase, even if the lease or rental agreement provides for the additional charge." Ordinance sec. 37.3(a)(11) says the same.

 No increase for occupants Verified Sec. 37.3 — Rent Limitations (full text)www.sf.gov · primary source
L16 Above-cap increases

Any increase above the allowable annual and banked amount requires a Rent Board petition.

Six increase types require a petition before the notice is served: capital improvement passthrough; operating and maintenance expense; special circumstances / comparable rents; Prop I past rent history; utility passthrough; Rule 1.21 increase. Tenants need not pay until the Board approves, and approval is retroactive to a valid notice date.

 6 petition types Verified Landlord Petitions and Passthroughs (the six increase types that need a petition)www.sf.gov · official summary
L17 Tenant challenge

A tenant may petition at any time, at no charge, to have the lawful rent determined.

Form 516C with Form C. "There is no time limit for filing a petition" and "There is no charge for filing the petition." A 518 Tenant Summary Petition exists for a notice that is "clearly unlawful on its face" and usually triggers a corrected notice without a hearing.

 No fee, no deadline Verified Report an unlawful rent increase or request a review of your rent historywww.sf.gov · official summary
L18 Hardship

Tenants cannot get hardship deferral of an annual or banked increase.

Hardship relief is limited to capital improvement passthroughs, water revenue bond passthroughs, general obligation bond passthroughs, utility passthroughs and O&M increases. "For all other types of rent increases or passthroughs, including annual and banked rent increases ... financial hardship relief is not available."

 Not available for annual/banked Verified Tenant Financial Hardship Applicationswww.sf.gov · official summary
L19 Harassment

A bad-faith rent increase intended to make a tenant vacate is tenant harassment.

Ordinance sec. 37.10B(a)(5) covers influencing a tenant to vacate "through fraud, intimidation or coercion", including a rent increase intended to coerce; the section lists a rent increase "substantially in excess of market rates for comparable units" as evidence. Remedies include treble damages, statutory damages of $1,000, and attorney's fees.

 Treble damages + fees Verified Sec. 37.10B — Tenant Harassment (full text)www.sf.gov · primary source
L20 Civil action

A tenant may sue over excess rent, and the prevailing party recovers attorney's fees.

Ordinance sec. 37.11A(a): where a landlord charges rent above the Chapter 37 limits, the tenant may seek injunctive relief and damages, and "in cases where the landlord has charged an excessive rent in violation of Section 37.9B(a)", damages of not less than three times the excess. Sec. 37.11A(c): "The prevailing party ... shall be entitled to recover reasonable attorneys' fees and costs."

 Fees to prevailing party Verified Sec. 37.11A — Civil Actions (full text)www.sf.gov · primary source
L21 Algorithmic pricing

San Francisco forbids landlords from using algorithmic devices to set rents.

Ordinance sec. 37.10C (added by Ord. 224-24, effective 10/14/2024; amended 10/6/2025) makes it unlawful to sell or use revenue-management software that calculates non-public competitor data to recommend rents or occupancy. Each month and each unit is a separate violation; penalties up to $1,000 per violation plus fees.

 Prohibited since 10/14/2024 Verified Sec. 37.10C — Use and Sale of Algorithmic Devices Prohibited (full text)www.sf.gov · primary source
L22 Rent Board fee

The 2026-27 Rent Board fee is $59.00 per dwelling unit, and up to 50% may be recovered from a qualifying tenant.

"For the 2026-2027 tax year, the fee is $59.00 per dwelling unit ... If you pay the Rent Board fee in full, you may be able to collect 50% of the fee from your tenants, which is $29.50 per dwelling unit." Due March 1; 5% penalty per month up to 15%.

 $59.00 / $29.50 Verified The Rent Board Fee ($59/dwelling unit for 2026-27)www.sf.gov · official summary
L23 Fee recovery conditions

The fee may only be billed to a tenant who was in occupancy on or before November 1 of the prior year, and not as a rent increase.

Recover by deducting from security deposit interest due, or by separate bill (only if no or insufficient deposit). The bill must state the amount, that its purpose is to fund the Rent Board, and that it is due within 30 days. Non-payment of the fee is not just cause for eviction.

L24 6.14 / Costa-Hawkins

An unlimited increase on remaining occupants is available only when the last original occupant has vacated.

Rule 6.14 allows an unlimited increase "when the last original occupant vacates the unit", provided a written 6.14 notice was timely served on each subsequent occupant (generally within 60 days). A co-tenant — as opposed to a subtenant — is not subject to a Costa-Hawkins market-rate increase regardless of when the co-tenant moved in.

 Only after last original tenant leaves Verified Rent Increases Under Section 6.14 and Costa-Hawkinswww.sf.gov · official summary
L25 Tenant organisation

Landlords must meet and confer in good faith with a certified Tenant Association, including about rent increases; a 2-unit building cannot certify one.

Admin Code ch. 49A: a Tenant Association requires "a building with five or more rental units" and a petition signed by tenants of at least 50% of occupied units. Landlords "shall confer ... in good faith regarding ... rent increases".
