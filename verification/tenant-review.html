Would this survive a tenant's lawyer?

A stress test, not a prediction. Each check below is pass/fail against a published rule, and the open items are the only places where the answer could change.

How this page is meant to be used

San Francisco tenants can obtain free advice and representation, and the Rent Board charges nothing to file a petition. That means any increase should be assumed to be read by someone whose job is to find the defect. The checks below are the ones that decide the outcome; each is either satisfied, or flagged as an open item on this project.

One unit or both — the discrimination question

What the sources do and do not say
QuestionVerified positionSource
Is there a rule requiring both units to be raised together?No official source imposes one. There is no requirement to raise rent at all, and no requirement of uniformity across tenancies.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF · Gov. Code §12955 — FEHA housing discrimination (leginfo)
Is raising only one unit unlawful by itself?Not on the face of the discrimination statutes. FEHA and the Fair Housing Act prohibit differential treatment because of a protected characteristic, not differential treatment as such. A lawful, documented, objective basis is the dividing line.Gov. Code §12955 — FEHA housing discrimination (leginfo) · 42 U.S.C. §3604 (govinfo)
What creates exposure?Choosing between tenancies on a ground that correlates with a protected characteristic — source of income, familial status, disability, national origin, marital status — or timing that coincides with a complaint, a repair request or organising.Gov. Code §12955 — FEHA housing discrimination (leginfo) · Civ. Code §1942.5 (leginfo)
What does the reviewer see?Two tenancies with different ceilings because they accrued different banks. Applying each unit's own lawful ceiling on its own anniversary is defensible and explainable; applying an identical percentage to both would in fact be less accurate here, because Unit 1 has 3.0% available and Unit 2 has far more.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Form 571, Allowable Annual Increases 26–27 (PDF)
What should be written down?A single written policy: the anniversary date rule, the rate-year rule, the banking method, the notice template, and the record-keeping. Then apply it to both units without exception, and keep the file.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Tenant Petitions

Where a lawyer would look hardest — and the current answer

Findings specific to these facts
IssueFindingSource
The April 2026 additional occupant in Unit 1Cannot support any charge. The prohibition applies "notwithstanding a rental agreement or lease that specifically permits a rent increase for additional occupants." The only lawful increase is the allowance plus banks.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF · S.F. Admin. Code §37.3 (American Legal Publishing)
Was Unit 1 reset by the April 2026 continuation?No. A continuing tenancy with an added occupant is not a new tenancy, so the 1 February anniversary and the $3,995.00 base rent stand.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF
Could Unit 1 be reset to market if the remaining original tenant leaves?Not automatically. The narrower unlimited-increase rule requires that the last original occupant has permanently vacated, applies to subtenants rather than co-tenants, and depends on a timely §6.14 notice. Whether the second occupant is a subtenant or a co-tenant is not established on the record. F-19sf.gov — Rent increases under §6.14 and Costa-Hawkins · Civ. Code §1954.53 — Costa-Hawkins (leginfo)
Is a 13.1% Unit 2 step "normal business practice"?It is lawful today but it is a seven-year catch-up, and the 90-day notice exists because increases above 10% are treated as materially different. The pending ordinance targets exactly this. A phased 10.0% step of $315.00 is the more defensible first move.Civ. Code §827 (leginfo) · sfgov.legistar.com — File #261020
Security deposit interestA separate annual obligation: 4.2% for 1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027. It is not a rent increase and does not consume an allowance.sf.gov — Current Rates (rent increase, relocation, deposit)
What the Rent Board will not decideHarassment, discrimination, retaliation and security deposits are outside its jurisdiction — those are court or agency matters, which is why a defensible increase should avoid the appearance of any of them.sf.gov — Tenant Petitions
