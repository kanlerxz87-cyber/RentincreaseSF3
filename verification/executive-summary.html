Executive summary

The rent-increase licence question first — because a correct percentage is worthless without it — then the lawful amount for each unit, the notice that carries it, and the checks a tenant's lawyer would run.

1. The question that has to be answered first

Do I need to inform the Rent Board and get a licence before notifying tenants, even though I am an individual owner and not a corporation?

Yes — and the answer does not depend on being a company. The ordinance ties the obligation to building size, not to the owner's legal form: buildings with 10 or more units from 1 July 2022, and condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units from 1 March 2023. This is a two-unit building, so the March 2023 date applies. Every owner must report into the Housing Inventory each year; reporting a tenant-occupied unit produces the rent-increase licence automatically. The licence must be in effect on the effective date of the increase. If it is not, the increase may be null and void and the tenant may recover the difference.

What you do not have to do: file the increase notice with the Rent Board, apply for approval of the increase, or petition for an annual or banked increase. The licence is a precondition, not an application about the amount.

5. Bottom line

  • Yes, the licence is required, and no owner type is exempt. File the Housing Inventory, confirm the licence covers the effective date, and only then serve notice.
  • Unit 1: the defensible step on 1 February 2027 is 3.0% = $119.85 → $4,114.85, composed of the unused 1.4% and the current 1.6%, with 30 days' notice. A 1.6%-only step of $63.92 is also lawful and leaves the 1.4% available.
  • Unit 2: $412.65 (13.1%) is the verified banked balance and would need 90 days' notice; the June 2027 ceiling is higher because the 2027-28 allowance is unpublished. A 10.0% step of $315.00 is lawful on 30 days' notice and stays inside the pending 10% cap.
  • Do both units under one written policy, each on its own anniversary, with the same method, the same notice template and the same documentation. Treating them differently is not automatically unlawful, but it is the fact pattern that prompts inquiries.
  • Open items that block certainty, not the plan: Unit 2's rent history, this building's licence status, and the owner's own record of whether Unit 1's 1.4% year was ever charged. See Flagged.
