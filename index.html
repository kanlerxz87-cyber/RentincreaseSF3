Rental increase review — dashboard

Two rent-controlled flats in a 1940 two-unit San Francisco building. Two questions drive everything else: does an individual owner need the Rent Board's rent-increase licence (yes — see below), and what amount is lawful for each unit. Answers are verified line by line against official sources and linked in every row.

Forefront answer: yes — an individual owner must report to the Rent Board and hold a rent-increase licence

Being a single individual owner, rather than a corporation, makes no difference. San Francisco requires all owners of residential property to report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory; reporting a tenant-occupied unit generates a rent-increase licence. That licence must be on file on the effective date of any annual or banked increase — not merely when the notice is served. Without it the increase may be null and void, and the tenant may claim a refund.

The licence is not an approval of your increase, and you do not file the notice with the Rent Board. It is a gate: file the Housing Inventory first, confirm the licence, then serve the notice. Doing it the other way round is the single most common way a correctly calculated increase is overturned.

1 · File
Housing Inventory report, every year, due 1 March (free, online)
2 · Confirm
Licence auto-issued for tenant-occupied units; check it in the Portal
3 · Check the date
The licence must be in effect on the effective date of the increase
4 · Then notice
Written notice, 30 or 90 days, served before the increase

Sources: sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Banked rent increases

Do I have to tell the Rent Board and hold a rent-increase licence — as an individual, non-corporate owner?Yes. Every owner of residential property must report into the Housing Inventory, and a current rent-increase licence must be on file before an annual or banked increase takes effect. The law distinguishes by building size (10+ units from 1 Jul 2022; 1–9 units and condominiums from 1 Mar 2023), not by whether the owner is a person or a company.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory
Is the licence an application the Rent Board approves for each increase?No. It is generated automatically when you report a tenant-occupied unit. There is no per-increase filing and no petition for an annual or banked increase — but the licence must exist on the effective date.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Annual rent increase for multiple years
What happens if the licence is missing on the effective date?The increase may be null and void, possibly for several years, and the tenant may be entitled to a refund. There is no charge and no time limit for a tenant to petition.sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review rent history
What is a lawful, normal increase for these units?The Rent Board's published allowance — 1.6% for an increase effective 1 Mar 2026 – 28 Feb 2027 — plus any correctly banked years. A larger amount is not available at the owner's discretion: it requires a Rent Board petition (capital improvement, operating and maintenance, comparable rents) or the narrow unlimited-increase rule that applies only once the last original occupant has permanently vacated. Banks are added, never compounded, and may not be rounded up.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Does the state cap (AB 1482) or federal law set a higher ceiling I could use?No. AB 1482 exempts housing that is already subject to a stricter local ordinance; the SF allowance is lower than the state 8.8% for 1 Aug 2026 – 31 Jul 2027. No federal statute caps rent; federal law governs reasons (fair housing).Civ. Code §1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act (leginfo) · oag.ca.gov — Limits on Rent Increases · 42 U.S.C. §3604 (govinfo)
Unit 1 — what can be imposed, and when?One increase per 12 months. The 1.4% allowance for the 1 Feb 2026 anniversary was not used; it banks once 24 full months pass without an increase, so on 1 Feb 2027 the ceiling is 3.0% = $119.85 → $4,114.85, with 30 days' notice. The 1 Apr 2026 continuation did not reset the anniversary.sf.gov — Banked rent increases · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF
Unit 2 — what can be imposed, and when?If no increase has been taken since 1 Jun 2019, seven banked years total 13.1% = $412.65. A 13.1% step exceeds 10%, so it needs 90 days' notice. The next anniversary is 1 Jun 2027, and the allowance then in effect is not yet published — so the true June 2027 ceiling is higher than 13.1% and cannot be stated yet.sf.gov — Form 571, Allowable Annual Increases 26–27 (PDF) · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Would a San Francisco tenant lawyer be able to overturn it?Only if a step is missed: no licence on the effective date, more than the allowance plus documented banks, a second increase inside 12 months, a base-rent error, a notice missing a required element, a retaliatory motive, or a protected-class reason. Each is a pass/fail check on the tenant-lawyer page.sf.gov — Tenant Petitions · Civ. Code §827 (leginfo)
Should I raise both units or only one?There is no legal requirement to raise both, and raising one is not unlawful by itself. The risk is an inference of differential treatment: applying one written policy to both units, each on its own anniversary, is the most defensible posture. Document any objective reason if you treat them differently.Gov. Code §12955 — FEHA housing discrimination (leginfo) · 42 U.S.C. §3604 (govinfo)
Anything pending that could change this?Board of Supervisors File #261020 would cap combined banked + capital-improvement increases at 10% a year for three years. Status: Pending Committee Action — not law. A banked notice above 10% should be re-checked before service.sfgov.legistar.com — File #261020 · sf.gov — Banked rent increases

Two corrections to earlier drafts of this analysis

  1. Unit 2's ceiling is not 13.1% on 1 June 2027. 13.1% is the sum of the seven allowances that accrued from 1 June 2020 to 1 June 2026, which is a verified floor of $412.65 ($3,562.65). The increase on 1 June 2027 also includes the allowance in effect on that date (the 1 Mar 2027 – 29 Feb 2028 rate), which the Rent Board has not yet published, so the full June 2027 figure cannot be stated. F-11 Details · sf.gov — Form 571, Allowable Annual Increases 26–27 (PDF) · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
  2. Repeating a rate year in a later notice overstates the ceiling. The increase takes the allowance in effect on its effective date — plus unused earlier years — so a notice served today for an effective date after 28 February 2027 must use the 2027-28 rate, not 1.6%. F-12 Details · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in SF
