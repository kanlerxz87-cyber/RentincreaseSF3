Executive summary

Yes — individual owners need the rent increase license too.

Being an individual rather than a corporation does not remove the Housing Inventory requirement. For rent-controlled units, report to the Rent Board Housing Inventory; a license is automatically generated for non-owner-occupied units. It must be current on the effective date of an annual or banked increase. Reporting is due March 1 every year; late reporting remains possible.

Obtain and check the license before serving notice as a practical safeguard. The official enforceability deadline is the effective date, not the service date. The license need not accompany the notice. Housing Inventory reporting is not a petition to approve each ordinary annual increase; the annual rate sheet says no petition is needed, and the banking page says banked increases do not require approval.

Housing Inventory / license · Official Form 571 · Rent Board banking

No guarantee against challenges. A license alone does not validate the base rent, calculation, timing, service, lease terms, fair-housing compliance or retaliation context. Missing property records remain unresolved; this site is not authorization to serve a notice.

Recommended sequence

  1. Confirm coverage, lease restrictions, lawful base rent and increase history.
  2. Report Housing Inventory and check the generated license for each unit.
  3. Select a lawful, neutral increase and effective date; verify notice/service timing.
  4. Retain the calculation, notice, service evidence and license. Use the tenant-lawyer checklist before acting.
