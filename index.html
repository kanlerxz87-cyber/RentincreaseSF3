⚠️ Forefront: Do I need to inform the Rent Board and get a license — even as an individual owner?

Yes. Every owner must. San Francisco requires all owners of residential property — individual persons, not just corporations — to report to the Rent Board Housing Inventory and hold a current rent-increase license on the effective date of any annual or banked increase. Without it, the increase is null and void and a tenant can get it overturned and refunded.

QuestionVerified answerOfficial source
Must I inform the Rent Board before raising rent?Yes — you must report to the Housing Inventory first. All residential-property owners must report annually, even if not currently a landlord in that unit. Reporting a tenant-occupied unit automatically generates the rent-increase license.sf.gov — Housing Inventory
Do I need a license as an individual (not a corporation)?Yes — same rule for everyone. The ordinance distinguishes by building size (10+ units vs. 1–9 units / condos), not by whether the owner is a person or a corporation.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases
When did this start?July 1, 2022 for buildings with 10+ residential units; March 1, 2023 for condos and buildings with 1–9 units (this building: 2 units → March 1, 2023). Applies every year thereafter.sf.gov — Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Ordinance 265-20 summary
What happens if I skip it?The annual/banked increase is null and void for as long as no license was on file on its effective date. Tenants may file an Unlawful Rent Increase Petition for a refund; the Rent Board may notify tenants of noncompliance.sf.gov — Notice of Noncompliance · sf.gov — Housing Inventory
Does correct math save me?No. Even a perfectly calculated 1.4% or 1.6% is void without the license.sf.gov

Step-by-step — what to do before you notify tenants

StepWhat to doOfficial source / how to verify
1 Confirm coverageVerify the building is rent-controlled (1940 building with pre-June 13, 1979 certificate of occupancy, 2 units → presumptively covered). Call Rent Board counselors 415-252-4600.sf.gov — SF rental laws
2 File Housing InventoryReport every residential unit to the Rent Board Housing Inventory via the Rent Board Portal — due March 1 every year. Provide owner/manager contact, business registration if any, square footage, bedrooms/bathrooms, occupancy/vacancy dates, and base rent in $250 increments + utility info for tenant-occupied units. Under penalty of perjury.sf.gov — Housing Inventory · Portal: portal.sfrb.org
3 Confirm the licenseAfter reporting a tenant-occupied unit, a rent-increase license is automatically generated — no separate application. Check the Portal (search the property) to confirm a current license is on file and when it expires. It must be in effect on the effective date of the increase, not just the notice date.sf.gov — Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Banked increases
4 Calculate correctlyMultiply base rent only (incl. housing services like parking/storage; excl. passthroughs) by the allowable % in effect on the effective date: 1.4% (3/1/2025–2/28/2026) or 1.6% (3/1/2026–2/28/2027). No rounding up. One increase per 12 months; anniversary date moves if you delay past 12 months. Banked amounts are additive, not compounded.sf.gov — rent increases · Form 571 PDF · sf.gov — 1.6% announcement
5 Serve written noticeWritten notice only. 30 days if the increase ≤10% (alone or combined with others in same 12 months); 90 days if >10%. +5 days if mailed within California. Notice must state: dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and for banked increases — which part is banked and from which years. Use the rate in effect on the effective date.sf.gov — Giving notice · Civ. Code §827 · CCP §1013 · sf.gov — Banked increases
6 Keep proofKeep copy of notice + proof of service (personal delivery receipt or certified-mail tracking). If mailed, document the +5-day calculation. File the Housing Inventory confirmation alongside.sf.gov · CCP §1013
7 Update annuallyRe-file Housing Inventory every March 1 and keep contact info current (update within 30 days of change). Tenants can check your license status in the Portal; the Rent Board may notify them if you are unlicensed.sf.gov — Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Noncompliance

TL;DR: File → confirm license → then notice tenants. Reversing the order (noticing first, filing after) invalidates the increase. This is the most common reason lawful-percentage increases get overturned.

Quick answers (sourced)

QuestionVerified answerSource
What is a reasonable and normal increase for these rent-controlled units?Only the Rent Board's annual allowable increase: 1.4% (3/1/2025–2/28/2026) or 1.6% (3/1/2026–2/28/2027), once per 12 months, on base rent only. Larger amounts only via banked increases or a Rent Board petition (capital improvements etc.).sf.gov · sf.gov — 1.6% announcement · Form 571 PDF
Does state law (AB 1482) control instead?No. AB 1482 caps at 5% + CPI, max 10% (SF area: 8.8% for Aug 1, 2026–Jul 31, 2027), but expressly exempts housing already under a stricter local rent-control ordinance. These 1940 units are under the local ordinance, so the local 1.4%/1.6% governs.sf.gov — AB 1482 · Civ. Code §1947.12
How do big corporations vs. individual owners handle increases?Same legal ceiling. Both must follow the SF cap, license, and notice rules. Corporations apply the published increase portfolio-wide on a calendar, track banking in software, and keep licenses current; individuals often skip years informally and lose banking records. No official dataset compares their rates — flagged as unverified.sf.gov · sf.gov — banking
Correct way to inform tenants & reasonable reasons?Written notice (30/90 days, +5 mailed) stating dollar, percent, effective date, and which part is banked. No reason is legally required for an annual/banked increase; reasons are required only for petitions (capital improvements, operating costs). If you state a reason, it must be neutral — never reference a protected characteristic, a complaint, or an additional occupant.sf.gov · sf.gov — banking · Civ. Code §827
Would a SF tenant lawyer find this reasonable?Yes, if: (1) license current, (2) amount ≤ allowable + documented banking, (3) one per 12 months, (4) base-rent calc, no rounding up, (5) compliant written notice, (6) applied uniformly. Any deviation invites a free Tenant Petition (e.g., unlawful rent increase) — tenants get free legal help.sf.gov — Tenant Petitions · sf.gov — banking
Raise one unit or both?Both — same formula, same schedule. Raising only one unit is not per se illegal, but differential treatment invites fair-housing/discrimination inquiries. A uniform written policy applied to both units (each on its own anniversary) is the lowest-risk, lawyer-proof posture.sf.gov — discrimination → HRC · hud.gov
