Two hard gates must be cleared before ANY notice is served

1. The rent increase license. Under Rent Ordinance Sec. 37.15 a current license must be on file on the effective date or the entire annual or banked increase is null and void — correct arithmetic does not save it. License status for both units is unknown and is left blank. Source: S8 · S9 · S6

2. Two official sf.gov pages disagree about banking. One says a skipped increase needs ‘at least 24 full months’ to bank; the controlling regulation, Rules and Regulations Sec. 4.12(a), says ‘a full 12 months’. This project applies the regulation and reproduces all four official worked examples exactly, but confirm with a Rent Board counselor first. Source: S5 · S4 · S3

Answers to the five questions asked

1. What is reasonable and normal?

Local: the published annual allowable increase (1.6% now; 1.96% average since 2019; 2.36% since 1982). State: AB 1482 would allow 8.8% but is displaced by the lower local cap. Federal: no ceiling exists.

Governing law

2. Corporate vs individual owners

Same ceiling for both. Corporations differ in three verifiable ways: no single-family exemption, size-phased licensing dates, and a flat ban on algorithmic rent-setting in SF (Sec. 37.10C).

Practice & risk

3. How to notify, and on what grounds

A written notice stating the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and which part is banked; 30 days, or 90 if the total exceeds 10%; plus 5 if mailed. Legitimate grounds are a closed list.

Practice & risk

4. Would it survive a tenant lawyer?

Tenants have free counsel and a free Rent Board petition. Fifteen attack vectors are modelled with the owner's position, residual risk and mitigation for each.

Practice & risk

5. Raise one unit, or both?

Raising only one is lawful here; forcing the same percentage on both would be UNLAWFUL because it would exceed Unit A's ceiling. The safe course is one written policy applied to both, each to its own limit.
