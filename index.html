Bottom line

On the owner's description of the building (1940, two rented units), both units sit under the San Francisco Rent Ordinance, so the ceiling is not "market rent" and not the state's 8.8% cap: it is the Rent Board's published annual increase, plus any increases the owner skipped for at least 24 months.

The two units differ because their tenancies started six years apart. That difference is a lawful, documentable reason for different dollar increases - and it is also the reason to apply one identical rule to both, in writing.

Current rent roll: $7,145.00/month. Under the recommended options: $7,579.85/month (+$434.85).

Seven findings

  1. The local cap governs, not the state cap. AB 1482 exempts housing already limited by a stricter local ordinance; the state ceiling for this region would be 8.8% for increases starting between 1 Aug 2026 and 31 Jul 2027. S16 S21
  2. The current lawful annual increase is 1.6%. On $3,995 that is $63.92; on $3,150 it is $50.40. S1 S4
  3. Unit A has a fresh tenancy. Because the lease began 1 Feb 2025, the first increase could not be imposed before 1 Feb 2026, and nothing was imposed - so one 1.4% year is now available to add to the 1.6%: 3.0%, or $119.85. S2 S3
  4. The April 2026 co-tenant adds nothing. San Francisco prohibits extra rent solely because an additional occupant moved in, even where the lease says otherwise. S2 S13
  5. Unit B has banked seven years. 1.8 + 0.7 + 2.3 + 3.6 + 1.7 + 1.4 + 1.6 = 13.1%, i.e. $412.65, but that is a large single step and it crosses the 10% threshold for longer notice. S3 S4 S15
  6. Process decides enforceability. Licence, 12-month spacing, correct notice length, and a notice that breaks out the banked portion by year are all prerequisites, not formalities. S6 S14
  7. The timing window matters. Any increase that relies on the 1.6% rate must take effect on or before 28 Feb 2027; 90 days' notice means Unit B must be served by 30 Nov 2026 at the latest. S15 S1

Required steps before any notice is served

  1. Confirm coverage. Pull the property record (year built, unit count, use) and keep a printout. Rent control generally attaches to units built on or before 13 June 1979 in a multi-unit building. S5
  2. Check the rent increase license. File or refresh the Housing Inventory report; the increase is unenforceable without a current license on its effective date. S6
  3. Rebuild the lawful rent history. Original lease, every prior increase notice, proof of service, and the rent actually charged. Do not rely on memory. S11
  4. Decide the amount. Either the published annual increase, or the banked total if at least 24 full months have passed without an increase. S3 S8
  5. Serve the right notice, early enough. 30 days for 10% or less; 90 days for more than 10%; 5 extra days if mailed. Include the banked breakdown. S15 S13 S14
  6. Apply the same rule to both units and write down the criteria before serving anything. S19 S20 S18
  7. Keep the file. Any tenant may petition the Rent Board at no charge and with no time limit. S10 S11
