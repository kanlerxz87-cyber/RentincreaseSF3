1. First question: is the Rent Board duty a state law or a local law?

It is a local law. The duty to report to the Rent Board and hold a rent-increase licence comes from San Francisco Ordinance No. 265-20 (File 201262, passed 8 December 2020), which added Administrative Code § 37.15 to the San Francisco Administrative Code. The “2023” date is only the commencement of reporting for condominiums and buildings with fewer than 10 units. No state statute imposes this. Verified 13 November 2026.

2. Do I have to inform the Rent Board and get a licence — as an individual owner, not a corporation?

Yes — the same rule applies to an individual owner. San Francisco requires all owners of residential property to report their units into the Rent Board Housing Inventory; for a tenant-occupied unit the report generates a rent-increase licence, and the licence must be in effect on the effective date of the increase. Annual and banked increases do not require a petition for approval; whether any separate notice-copy filing applies is not established by those sources (FR-20).

Your questionVerified answerOfficial source
Do I need to tell / report to the Rent Board?Yes. “All owners of residential property are required to report” into the Housing Inventory, online through the Rent Board Portal. Reporting is free.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Do I need a licence even though I am a person, not a corporation?Yes. The rules distinguish by building size (10+ units vs. condominiums and 1–9 unit buildings), not by the owner's legal form. “Property owners who have tenants will receive a rent increase license after the submission.” The total number of units in the building is unverified. For buildings with fewer than 10 units and condominiums reporting began 1 March 2023; for other buildings with 10 or more units it began 1 July 2022.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
Is there a separate application or fee for the licence?No separate application. “Once you have reported into the Housing Inventory, a rent increase license will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units.” Reporting is free and the licence “does not need to be included with your rent increase notice”.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Do I have to file the rent-increase notice with the Rent Board?For ordinary annual and banked increases the cited guidance requires no approval petition. It does not explicitly address filing a copy of the tenant notice; confirm any separate filing requirement with the Rent Board. Other increases can have different rules.sf.gov — Form 571, allowable annual increases 3/1/26–2/28/27 (PDF) · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
What happens if I serve the notice without a current licence?Two official consequences. The Housing Inventory page: “your rent increase notice will not be enforceable without having a rent increase license on the effective date of the notice”. The noncompliance page: a notice that takes effect while the unit is unlicensed “may be null and void (this may apply for multiple years)”, the tenant “may be entitled to a rent refund”, and the tenant can challenge it by Tenant Petition (Form 516C) or — if the increase has not yet taken effect — by Tenant Summary Petition (Form 518).sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · SF Administrative Code § 37.3 — Rent Limitations (American Legal)
Is the licence checked on the notice date or the effective date?The effective date. Reporting after the deadline is allowed, but the licence must exist on the effective date of the notice.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory
Does a correct 1.6% calculation cure a missing licence?No. The licence is a separate pre-condition; the licensing page states the licence must be on file “before any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect”.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Banked rent increases
Is what I report kept private?No. “The information entered in the housing inventory submission is public.” The Rent Board lists name and business contact information, approximate square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms and the history of vacancy or occupancy during the past 12 months. The owner reports “the name and business contact information of the owner(s), or of the property manager, if any, designated by the owner(s) to address habitability issues”. The placeholder names on this site therefore cannot be used in the official filing (FR-34).sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Do I need a professional licence to rent my own property?Not from this rule. The Rent Board “rent increase license” is a unit-level permit generated by the Housing Inventory report — not an occupational licence. Separately, California's broker-licence law regulates people who let, rent or collect rents for another for compensation; renting your own property is not that. Whether an SF business registration number is expected for your rental (“the business registration number for the unit, if any”) is an open item this site cannot confirm — published as FR-37.Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 10131 — who is a real estate broker (licence definition) · sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory
Are there increases that need no licence?The Rent Board's noncompliance page states the licence rules “apply ONLY to units under San Francisco Rent Control” and that a licence “is not required in some situations where the original occupant(s) no longer live in the unit” (a Section 6.14 or Costa-Hawkins market-rate increase), or for passthroughs allowed under the Rent Ordinance. Petition-based increases have their own filing rules instead.sf.gov — Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory · sf.gov — Rent increases under Section 6.14 and Costa-Hawkins · sf.gov — Landlord petitions and passthroughs
Is the Housing Inventory report the same as the annual Rent Board Fee?No — separate obligations. Reporting into the Housing Inventory is free and generates the licence; the Rent Board Fee is a separate annual bill ($59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026–27, due 1 March; 50% = $29.50 recoverable from qualifying tenants; 2027 exemption window closes 14 December 2026). Neither satisfies the other.sf.gov — The Rent Board Housing Inventory · sf.gov — The Rent Board Fee

Bottom line: file the Housing Inventory → confirm the licence is current on the effective date → then calculate, then serve notice. Full sequence: Licence & process →

4. What changed on 9 November 2026 — read before serving any notice

The Rent Board added a notice to its rent-increase and banking pages on 9 November 2026: the Board of Supervisors is considering legislation (File No. 261020) that would temporarily limit the combined amount of banked rent increases and capital improvement rent increases to 10% per year, for the next three years. The Rent Board states that, if enacted as currently proposed, it would apply to banked rent increases noticed on or after 15 September 2026 — and that “this legislation has not been enacted and is subject to change”.

5. Short answers to the rest of the brief

QuestionAnswer in one lineDetail + source
What is a reasonable / normal increase under local, state and federal law?The annual allowance is not a measure of what is typical or fair. The ceiling is generally the Rent Board percentage (1.6% for 1 Mar 2026 – 28 Feb 2027) plus any properly banked amounts. State law (AB 1482, 8.8% in this region) does not govern because the local cap is lower; the Fair Housing Act prohibits discriminatory terms; federal program rules may also apply.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · Cal. Civ. Code § 1947.12 — Tenant Protection Act rent cap · 42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Fair Housing Act (govinfo, U.S. Code 2023)
How do large corporations handle increases; how do individual owners?Official records document allegations involving some large operators and algorithmic pricing; San Francisco prohibits specified algorithmic pricing devices. New-tenancy pricing is subject to exceptions. Individual owners use the Rent Board's annual + banked mechanism, with no petition needed.sf.gov — New law prohibits algorithmic devices used to set rents in San Francisco · U.S. DOJ — suit against six large landlords over algorithmic pricing (7 Jan 2025) · sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco
How should tenants be told, and what reasons are acceptable?Written notice stating the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and (if any) the banked part, served 30 or 90 days ahead (+5 if mailed). No reason is required for an annual or banked increase, but the increase must not be retaliatory or based on a protected characteristic or on a new occupant.sf.gov — Learn about rent increases in San Francisco · Cal. Civ. Code § 827 — notice to change terms of tenancy · Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 — retaliation prohibited
Would a tenant lawyer accept it?No result is guaranteed. The 16-point checklist covers lawful percentage, base rent, licence, service, retaliation and discrimination, but it is not a substitute for an independent legal review.sf.gov — Report an unlawful rent increase / review your rent history · sf.gov — Tenant petitions · Cal. Gov. Code § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination
Raise one unit or both?No official source requires both units to be raised. Discrimination law prohibits different treatment because of a protected characteristic, so the risk is about the reason and the record, not the number of units. The Rent Board does not hear discrimination claims — those go to the Civil Rights Department or HUD.Cal. Gov.
